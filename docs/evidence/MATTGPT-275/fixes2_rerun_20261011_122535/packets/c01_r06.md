## Turn 3. Visitor: What went wrong?

### A

🐾 Here's what I found.

During the development of the ACCESS Next Generation platform at **JP Morgan Chase**, several challenges emerged. The project involved consolidating multiple regional and legacy systems into a single global portal, which was a complex and high-pressure task. One significant issue was the need to build bespoke payment modules and security controls beyond what the existing platform could offer. This required extensive custom engineering to meet the operational scale and compliance requirements of **135,000+** clients across **180+** countries.

The integration of various payment types, each with distinct formatting, validation, compliance, and workflow requirements, added to the complexity. The team faced aggressive delivery timelines, demanding constant velocity across a multi-workstream program. Additionally, the legacy entitlement management system was cumbersome, requiring days of manual configuration per user, which increased the risk of unauthorized access and operational inefficiencies.

To address these issues, Matt led the design and implementation of an advanced user administration engine, introducing role-based access control (RBAC) and centralized visibility. This overhaul reduced entitlement management from days to minutes, significantly improving operational efficiency and compliance governance. Despite these challenges, the ACCESS platform successfully launched in April 2011, transforming JP Morgan's corporate digital channel.
