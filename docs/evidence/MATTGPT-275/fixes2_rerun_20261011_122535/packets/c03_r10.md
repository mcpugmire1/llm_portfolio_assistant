## Turn 2. Visitor: Give me an example where that didn’t work.

### A

🐾 Here's a story where Matt's leadership style faced challenges.

During his time at **Norfolk Southern**, Matt encountered a quality crisis despite having near-complete test coverage. The client raised concerns about rising defects, which caught Matt and his team off guard. The issue stemmed from an engineer who was interpreting backlog stories on his own and writing tests after the code, which made the coverage appear complete while defects went undetected. The backlog itself had vague stories without defined scenarios or a clear definition of done, allowing room for misinterpretation.

Instead of defending the metrics, Matt took ownership of the failure and conducted a thorough audit of the codebase and test suite. He found that the backlog was part of the problem and rebuilt it with SMART, testable stories. Matt brought in experts to demonstrate test-driven development and used the Samman coaching approach to rebuild the discipline within the team. This included boot camps, coding katas, and practice learning sessions.

The result was a significant drop in defect rates as the team adopted a test-first discipline, and client confidence was restored through transparency and visible improvement. This experience reinforced that cultural transformation requires consistent coaching, not just process change.

### B

🐾 Here's a story where Matt's leadership approach faced challenges.

During his time as a Senior Technology Architecture Manager for a **Fortune 500 financial services client**, Matt encountered a significant issue due to assumptions he made about the existing infrastructure. He was leading a global platform modernization delivery and assumed that the Identity and Access Management (IAM) controls and environment boundaries were correctly configured. This assumption led to an offshore tester accidentally triggering an email job in the User Acceptance Testing (UAT) environment, which sent communications containing unmasked production data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability for the incident and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. He realized that relying on inherited documentation without verification was a mistake. This experience taught him that assumptions can be risks in disguise and that validating IAM configuration, environment verification, and data masking are critical leadership responsibilities.

The incident resulted in the client demanding the removal of the tester from the project, highlighting the systemic failure that led to individual consequences. This reinforced Matt's belief that leaders must own the environment their teams operate in and ensure that all configurations are verified rather than assumed.
