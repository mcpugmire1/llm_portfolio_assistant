Under delivery pressure, Matt relied on existing documentation and assumed that the Identity and Access Management (IAM) controls and environment boundaries were correctly configured. This assumption led to an offshore tester accidentally triggering an email job in the UAT environment, which sent communications containing unmasked production data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability for the incident and led the team through a thorough root cause analysis and remediation process. The incident highlighted the risks of relying on assumptions rather than verifying configurations, especially in high-stakes environments. Despite Matt's efforts to address the issue, the client demanded the removal of the tester from the project, illustrating the systemic failure that resulted in individual consequences.

This experience reinforced Matt's belief that leaders must own the environment their teams operate in and that assumptions can be risks in disguise. He learned the importance of IAM validation, environment verification, and risk ownership as day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach.
