## Turn 2. Visitor: Give me an example where that didn’t work.

### A

🐾 Here's a great example.

During Matt's sabbatical in 2026, he worked on building MattGPT, a chatbot project. Initially, the chatbot, Agy, kept generating evaluative language despite having a post-processing filter designed to catch it. The filter, BANNED_PHRASES_CLEANUP, was supposed to strip such language but failed because it only matched the exact phrase "Matt's ability," while the model rephrased it to "His ability," slipping past the filter. This issue highlighted a flaw in relying solely on post-processing fixes.

Matt discovered that the root cause was conflicting instructions within a single system prompt. Instead of just adding more patches to the ban list, he decided to rebuild the prompt architecture. He deleted the existing 500+ line inline prompt and created a dedicated module with a BASE_PROMPT and mode-specific DELTA pattern. This new structure consolidated rules and eliminated conflicting instructions, reducing the prompt from 220 lines to about 90. The result was a significant drop in eval suite failures from **32.3%** to roughly **3-6%**.

This example shows that even with a strong leadership approach, sometimes the initial solution might not work as expected, and it requires a deep dive to find and fix the structural cause rather than applying superficial patches.

### B

🐾 Here's a story where Matt's leadership approach faced challenges.

During a global platform modernization delivery for a **Fortune 500 financial services client**, Matt encountered a significant setback. He was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup but relied on assumptions rather than verifying the Identity and Access Management (IAM) configuration and downstream access controls. This oversight led to an offshore tester accidentally triggering an email job in the User Acceptance Testing (UAT) environment, sending unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

The root cause was insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed from downstream environments. Matt took accountability for the incident and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. Unfortunately, the client demanded the tester be removed from the project, highlighting a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence. This experience reinforced Matt's belief that leaders own the environment their teams operate in and that assumptions can be risks in disguise.
