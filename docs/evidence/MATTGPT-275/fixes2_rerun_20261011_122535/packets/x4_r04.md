### B

🐾 Here's a great example.

In his role at **Accenture**, Matt was deeply involved in managing financial accountability across multiple engagements, including those with **JP Morgan**, **RBC**, **Fiserv**, and **HSBC**. He owned the engagement margin directly, which involved managing the resource pyramid, forecasting, variance, and contingency release. To calculate ROI, Matt focused on protecting and improving the economics of the work he was accountable for. He managed the resource pyramid by staffing senior and specialized resources early for solution and architecture work, then rolling them off to offshore and nearshore as the backlog matured. This approach ensured that the work became repeatable and cost-effective.

Matt also pushed scope creep into change requests rather than absorbing it, either reducing or deferring scope elsewhere to offset at no cost to the client or running the full SOW change request process. He held cost discipline at the line-item level, including advance travel booking for policy compliance and negotiated group hotel rates for project teams. This meticulous approach to managing costs and resources allowed him to recover an **\$8.5M** program at **Fiserv** seven months in, delivering **3%** under budget, avoiding **\$500K** in contractual penalties, and securing a **\$3M** contract extension.

These actions demonstrate how Matt calculated ROI by focusing on efficient resource management, cost control, and strategic decision-making to improve the financial outcomes of his engagements.
