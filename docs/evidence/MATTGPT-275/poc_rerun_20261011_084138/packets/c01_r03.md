## Turn 1. Visitor: Tell me about his payments work.

### A

🐾 Here's a great example of Matt's work in the payments domain.

At **JP Morgan Chase**, Matt played a pivotal role in building the payment engine behind the **JP Morgan ACCESS** platform. The project was driven by the need to serve over **135,000** clients across **180+** countries with a unified global corporate banking portal. The existing platform, based on Bottomline Technologies' WebSeries, required extensive customization to meet the diverse needs of JP Morgan's vast client base. Each payment type had unique formatting, validation, compliance, and workflow requirements, making the task both high-stakes and high-pressure.

As the Technology Delivery Lead, Matt managed a blended team of **40+** professionals from **Accenture** and JP Morgan. He was responsible for designing and delivering bespoke payment modules, including ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and EFTPS tax payments. Matt also implemented a Message Authentication Code (MAC) security layer and integrated real-time global reporting and trade finance capabilities. His work included developing contextual help, payment-type-specific RBAC, and SSO to enhance user experience and security.

The result was the successful launch of the ACCESS Next Generation platform in April 2011. This platform consolidated previously separate regional systems into a single digital experience, transforming the corporate treasurer experience. It was recognized as the #1 digital channel by Coalition Greenwich, highlighting its impact and success.

## Turn 3. Visitor: What went wrong?

### A

🐾 On it!

During the development of the JP Morgan ACCESS platform, several challenges arose. The project was high-stakes and high-pressure, with aggressive Wall Street delivery timelines demanding constant velocity across a complex, multi-workstream program. One significant issue was the complexity of integrating distinct payment types, each with unique formatting, validation, compliance, and workflow requirements. The team had to purpose-build these modules to accommodate JP Morgan's operational scale and diverse client needs.

Another challenge involved the Message Authentication Code (MAC) security layer. A critical defect was discovered in this layer, which was traced back to missing unit tests and weak error handling. This issue required Matt to have break-glass production access to query live payment data via SQL for triage and resolution. The defect highlighted the need for robust testing and error management practices within the platform's security architecture.

Additionally, the project faced complexities in managing the build-vs-configure boundary between Bottomline's WebSeries platform and JP Morgan-specific custom development. Determining where the platform's native capabilities ended and where bespoke engineering was necessary was a continuous challenge that required careful oversight and coordination with Bottomline Technologies.

Despite these challenges, Matt successfully delivered the ACCESS Next Generation platform, which launched in April 2011, transforming JP Morgan's corporate digital banking experience.
