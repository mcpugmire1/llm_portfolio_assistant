## Turn 1. Visitor: Tell me about a time he failed.

### A

🐾 Here's a story that stands out.

During a global platform modernization delivery for a **Fortune 500 financial services client**, Matt faced a significant challenge. He was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup across multiple environments, including dev, QA, UAT, and production. However, due to delivery pressures and existing documentation, Matt made assumptions about the correctness of IAM controls, environment boundaries, and data masking.

These assumptions led to a critical failure when an offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in the UAT environment. This job sent communications containing unmasked production data to thousands of real clients. The incident highlighted the risks of relying on inherited documentation without verification.

Matt took accountability for the incident and led his team through a root cause analysis and remediation process. He learned that assumptions are risks in disguise and emphasized the importance of IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership as day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach. This experience reinforced his belief that leaders must own the environments their teams operate in to prevent such failures.

This incident, though challenging, provided Matt with valuable lessons on the importance of verification and risk management in technology leadership.
