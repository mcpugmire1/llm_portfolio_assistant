## Turn 2. Visitor: Give me an example where that didn’t work.

### A

🐾 Here's a story where Matt's leadership style faced challenges.

At **Norfolk Southern**, during the modernization of their Revenue and Shipment Planning platform, Matt encountered a quality crisis despite having near-complete test coverage. The client raised concerns about rising defects, which caught Matt off guard since the metrics seemed strong. The issue stemmed from an engineer who was interpreting backlog stories on his own, writing tests after code completion, which made the coverage appear complete but allowed defects to slip through. The backlog itself was vague, lacking defined scenarios or a clear definition of done, which contributed to the problem.

Matt took ownership of the situation in front of the client and **Accenture** leadership, choosing to dig into the root causes rather than defending the metrics. He conducted a bottom-up audit of the codebase and test suite, openly showing the gaps. He then rebuilt the backlog with SMART, testable stories and brought in TDD experts to coach the team. Through hands-on coaching and restructuring agile rituals, the team learned to align better and improve their practices.

The result was a reduction in defect rates as the story quality and test-first discipline took hold. The client stopped finding defects that the dashboards couldn't see, restoring confidence. This experience reinforced for Matt that metrics mean nothing without the practice behind them, and cultural transformation requires consistent coaching, not just process change.

### B

🐾 Here's a story where Matt's leadership style faced challenges.

At the **Accenture Cloud Innovation Center**, Matt initially encountered difficulties when implementing a decentralized decision-making model. The center was designed to operate with autonomy at the team level, emphasizing collective ownership and minimal hierarchy. However, many team members came from legacy delivery organizations where top-down, command-and-control patterns were the norm. This led to decisions drifting upward, teams waiting for direction, and bottlenecks forming where autonomy was supposed to be.

To address these challenges, Matt had to enforce and revalidate the model multiple times. He established cross-functional teams, adopted a servant leadership model, and embedded agile and lean practices. By doing so, he aimed to empower teams with the autonomy to innovate while staying aligned with project objectives. Despite the initial resistance, this approach eventually removed bottlenecks, allowing teams to act quickly and efficiently.

The transformation was not without its bumps, but it ultimately resulted in teams delivering high-quality, innovative solutions that aligned more closely with client needs. Empowered teams reported higher engagement and satisfaction, contributing to improved retention and long-term project stability. Through persistence and adaptation, Matt was able to overcome the initial challenges and foster a culture of accountability and innovation.
