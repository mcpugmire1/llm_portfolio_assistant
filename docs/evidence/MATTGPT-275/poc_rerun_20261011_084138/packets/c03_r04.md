## Turn 1. Visitor: What’s his leadership style?

### A

Matt believes in decentralizing decision-making, empowering teams with autonomy to foster a culture of accountability and innovation. He adopted a servant leadership model, where leaders act as facilitators, guiding teams toward solutions while allowing them to take ownership of decisions. This shift resulted in faster responses to project needs and higher-quality solutions.

He also emphasizes sustainable leadership, redefining his approach to include boundaries and work-life integration as essential disciplines. This focus on sustainable pace and self-care has helped him model a balanced environment for his teams, ensuring long-term impact and engagement.

Overall, Matt's leadership is characterized by a commitment to shared goals, open communication, and a supportive culture that encourages innovation and collective success.

## Turn 2. Visitor: Give me an example where that didn’t work.

### A

🐾 Here's a situation where Matt's leadership approach faced challenges.

During a global platform modernization delivery for a **Fortune 500 financial services client**, Matt encountered a significant issue due to assumptions about the existing infrastructure. He was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup but relied on inherited documentation and did not verify the IAM configuration and downstream access controls. This oversight led to an offshore tester accidentally triggering an email job in UAT, which sent communications containing unmasked production data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability for the incident and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. The root cause was traced back to insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed from downstream environments. As a result, the client demanded the removal of the tester from the project, highlighting a systemic failure that resulted in individual consequences. This experience reinforced Matt's belief that leaders must own the environment their teams operate in and verify assumptions rather than inherit them.

### B

🐾 At **Norfolk Southern**, Matt faced a significant challenge when his leadership style initially didn't yield the expected results. The project involved modernizing the Revenue and Shipment Planning platform, and despite having near-complete test coverage, the client raised concerns about rising defects. This feedback was unexpected and caught the team off guard, as the metrics suggested everything was on track.

Upon investigation, Matt discovered that an engineer was deviating from the backlog stories, crafting solutions based on personal interpretations and writing tests post-code, which made the coverage appear complete while defects went unnoticed. The backlog itself was part of the problem, with vague stories lacking defined scenarios or a clear definition of done.

To address this, Matt took ownership of the issue, conducted a thorough audit of the codebase, and openly shared the findings with both the client and **Accenture** leadership. He then rebuilt the backlog with SMART, testable stories and brought in TDD experts to guide the team. Through consistent coaching, boot camps, and practice sessions, the team realigned with proper agile practices.

The outcome was positive: the client stopped finding defects, defect rates dropped, and client confidence was restored. This experience reinforced the importance of consistent coaching and the realization that metrics alone don't equate to quality without the right practices behind them.
