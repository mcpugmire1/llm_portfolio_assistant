## Turn 1. Visitor: Tell me about a time he failed.

### A

🐾 Found it!

During a global platform modernization delivery for a **Fortune 500 financial services client**, Matt faced a significant failure. He was the tech lead responsible for ensuring the safety of the environment setup across multiple environments: dev, QA, UAT, and production. Under delivery pressure, Matt assumed the IAM controls, environment boundaries, and data masking were correct, relying on inherited documentation from prior phases without verifying them.

This assumption led to an offshore tester accidentally triggering an email job in UAT that sent communications containing unmasked production data to thousands of real clients. The root cause was insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed from downstream environments. Matt took accountability for the incident and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. Unfortunately, the client demanded the tester be removed from the project, highlighting the systemic failure that resulted in individual consequences.

Matt learned that assumptions are risks in disguise and emphasized the importance of IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership as day-one leadership responsibilities in his future delivery approaches.

## Turn 2. Visitor: What did he change afterward?

### A

🐾 Here's a great example.

### B

He emphasized the importance of incremental modernization using Domain-Driven Design (DDD) principles and microservices transitions. This approach allowed for a gradual transformation of legacy systems into more scalable and flexible architectures. Additionally, Matt focused on OKR-driven sprints to prioritize modernization efforts based on business value rather than just technical debt. This approach ensured that modernization was aligned with organizational goals and provided transparency and trust among stakeholders.

These changes led to faster deployment cycles, improved scalability, and reduced downtime across various projects, demonstrating a more robust and reliable modernization process.
