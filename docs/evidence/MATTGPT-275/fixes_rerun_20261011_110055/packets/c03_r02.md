### A

Matt's approach blends empathy and clarity with modern engineering rigor. He leads with purpose, making intent explicit, and fosters an environment where team members can take risks and speak honestly. His leadership philosophy also includes building cross-functional teams with shared ownership, adopting servant leadership models, and encouraging decentralized decision-making to remove bottlenecks and empower teams.

In practice, Matt established weekly brainstorming sessions to promote idea sharing without judgment, aligned teams around shared goals, and celebrated contributions that advanced the mission. This shift from individual recognition to collective ownership strengthened team cohesion and led to innovative solutions that positively impacted project timelines and quality.

Additionally, Matt has redefined his leadership to include sustainable practices, such as setting clear boundaries for work-life integration and prioritizing health and recovery. This holistic approach ensures that both he and his teams maintain a sustainable pace, balancing intensity with humanity.

## Turn 2. Visitor: Give me an example where that didn’t work.

### A

🐾 Here's a story where Matt's leadership approach faced challenges.

During a global platform modernization delivery for a **Fortune 500 financial services client**, Matt encountered a significant issue. He was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup but relied on assumptions rather than verifying the IAM configuration and downstream access controls. The project was under delivery pressure, and he focused on inherited documentation without confirming its accuracy. This oversight led to an offshore tester accidentally triggering an email job in UAT that sent communications containing unmasked production data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability for the incident and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. The root cause was insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed from downstream environments. This incident reinforced his belief that leaders must own the environment their teams operate in and verify assumptions rather than inherit them. Unfortunately, the client demanded the removal of the tester from the project, highlighting the systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence.
