## Turn 1. Visitor: Tell me about a time he failed.

### A

🐾 Here's a story about a significant challenge Matt faced.

While working as a Senior Technology Architecture Manager on a global platform modernization delivery for a **Fortune 500 financial services client**, Matt encountered a critical failure. The project involved multiple environments, including development, QA, UAT, and production. Under delivery pressure, with offshore teams executing test cycles, Matt assumed that the existing IAM controls, environment boundaries, and downstream access configurations were correct, relying on inherited documentation from prior phases.

However, this assumption led to a severe incident. An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in the UAT environment, which sent communications containing unmasked production data to thousands of real clients. This was due to insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and unmasked production data in downstream environments—assumptions Matt should have verified rather than inherited.

Matt took full accountability for the incident and led the team through a root cause analysis and remediation process. Unfortunately, the client demanded the removal of the tester from the project, highlighting a systemic failure that resulted in individual consequences. This experience reinforced Matt's belief in the importance of leaders owning the environments their teams operate in and ensuring rigorous validation of all assumptions.
