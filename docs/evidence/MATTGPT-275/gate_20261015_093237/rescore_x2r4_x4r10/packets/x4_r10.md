# Extra x4 (ROI), run 10

## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt calculate ROI?

### A

🐾 Here’s what Matt measured, and where the ROI detail stops.

Matt used cost-to-fix versus benefit comparisons, delivery measures, and engagement financials. The stories do not provide a single ROI formula, a calculated ROI percentage, or a payback period.

At Accenture’s Cloud Innovation Centers, clients questioned the cost of pair programming, while traditional delivery involved siloed teams, handoffs, and separate testing phases. Matt addressed those concerns with measured delivery results rather than methodology presentations alone. He compared velocity, team size, deployment quality, and delivery timelines against traditional approaches. The centers reported 4X faster velocity, with 10-person balanced teams delivering equivalent output to traditional 20-person teams, and zero-defect deployments through test-driven development and automated pipelines. Those were delivery comparisons, not a stated financial ROI percentage.

He also moved beyond velocity alone. Team scorecards tracked lead time, cycle time, throughput, team stability, and backlog health. These measures identified bottlenecks and where teams could reduce handoffs and rework.

For investment decisions at RBC, Matt worked with the enterprise architecture team on a transparent “cost-to-fix vs. benefit comparison.” Code reviews, stakeholder interviews, architectural heat maps, and target-system models exposed gaps in WebSeries before significant spending was committed. The assessment led the bank away from that platform path; the story does not quantify the avoided investment.

For engagement economics, Matt tracked actuals, forecast variance, contingency, staffing costs, and scope changes. At Fiserv, the \$8.5M program finished 3% under budget, preserving \$255K in margin, avoiding \$500K in contractual penalties, and securing a \$3M extension. Those figures are reported separately, not combined into an ROI calculation.
