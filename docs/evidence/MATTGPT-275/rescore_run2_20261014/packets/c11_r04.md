## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt scale engineering teams from 4 to 150+ people?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt scaled the organization.

The CIC stories describe a **10-person pilot growing to **150+ practitioners** over four years**, rather than a starting team of **4**. That organization included engineering, architecture, product, and design across **Accenture’s** Atlanta and Tampa Cloud Innovation Centers.

Growing client demand created two problems: many new hires lacked hands-on cloud-native or Agile experience, and the organization needed to handle hiring, development, and performance without adding layers of management. Clients also needed to move from waterfall delivery and siloed functions to balanced teams.

Matt built a hiring engine across **four** channels: internal bench search, campus recruiting, contract-to-hire, and job requisitions. Engineers completed hands-on TDD pairing auditions; product managers and designers worked through live scoping exercises. Interviewers recorded independent votes, and Matt held the closing interview and made every final hiring decision. During scaling pushes, the process produced **4–6 net-new practitioners per week**, with interview participation capped at **two slots per practitioner per week** to protect delivery time.

Hiring was followed by tailored enablement, not just classroom training. Matt used skills assessments, custom learning material, and an “I do, we do, you do” progression. New joiners paired with seasoned practitioners; senior engineers coached junior colleagues. Weekly peer-led tech talks, practice forums, and live client work continued the learning. Teams embedded pair programming, TDD, CI/CD, user research, and Build-Measure-Learn cycles into delivery.

He kept the organization deliberately flat: **11–13 practice and client leads** reported directly to him, with experienced engineering anchors inside balanced teams. Monday allocation reviews assigned practitioners across engagements and internal learning work. Performance evaluations drew on recent pair partners, product managers, and anchors—not just a manager’s impressions. Career-level expectations, calibration panels, and compensation reviews supplied the promotion machinery.

The organization reached **150+ practitioners**, with **near-zero attrition in the first two years** and multiple hires promoted. Across CIC delivery, **10-person teams** produced output equivalent to traditional **20-person teams**, with **4X faster velocity** and **zero-defect deployments**. Delivery excellence and client advocacy helped generate **\$100M+ in repeatable business by FY23**.

## Turn 2. Visitor: Tell me about his payments work

### A

🐾 Here’s Matt’s payments work at **JP Morgan Chase** and **American Express**.

At **JP Morgan Chase**, treasury services ran across separate regional and legacy systems. Corporate treasurers faced different logins and payment systems, while administrators spent days configuring individual users’ permissions. The new ACCESS platform needed payment capabilities beyond Bottomline Technologies’ WebSeries offering, under aggressive delivery timelines.

As Technology Delivery Lead, Matt managed a **40+** blended team across **Accenture** and **JP Morgan Chase** in the US, Mumbai, and Bangalore. He designed and delivered ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and government tax payment modules. That included NACHA file validation, payment-integrity verification through a Message Authentication Code security layer, sanctions screening, risk-based controls, and payment-type-specific permissions. Contextual help explained wire fields, tax codes, and FX requirements directly within payment workflows.

He also designed the centralized payments gateway: message transformation, protocol translation, and routing across clearing networks in **12 countries**. Rather than retaining fragmented point-to-point integrations, the gateway gave treasurers a single entry point for wire, ACH, and cross-border transfers. Matt led legacy-data migration while preserving client configurations, entitlements, transaction histories, and compliance traceability—with **no disruption to live payment operations**. His entitlement engine replaced per-user configuration with role-based profiles and bulk changes, reducing administration from **days to minutes**.

ACCESS launched in **April 2011**, consolidating regional systems into a global portal serving **135,**000+ client**s** across **180+ countries**.

At **American Express**, Matt addressed a different payments problem: clients struggled to adopt its Virtual Payments Platform because SDKs were missing, documentation was fragmented, and there was no sandbox—especially difficult for clients without mature development teams. He co-created a developer portal, developed Java, .NET, and Python SDKs, added real-world API documentation, and built a secure sandbox. Client onboarding time decreased, and API consumption and trial usage increased within **3 months** of relaunch.
