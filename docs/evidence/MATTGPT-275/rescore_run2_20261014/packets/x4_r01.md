## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt calculate ROI?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt put costs and benefits side by side.

Matt’s stories describe cost-to-fix versus benefit comparisons and measurable delivery results, but they do not specify an exact ROI formula or a calculated ROI percentage.

At **Accenture’s** Cloud Innovation Centers, clients questioned whether pair programming justified its cost. Matt addressed those concerns by comparing velocity, team size, and quality against traditional delivery approaches. The centers reported **4X faster velocity**, with **10-person** balanced teams delivering equivalent output to traditional **20-person** teams, and zero-defect deployments through test-driven development and automated pipelines. Those were the delivery measures used to justify investment—not a stated financial ROI percentage.

Matt also owned full P&L accountability for the Atlanta and Tampa centers, including budget and cost management. He tracked chargeability, unassigned and unbillable downtime, and contractor spend. He required internal bench deployment before approving external contractors, keeping practitioner cost tied to billable demand. The centers grew to **150+ practitioners**, delivering **30+ product engagements** across **15+ Fortune **500 client**s**, and contributed to **\$100M+** in repeatable business by **FY23** through delivery excellence and client advocacy.

At **RBC**, the investment question was whether Bottomline WebSeries could meet the bank’s corporate banking requirements. Matt conducted code reviews, stakeholder interviews, and a gap/fit analysis, then delivered target-system models with a “transparent cost-to-fix vs. benefit comparison.” The assessment found that WebSeries lacked the required modularity. **RBC** avoided further investment in that platform path and redirected technical resources to higher-priority initiatives; the story does not quantify the avoided spend.
