# Conversation 1, run 5

## Turn 1. Visitor: Tell me about his payments work.

### A

🐾 Matt’s payments work includes corporate payment engines, global payment connectivity, legacy migration, and virtual payments integration.

At **JP Morgan Chase**, ACCESS Next Generation needed more than Bottomline Technologies’ WebSeries platform provided out of the box. Different payment types had distinct formatting, validation, compliance, and workflow requirements. Regional systems also had different data models, client configurations, and entitlement structures, and live payment processing had to continue during migration.

As Technology Delivery Lead, Matt managed a blended team of **40+** across **Accenture** and **JP Morgan Chase**, with engineers in Mumbai, Bangalore, and the US. He designed and delivered bespoke ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and government tax payment modules. The work included NACHA file validation, embedded FX rate visibility, Message Authentication Code security, and payment-type-specific permissions—for example, allowing a user to approve wires but not initiate FX payments. He also incorporated risk-based transaction restrictions and sanctions screening before payment release.

Rather than retain fragmented regional integrations, Matt designed a centralized gateway for message transformation, protocol translation, and routing across **12 countries**. He led payments data migration, preserved compliance traceability, and introduced compliance dashboards that became standard operational tooling. ACCESS launched in **April 2011**, serving **135,000+** clients across **180+ countries**, with migration completed without disruption to live payment operations.

At **American Express**, the Virtual Payments Platform offered real-time controls and flexible APIs, but adoption lagged: SDKs were missing, documentation was fragmented, and clients had no sandbox. Matt co-created a developer portal, developed Java, .NET, and Python SDKs, and built a secure sandbox with refreshed client data. Environment virtualization enabled earlier testing without downstream dependencies; a Client Onboarding Test Drive gathered end-user feedback. API consumption and trial usage increased within **3 months** of relaunch, onboarding time declined, and the portal team delivered over **261 additional story points** beyond its commitment.

## Turn 3. Visitor: What went wrong?

### A

🐾 There were production defects—not just integration challenges—on **JP Morgan Chase**’s ACCESS payments platform.

One was a Message Authentication Code (MAC) security-layer failure. That layer let corporate users validate payment integrity before release. Matt’s root-cause investigation traced the failure to **missing unit tests and weak error handling**. He had break-glass production access and queried live payment data via SQL to triage the platform’s hardest defects. The account does not specify the incident’s duration or how many customers or payments were affected.

Other production defects emerged after migration cutover, primarily **data issues carried over from the legacy platform**. Regional systems had different schemas, formats, client configurations, entitlement structures, and compliance records. Consolidating them into a single platform meant preserving those details while payment processing remained live. Matt ran UAT and production validation/shakeout testing, owned production support during rollout, and triaged the post-cutover defects.

Across successive releases, waves, and migrations, Matt also owned on-call and Sev-**1** incident response. He coordinated root-cause analysis across the New York team, engineers in Mumbai and Bangalore, and Bottomline Technologies, while running status updates and daily leadership calls during live issues.

The broader starting problem was fragmented regional connectivity and requirements that exceeded WebSeries’ out-of-box capabilities. Matt’s team built custom payment modules and a centralized gateway rather than relying solely on the vendor platform. ACCESS launched in **April 2011**; the legacy data migration was completed **without disruption to live payment operations**. That migration outcome does not mean the platform had no production incidents—the stories explicitly record them.
