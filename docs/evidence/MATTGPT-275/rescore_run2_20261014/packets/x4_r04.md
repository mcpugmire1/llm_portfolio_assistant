## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt calculate ROI?

### A

🐾 Here’s what Matt compared—and what the stories don’t specify.

Matt used a “cost-to-fix vs. benefit comparison” for investment decisions and tracked delivery economics through budgets, actuals, forecasts, and margin. His stories do not specify a formal ROI formula, discount rate, payback period, or calculated ROI percentage.

At **RBC**, aging corporate banking systems had performance and scalability limitations, and leadership was evaluating Bottomline WebSeries before committing further investment. Matt conducted code reviews, stakeholder interviews, and a gap/fit analysis across Data, Application, Technology, and Business Architecture. He created architectural heat maps and worked with the enterprise architecture team on target-system models with a transparent cost-to-fix versus benefit comparison. The assessment found that WebSeries lacked the required modularity. **RBC** avoided further investment in that platform path and redirected technical resources; the story does not attach a dollar amount to the avoided spend.

For the Cloud Innovation Centers, clients questioned the cost of pair programming. Matt addressed those concerns with measured velocity gains and quality improvements: **4X faster velocity**, **10-person** balanced teams delivering equivalent output to traditional **20-person** teams, and zero-defect deployments through TDD and automated pipelines. He owned the Atlanta and Tampa centers’ full P&L, including budget and cost management.

On delivery engagements, he tracked the resource pyramid, loaded rates, actuals, forecast variance, contingency, and scope changes. At **Fiserv**, recovery of an **\$8.5M** program produced **3%** under-budget delivery—**\$255K** in preserved margin—plus **\$500K** in contractual penalties avoided and a **\$3M** contract extension. Those were reported separately, not combined into a single ROI percentage.
