## Turn 1. Visitor: Compare his Fiserv and JP Morgan ACCESS work.

### A

🐾 Here’s how the two engagements compare.

At **Fiserv**, Matt took over a delivery recovery: an **\$8.5M** white-label card portal serving **2M+ cardholders** across **15 financial institutions** had missed multiple deadlines, had production quality problems, and faced contract cancellation. At **JP Morgan Chase**, ACCESS Next Generation required custom engineering beyond Bottomline Technologies’ WebSeries offering, while consolidating regional systems for **135,**000+ client**s** across **180+ countries**. The pressure there was aggressive delivery timelines, distinct payment requirements, and conflicting regional regulations.

For **Fiserv**, Matt became Development Manager roughly **7 months** into the engagement, coordinating **45 team members** and **3 external vendors**. He reset the backlog and timelines, ran offshore/onshore transition calls, and managed the SOA integration layer connecting VisionPLUS **350** mainframe processing to the Java EE/Spring portal. He replaced SVN with Git, introduced mandatory senior-developer reviews, unit tests, Hudson CI, and SonarQube quality gates. He also moved the team from **6-month** release cycles to **2-week** sprints within **3 months**, with persona-based user stories, sprint demos, and JIRA accepted as the compliance system of record.

For **JP Morgan Chase**, Matt was Technology Delivery Lead for a **40+ person** blended team, spending **70–80%** of his time embedded at Bottomline’s Portsmouth offices. He designed and delivered ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and tax-payment modules; MAC payment verification; payment-type-specific entitlements; and a centralized gateway connecting payment operations across **12 countries**. Bulk permission changes and role-based profiles reduced entitlement management from days to minutes. He also coordinated jurisdiction-specific configurations and regional rollout sequencing, and led payments data migration without disruption to live payment operations.

The **Fiserv** program finished **3% under budget**, saving **\$255K**, met **47 acceptance criteria**, and launched with **zero critical defects**. Recovering a release that was **3 weeks behind** avoided **\$500K in penalties**; the portal processed **\$45M** in its first month at **99.**97%** uptime** and secured a **\$3M contract extension**. ACCESS launched in **April 2011**, consolidating regional systems into a unified corporate banking portal, with regional launches completed without regulatory blockers.

## Turn 2. Visitor: Which was bigger?

### A

🐾 **JP Morgan Chase ACCESS was broader in global reach and payment scope.** Team sizes were comparable; budget size can’t be compared from the available figures.

ACCESS served **135,000+ corporate, financial institution, government, and municipal clients** across **180+ countries**. Matt led a **40+ person** blended team delivering ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and tax-payment capabilities. Its centralized payments gateway connected payment operations across **12 countries**, alongside regional compliance configuration and consolidation of legacy systems into a unified portal. The **180+ countries** describes the platform’s client reach—not the gateway’s country count.

**Fiserv** had the larger stated individual-user population: **2M+ cardholders** across **15 financial institutions**. Matt coordinated **45 team members** and **3 external vendors** on a **14-month, **\$8.5**M** white-label card portal program. His remit included delivery recovery, budget management, mainframe-to-API integration, accessibility compliance, and changes to the team’s release and development practices. He also assumed program manager responsibilities during the program lead’s paternity leave.

Those customer counts aren’t like-for-like: **Fiserv** counted cardholders; **JP Morgan Chase** counted organizations. Likewise, **45** versus **40+** doesn’t establish which team was larger, because ACCESS’s exact headcount isn’t stated.

For financial scale, **Fiserv** has a documented **\$8.5M** program budget and **\$45M** in transactions processed during the portal’s first month. There isn’t a comparable ACCESS budget or transaction-volume figure in these stories, so neither supports a financial-size ranking.
