## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt calculate ROI?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt put numbers behind the investment case.

At **Accenture’s** Cloud Innovation Centers, clients questioned whether pair programming justified its cost, and the centers needed measurable results to justify continued investment. Matt compared delivery velocity, team size, and deployment quality against traditional delivery approaches. The stories describe those comparisons and his financial controls, but do not specify a single ROI formula or a calculated ROI percentage.

He defined the “CIC Standard of Value”: **4X faster velocity**, zero-defect code, customer journey-led development, and lean sustainable delivery. The centers reported **10-person** balanced teams delivering equivalent output to traditional **20-person** teams, with zero-defect deployments through test-driven development and automated pipelines. Matt used executive briefings and client workshops to address pair-programming cost concerns with the measured velocity gains and quality improvements. At **JP Morgan**, teams outpaced peer velocity within the first **2 sprints**.

He also owned the centers’ full P&L: budgets, cost management, chargeability, unbillable downtime, and contractor approval. He required internal bench deployment before contractor spending, keeping practitioner cost tied to billable demand.

For a platform investment at **RBC**, Matt used a different comparison: a transparent **cost-to-fix versus benefit** assessment. He worked with the enterprise architecture team on target-system models and held leadership workshops connecting business requirements to technical findings. The assessment found that WebSeries lacked the required modularity, preventing further investment in that platform path.

On delivery economics at **Fiserv**, the recorded results were **\$255K in preserved margin**, **\$500K in contractual penalties avoided**, and a **\$3M contract extension**—separate financial outcomes, not a combined ROI percentage.
