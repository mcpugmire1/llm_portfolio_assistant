## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt calculate ROI?

### A

🐾 Here’s what Matt measured.

Matt used cost-to-fix versus benefit comparisons, delivery results, and engagement financials to make investment cases. The stories do not specify an ROI formula, payback period, or discounted cash-flow model.

At **Accenture’s** Cloud Innovation Centers, clients questioned the cost of pair programming and whether modern product engineering would work with legacy systems, compliance requirements, and enterprise governance. Matt addressed those concerns with measured delivery results rather than presentations about methodology. He used small **4–8-week** discovery engagements to prove value before expanding into larger programs, and compared velocity, team size, deployment quality, and delivery timelines with traditional approaches.

The centers reported **4X faster velocity**, with **10-person** balanced teams delivering equivalent output to traditional **20-person** teams, zero-defect deployments through TDD and automated pipelines, and full MVP delivery in approximately **16–**18 week**s**. Matt also owned the centers’ budget and cost management, tracking chargeability, unbillable downtime, and contractor spend. Separately, team scorecards tracked lead time, cycle time, throughput, team stability, and backlog health rather than velocity alone.

At **RBC**, Matt worked with the enterprise architecture team on a transparent cost-to-fix versus benefit comparison. Code reviews, stakeholder interviews, architectural heat maps, and target-state models identified where WebSeries could not meet the bank’s requirements. The assessment prevented further investment in that platform path; the story does not give a dollar value for the avoided investment.

At **Fiserv**, the financial results were stated separately: an **\$8.5M** program finished **3% under budget**, preserving **\$255K** in margin, avoiding **\$500K** in contractual penalties, and securing a **\$3M** extension. Those were distinct outcomes—not a reported ROI percentage.
