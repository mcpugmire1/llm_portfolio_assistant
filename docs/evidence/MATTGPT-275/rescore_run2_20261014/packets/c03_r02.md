# Conversation 3, run 2

## Turn 1. Visitor: What’s his leadership style?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt leads.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. Many engineering teams he inherited were “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” At **Accenture’s** Cloud Innovation Center, teams also faced role silos, fear of blame, and knowledge gaps as cloud-native technologies evolved. He focused on removing those barriers rather than adding more process.

He made intent explicit, pushed decisions to the people closest to the work, and used transparency, direct feedback, and open communication. He coached teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks. Engineers, product managers, and UX architects worked together in balanced teams with equal participation and shared accountability. Pair programming, collaborative coding, retrospectives, and continuous delivery became part of daily work.

His “permit to fail” culture meant non-punitive post-mortems and lean, low-stakes experiments—not pointing fingers. In innovation engagements, he set expectations that early prototypes and user tests would likely fail, intentionally. Teams used user research, JTBD framing, and rapid testing to challenge assumptions before committing to costly builds. Failed experiments became decision inputs, and teams shaped product direction week by week.

Matt also paired senior engineers with junior team members, established weekly tech talks with peer-led demos, and embedded learning in live client projects. Teams applied post-mortem lessons to subsequent sprints, prevented recurring failure patterns, and reduced onboarding time for new hires.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, his leadership approach produced **4x delivery acceleration** once teams embraced lean product and modern engineering practices. The balanced-team work also achieved **zero-defect production code** through iterative development and real-time feedback loops.

## Turn 2. Visitor: Give me an example where that didn’t work.

### A

🐾 Yes—the initial approach at **Norfolk Southern** didn’t work as intended.

In early **2020**, Matt launched the Cloud Innovation Center’s first major engagement: modernizing **Norfolk Southern’s** legacy Revenue and Shipment Planning platform. He brought in a balanced team and emphasized test-driven development. A couple of months in, as the pandemic shifted work to remote, the client reported rising defects—even though the dashboards showed near-complete test coverage. The credibility hit landed in front of both the client and **Accenture** account leadership.

Matt found that an engineer was building from his own interpretation rather than the backlog story, then writing tests after the code. Coverage looked complete while defects went undetected. The backlog was also part of the problem: vague stories lacked defined scenarios and a definition of done. The team structure and stated engineering practices had not prevented those gaps.

He took ownership rather than defending the metrics. Matt ran a bottom-up audit of the codebase and test suite and showed the gaps openly. He rebuilt the backlog with SMART, testable stories and brought in frontend and backend TDD experts from Columbus to work alongside the team, redoing code and test cases.

He then added sustained, hands-on coaching: boot camps, coding katas, practice learning sessions, and pairing with subject-matter experts through an “I do, we do, you do” progression. He also restructured agile ceremonies and delivered product management and SMART story training.

Defect rates dropped, and the client stopped finding defects the dashboard could not see. Client confidence was restored through transparency and visible improvement. The story does not provide a numerical defect reduction.
