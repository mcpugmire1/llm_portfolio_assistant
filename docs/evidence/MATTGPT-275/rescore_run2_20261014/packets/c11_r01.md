## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt scale engineering teams from 4 to 150+ people?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt scaled the organization.

The CIC stories describe a **10-person pilot growing to **150+ practitioners** over four years**, rather than a **4-person** starting team. At **Accenture**, Matt built the Atlanta and Tampa Cloud Innovation Centers as client demand grew. Many new hires lacked hands-on cloud-native or Agile experience, and the organization was deliberately flat: managers did not sit beside practitioners on daily delivery teams.

Matt built a hiring engine across bench searches, campus recruiting, contract-to-hire, and approved job requisitions. Georgia Tech recruiting, hackathons, and job fairs expanded the talent pipeline. Instead of relying only on interviews, engineers completed hands-on TDD pairing auditions; product managers and designers worked through messy client briefs or wireframes live. A failed pairing audition ended the candidacy. Matt held the closing interview and made every final hiring decision. During scaling pushes, the process produced **4–6 net-new practitioners per week**, with interviewer participation capped at **2 slots per week** to protect delivery.

He recruited for mindset and potential, then used tailored enablement rather than assuming hires already knew the working model. Practitioners progressed through “I do, we do, you do,” round-robin pairing with experienced colleagues, tiered coaching, and peer-led tech talks. Training covered lean product management, user-centered design, pair programming, TDD, and CI/CD. Skills matrices and individual development plans tracked progress; reusable playbooks and technical accelerators supported consistent delivery across engagements.

Matt ran the organization through **11–13 direct-report practice and client leads**, with team anchors rather than another management layer. Monday allocation reviews moved practitioners among client work, training, and accelerator development. Because managers were not on daily teams, performance feedback came from recent pair partners, product managers, and anchors—not visibility to the director.

The organization reached **150+ practitioners**, with **near-zero attrition in the first two years**. Across CIC engagements, **10-person balanced teams** delivered output equivalent to traditional **20-person teams**, with **4X faster velocity** and **zero-defect deployments**.

## Turn 2. Visitor: Tell me about his payments work

### A

🐾 Here’s Matt’s payments work at **JP Morgan Chase** and **American Express**.

At **JP Morgan Chase**, ACCESS Next Generation needed more than an out-of-box deployment of Bottomline Technologies’ WebSeries platform. Payment types had different formatting, validation, compliance, and workflow requirements, while regional systems used different data models and connectivity. Matt was Technology Delivery Lead, managing a **40+** blended onshore/offshore team across **Accenture** and bank resources in Mumbai, Bangalore, and the US.

Matt managed the build-versus-configure boundary: determining where WebSeries’ native capabilities ended and bespoke engineering was required. He designed and delivered ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and federal tax payment modules, plus Message Authentication Code security and payment-type-specific entitlements. Corporate users could be authorized to approve wires without being authorized to initiate FX payments. He also incorporated sanctions screening and customer risk confidence scores into payment controls.

For the gateway, Matt designed message transformation, protocol translation, and routing across payment networks in **12 countries**. He led payments-workstream data migration from regional systems, preserving client configurations, entitlement structures, transaction histories, and regulatory traceability **without disruption to live payment operations**. ACCESS launched in **April 2011**, serving **135,**000+ client**s** across **180+ countries**.

At **American Express**, the challenge was adoption of its Virtual Payments Platform: clients faced missing SDKs, fragmented documentation, no sandbox, dependency bottlenecks, and delayed testing. Rather than only refreshing the portal interface, Matt introduced environment virtualization so developers could test earlier without waiting on downstream systems. He led work on Java, .NET, and Python SDKs, use-case-driven API documentation, a secure sandbox with refreshed client data, and CI/CD updates that kept documentation synchronized with backend changes.

Discovery and Framing workshops and a Client Onboarding Test Drive informed the portal work. The team delivered over **261 additional story points** beyond its commitment; onboarding time fell, and API consumption and trial usage increased within **3 months** of relaunch.
