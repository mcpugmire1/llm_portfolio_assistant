## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt scale engineering teams from 4 to 150+ people?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt built the organization.

The CIC stories describe a **10-person** pilot growing to **150+ practitioners** over **4 years**, rather than a starting team of **4**. The organization included engineering, architecture, product, and design across **Accenture’s** Atlanta and Tampa Cloud Innovation Centers.

Growing client demand required more than recruiting. Many new hires lacked hands-on experience with cloud-native technologies or Agile ways of working. Meanwhile, clients needed teams to move from waterfall and siloed handoffs to iterative, customer-centric product development. Matt also had to establish hiring, evaluation, career development, and promotion processes without adding layers of middle management.

Matt built a hiring engine across bench search, campus recruiting, contract-to-hire, and governed job requisitions. Engineers completed hands-on TDD pairing auditions; product managers and designers worked through live scoping exercises. Interviewers recorded independent votes, and Matt held the closing interview and made the final decision on every offer. During scaling pushes, this produced **4–6 net-new practitioners per week**, with interviewer participation capped at **2 slots per practitioner per week** to protect delivery.

He developed rotating training cohorts tailored to skill gaps, covering lean product management, user-centered design, pair programming, TDD, and CI/CD. New joiners paired with seasoned practitioners and progressed through “I do, we do, you do.” Senior engineers coached junior colleagues; Friday tech talks, peer-led demos, and learning within live client projects continued after onboarding.

Matt kept the organization deliberately flat, with **11–13 practice and client leads** reporting directly to him. Practice leads, client leads, and Matt met for Monday allocation reviews, assigning practitioners across engagements and internal learning tracks. Team anchors surfaced technical progress, team dynamics, and resource needs. Career conversations and evaluations used evidence from recent pair partners, product managers, and anchors—not just a manager’s impressions.

The organization reached **150+ practitioners**, with **near-zero attrition in the first 2 years** and multiple hires promoted. CIC delivery achieved **4X faster velocity** and **zero-defect deployments**, while delivery excellence and client referrals supported **\$100M+ in repeatable business by FY23**.

## Turn 2. Visitor: Tell me about his payments work

### A

🐾 Here’s Matt’s payments work, from global payment processing to client integration.

At **JP Morgan Chase**, Matt worked on ACCESS Next Generation, the bank’s unified corporate banking portal. Regional payment systems had different formats, protocols, entitlement structures, and compliance requirements. Corporate treasurers faced separate local logins, while client administrators spent days configuring permissions manually. The underlying Bottomline Technologies WebSeries platform needed custom engineering to support the bank’s **135,**000+ client**s** across **180+ countries**.

As Technology Delivery Lead, Matt managed a **40+ person** blended onshore/offshore team across the US, Mumbai, and Bangalore. He designed and delivered payment modules for ACH credits and direct debits, Fedwire and international wires, FX/cross-border payments, and federal tax payments. He also delivered payment verification through Message Authentication Codes, payment-type-specific permissions, sanctions screening, and connections to internal compliance and settlement systems.

Matt built the centralized payments gateway across **12 countries**, handling message transformation, protocol translation, and routing. He led the payments data migration without disruption to live payment operations and owned on-call and critical production incident response. For administrators, he introduced role-based access and bulk permission changes from a single screen, reducing entitlement management from days to minutes. ACCESS launched in **April 2011**, giving corporate treasurers a single entry point for payments across regions, real-time global reporting, and single sign-on across treasury functions.

At **American Express**, Matt addressed adoption of its Virtual Payments Platform. Clients—especially those without mature development teams—had no sandbox, missing SDKs, and fragmented documentation. He co-created a developer portal, developed Java, .NET, and Python SDKs, added use-case-driven API documentation, and built a secure sandbox with refreshed client data. CI/CD kept documentation and APIs synchronized with backend changes.

API consumption and trial usage increased within **3 months** of relaunch. Client onboarding time decreased significantly, and clients integrated faster as they migrated away from legacy systems.
