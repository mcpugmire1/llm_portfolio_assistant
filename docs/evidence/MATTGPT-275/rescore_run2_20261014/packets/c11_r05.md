## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt scale engineering teams from 4 to 150+ people?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt built and ran that organization.

The CIC growth story starts with a **10-person pilot**, rather than **4**, and reaches **150+ practitioners** across engineering, architecture, product, and design over **four years**. At **Accenture**, growing demand required new Cloud Innovation Centers in Atlanta and Tampa, but many hires lacked hands-on cloud-native or Agile experience. Matt needed to recruit and develop people while maintaining client delivery—without adding layers of middle management.

He built a hiring engine across internal bench searches, campus recruiting, contract-to-hire, and approved job requisitions. Engineers completed hands-on TDD pairing auditions; product managers and designers worked through live scoping exercises. Matt held the closing interview and made every final hiring decision. During scaling pushes, the process added **4–6 net-new practitioners per week**, with interview participation capped at **two slots per practitioner per week** so recruiting did not burn out delivery teams.

New joiners entered structured enablement and then round-robin pairing with seasoned practitioners. Training covered pair programming, TDD, CI/CD, lean product management, and user-centered design. The “I do, we do, you do” progression moved practitioners from guided work to independent delivery. Senior-to-junior coaching, peer-led tech talks, live-project learning, and skills matrices gave people specific development paths rather than training alone.

Matt kept the organization deliberately flat: **11–13 practice and client leads** reported directly to him through a **two-layer structure**. Experienced engineers served as team anchors without becoming another management layer. Weekly allocation reviews matched people to engagements and learning opportunities. Because managers did not sit beside practitioners daily, performance feedback came from recent pairing partners, product managers, and anchors—not visibility to a boss.

The organization reached **150+ practitioners**, with **near-zero attrition in the first two years** and multiple hires promoted. Across CIC delivery, **10-person balanced teams** produced output equivalent to traditional **20-person teams**, with **4X faster velocity** and **zero-defect deployments**.

## Turn 2. Visitor: Tell me about his payments work

### A

🐾 Here’s Matt’s payments work at **JP Morgan Chase** and **American Express**.

At **JP Morgan Chase**, treasury operations ran through separate regional and legacy systems. Corporate treasurers faced different logins and payment workflows, while each payment type had its own formatting, validation, compliance, and approval requirements. ACCESS Next Generation needed custom engineering beyond Bottomline Technologies’ WebSeries platform to serve **135,**000+ client**s** across **180+ countries**.

As Technology Delivery Lead, Matt managed a **40+ person** blended team across the US, Mumbai, and Bangalore, working directly with Bottomline’s engineers in Portsmouth. He designed and delivered ACH credits and direct debits, wire payments, FX/cross-border payments, and federal tax payment capabilities. He built payment verification through Message Authentication Codes, payment-type-specific permissions, sanctions screening, and risk-based approval controls. Contextual help explained wire fields, tax codes, and FX requirements within payment screens.

Matt also designed the centralized payments gateway across **12 countries**, handling message transformation, protocol translation, and routing to payment networks. Alongside that work, he led payments data migration from regional systems, preserving client configurations, entitlements, transaction histories, and compliance traceability. ACCESS launched in **April 2011**; the migration completed **without disruption to live payment operations**. Corporate treasurers gained a single entry point for payments across regions, with unified cash reporting.

At **American Express**, the Virtual Payments Platform’s adoption was slowed by missing SDKs, fragmented documentation, dependency bottlenecks, and no sandbox—particularly for clients without mature development teams. Matt led developer-portal work that provided Java, .NET, and Python SDKs, sample integrations, use case-driven documentation, and secure virtualized testing environments. His team used a Client Onboarding Test Drive to gather end-user feedback.

Client onboarding time fell, and API consumption and trial usage increased within **3 months** of relaunch. The portal delivery also exceeded the team’s commitment by **over 261 story points** of customer-focused value.
