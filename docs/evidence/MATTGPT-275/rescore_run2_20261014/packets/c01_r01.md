# Conversation 1, run 1

## Turn 1. Visitor: Tell me about his payments work.

### A

Matt’s payments work includes building the payment engine and global gateway for **JP Morgan Chase’s** ACCESS platform, consolidating legacy payment systems, and improving developer integration for **American Express’s** Virtual Payments Platform.

At **JP Morgan Chase**, ACCESS needed capabilities beyond Bottomline Technologies’ WebSeries offering. Different payment types had distinct formatting, validation, compliance, and workflow requirements. Regional systems also had different data models, entitlements, and transaction histories—all while live payment processing had to continue.

As Technology Delivery Lead, Matt managed a blended team of **40+** **Accenture** and **JP Morgan Chase** resources across Mumbai, Bangalore, and the US. He defined the onshore-offshore engagement model and spent **70–80%** of his time embedded at Bottomline’s Portsmouth offices, coordinating with bank stakeholders in Chicago and NY/NJ.

He designed and delivered ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and tax-payment modules, alongside Message Authentication Code security and payment-type-specific entitlements. He also designed the centralized gateway for message transformation, protocol translation, and routing across **12 countries**. He coordinated with compliance teams on legacy-data verification and owned production incident response, including cross-team root-cause analysis and daily leadership calls.

ACCESS launched in **April 2011**, serving **135,000+** clients across **180+ countries**. The gateway gave corporate treasurers a single entry point across regions; the legacy migration completed **without disruption to live payment operations**.

At **American Express**, adoption of its Virtual Payments Platform lagged because SDKs were missing, documentation was fragmented, and clients lacked a sandbox. Matt brought business and technical stakeholders together through North Star and Discovery and Framing workshops. He led developer-portal changes, cross-stack SDKs, use case-driven documentation, and secure virtualized testing environments, then gathered feedback through a Client Onboarding Test Drive.

API consumption and trial usage increased within **3 months** of relaunch, client onboarding time decreased, and the portal team exceeded its delivery commitment by **over 261 story points** of customer-focused value.

## Turn 3. Visitor: What went wrong?

### A

🐾 The clearest production failure was in the payment security layer at **JP Morgan Chase**.

A Message Authentication Code (MAC) failure—the layer corporate users relied on to validate payment integrity before release—was traced to missing unit tests and weak error handling. Matt had break-glass production access and queried live payment data through SQL to triage the defect. The story does not specify the outage duration, how many payments were affected, or whether customers suffered financial losses.

There were also production defects after the legacy-system cutover, primarily data issues carried over from the old platform during migration. The regional systems had different schemas, client configurations, entitlement structures, and compliance records. Consolidating them into ACCESS required preserving those details while live payment processing continued. Matt ran UAT and production validation/shakeout testing, coordinated data verification with compliance teams, and triaged the post-cutover defects.

Across successive gateway releases, waves, and migrations, Matt repeatedly owned Sev-**1** incident response. He brought together the New York team, engineers in Mumbai and Bangalore, and Bottomline Technologies for cross-team root-cause analysis. While incidents were live, he ran status updates and daily leadership calls rather than leaving technical investigation and stakeholder communications disconnected.

The underlying delivery constraint was that WebSeries’ out-of-box capabilities did not cover **JP Morgan Chase’s** payment, security, and workflow requirements. Matt coordinated with Bottomline on where native platform capabilities ended and custom engineering was required.

Despite the production defects, the legacy migration completed **without disruption to live payment operations**. ACCESS launched in **April 2011**. Those outcomes do not mean the rollout was defect-free.
