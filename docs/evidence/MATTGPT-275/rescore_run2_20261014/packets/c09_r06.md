## Turn 2. Visitor: What’s his leadership style?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt leads in practice.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. In teams he inherited, engineers were often over-processed, under-trusted, and waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes. At **Accenture’s** Cloud Innovation Center, some teams avoided risks because they feared blame; elsewhere, hierarchical structures and silos between engineering, design, and product slowed delivery.

He made intent explicit, pushed decisions to the people closest to the work, and coached teams to think clearly—not just execute tasks. His “permit to fail” environment included non-punitive post-mortems and lean, low-stakes experiments. Teams shared errors openly, applied lessons in subsequent sprints, and began experimenting more freely. He assembled balanced teams of engineers, product managers, and UX architects with equal participation and shared accountability, using pair programming, retrospectives, and direct feedback to reduce handoffs.

At **Norfolk Southern**, that meant taking ownership when the client reported rising defects despite near-complete test coverage. Rather than defending the dashboard, Matt audited the codebase and test suite and showed the gaps openly. An engineer was writing tests after the code, and vague backlog stories lacked defined scenarios or a definition of done.

Matt rebuilt the backlog with SMART, testable stories and brought frontend and backend TDD experts alongside the team. Coaching followed an “I do, we do, you do” progression, supported by boot camps, coding katas, and practice learning sessions. The client stopped finding defects the dashboard could not see, and defect rates dropped.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams accelerated delivery **4x** once they embraced lean product and modern engineering practices. In the balanced-team work, project delays declined, team satisfaction and retention improved, and teams achieved **zero-defect production code**.

## Turn 3. Visitor: Give me an example.

### A

🐾 Here’s a specific example from **Norfolk Southern**.

During the Cloud Innovation Center’s first major engagement in early **2020**, Matt’s team was modernizing the railroad’s legacy Revenue and Shipment Planning platform. A couple of months in, as the pandemic forced work remote, the client reported rising defects—even though the dashboards showed near-complete test coverage. Both the client and **Accenture** account leadership, already accustomed to waterfall delivery, saw the problem.

Matt took ownership of the failure rather than defending the metrics. He ran a bottom-up audit of the codebase and test suite and showed the gaps openly. An engineer had been building from his own interpretation rather than the backlog story, then writing tests after the code. The backlog also lacked defined scenarios and a definition of done, leaving no clear way to test the requirements.

Matt rebuilt the backlog with SMART, testable stories. He brought frontend and backend TDD experts from Columbus to work side by side with the team and redo the code and test cases properly. Coaching followed an “I do, we do, you do” progression through boot camps, coding katas, practice learning sessions, and pairing—not just instructions to follow a process.

After the experts left, he reinforced the daily workflow: rotating pairs, test cases before the solution, and checks at standup. Stories entered sprints only when testable and small enough for a **one-week** iteration.

The client stopped finding defects the dashboard could not see. Defect rates dropped, client confidence was restored, and the failure mode did not recur after the outside experts went home.
