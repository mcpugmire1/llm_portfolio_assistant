## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt scale engineering teams from 4 to 150+ people?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt built and scaled the organization.

The CIC growth story starts with a **10-person pilot**, rather than **4**, and reaches **150+ practitioners** across engineering, architecture, product, and design. At **Accenture**, growing demand required new Cloud Innovation Centers in Atlanta and Tampa. Many new hires lacked hands-on cloud-native or Agile experience, so expansion required recruiting, training, and a way to manage people without adding layers of supervision.

Matt built a hiring engine across **4 sourcing channels**: bench search, campus recruiting, contract-to-hire, and approved job requisitions. Engineers completed hands-on TDD pairing auditions; product managers and designers worked through live scoping exercises. Interviewers recorded independent votes, and a failed pairing audition ended the candidacy. Matt held the closing interview and made the final decision on every offer. During scaling pushes, the process produced **4–6 net-new practitioners per week**, with interview participation capped to protect delivery time.

New hires progressed through structured “I do, we do, you do” learning, tailored technical training, and round-robin pairing with seasoned practitioners. Matt embedded pair programming, TDD, CI/CD, user research, and Build-Measure-Learn cycles into daily work—not just classroom instruction. Weekly peer-led tech talks, engineering forums, and learning within live client projects continued that development.

The organization remained deliberately flat: **11–13 practice and client leads** reported directly to Matt. Balanced teams held execution power, while experienced engineering “anchors” kept leads informed without becoming another management layer. Monday allocation reviews assigned practitioners across engagements and internal learning tracks. Because managers did not sit beside daily teams, evaluations drew on evidence from recent pair partners, product managers, and anchors; career-level expectations and promotion calibration supplied the development structure.

Over **4 years**, the centers reached **150+ trained practitioners**, with near-zero attrition in the first **2 years**. CIC delivery results included **4X faster velocity**, zero-defect deployments, and **10-person teams** delivering equivalent output to traditional **20-person teams**.

## Turn 2. Visitor: Tell me about his payments work

### A

🐾 Here’s Matt’s payments work, from payment engines to cross-border workflows and platform adoption.

At **JP Morgan Chase**, ACCESS Next Generation had to serve **135,**000+ client**s** across **180+ countries**, but Bottomline’s WebSeries platform did not provide all the required payment modules, security controls, or workflows. Regional legacy systems also had different data models, entitlements, and transaction histories. Connectivity to payment networks across **12 countries** involved different formats, protocols, and regulatory requirements.

As Technology Delivery Lead, Matt managed a **40+ person** blended team across **Accenture** and bank resources in Mumbai, Bangalore, and the US. He designed and delivered ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and federal tax payment modules. His work included NACHA file validation, Message Authentication Code security, payment-type-specific entitlements, sanctions screening, and risk-based approval controls.

He also delivered the centralized payments gateway for message transformation, protocol translation, and routing to clearing networks. For the legacy-system migration, he coordinated data verification with compliance teams, preserved client configurations and transaction histories, and introduced compliance dashboards. He owned production support and critical incident response.

ACCESS launched in **April 2011**. The gateway enabled payments across **12 countries** through a unified architecture, and the migration completed **without disruption to live payment operations**.

At **American Express**, adoption of its Virtual Payments Platform lagged because SDKs were missing, documentation was fragmented, and clients had no sandbox. Matt led work on cross-stack SDKs, use case-driven API documentation, and a secure sandbox. API consumption and trial usage increased within **3 months**, and client onboarding time decreased; the story does not quantify that reduction.

At **RBC**, Matt designed cross-border workflows spanning initiation, settlement, and reconciliation, with real-time FX integration, automated alerts, and batch processing. He delivered an implementation blueprint ready for development execution; revised payment cutoff processes reduced wire-transfer delays and improved SLA compliance.
