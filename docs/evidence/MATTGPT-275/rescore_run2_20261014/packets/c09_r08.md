Matt partnered with business and technical stakeholders in North Star and Discovery and Framing workshops. He led developer-portal changes, cross-stack SDKs, use-case-driven documentation, secure sandbox environments, and environment virtualization for earlier testing. He coordinated client teams and ran a Client Onboarding Test Drive to gather end-user feedback. API trial usage increased within **3 months** of relaunch, onboarding time decreased, and the portal team exceeded its delivery commitment by over **261 story points** of customer-focused value.

## Turn 2. Visitor: What’s his leadership style?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes—and practices—his leadership.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. Across teams he inherited, engineers were often “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” At **Accenture’s** Cloud Innovation Center, a rigid hierarchical culture also made people slow to share ideas, while fear of blame discouraged experimentation.

He made intent explicit, pushed decisions to the people closest to the work, and used transparency, direct feedback, and open communication. He coached teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks. He brought product managers, designers, and engineers together into balanced teams, prioritized essential features, and delivered thin-slice, production-ready solutions for rapid user feedback.

His “permit to fail” environment involved specific practices: non-punitive post-mortems, transparent sharing of errors, and lean, low-stakes experiments with structured hypotheses and controlled iterations. Teams applied post-mortem learnings to subsequent sprints to prevent recurring failure patterns. In early-stage product work, he set expectations that prototypes and user tests would likely fail—and treated those results as evidence for shaping product direction, rather than as incompetence or rework.

Matt also established weekly brainstorming sessions where ideas were considered without judgment. He emphasized the shared mission over individual accolades and recognized contributions that advanced project goals.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams accelerated delivery **4x** after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. Teams began experimenting more freely, repeat errors declined, and delivery timelines shortened. Client teams continued using Lean methodologies and iterative delivery practices after engagements ended.

## Turn 3. Visitor: Give me an example.

### A

🐾 Here’s a concrete example from **Norfolk Southern**.

In early **2020**, Matt’s Cloud Innovation Center team was modernizing the client’s legacy Revenue and Shipment Planning platform. A couple of months into the engagement, just as the pandemic moved everyone to remote work, the client reported rising defects—even though the team’s dashboards showed near-complete test coverage. The concerns landed in front of both the client and **Accenture** account leadership, who were already accustomed to waterfall delivery.

Matt took ownership of the failure rather than defending the metrics. He ran a bottom-up audit of the codebase and test suite and shared the gaps openly. An engineer had been building from his own interpretation instead of the backlog story, then writing tests after the code. The backlog was also part of the problem: stories lacked defined scenarios and a definition of done, leaving no clear way to test them.

Matt rebuilt the backlog with SMART, testable stories. He brought frontend and backend test-driven development experts from Columbus to work side by side with the team, then had the code and test cases redone properly. Coaching followed an “I do, we do, you do” progression, supported by boot camps, coding katas, practice learning sessions, and lunch and learns. He also restructured agile ceremonies and delivered product management and SMART story training.

Defect rates dropped as story quality and test-first discipline took hold. The client stopped finding defects the dashboard could not see, and client and account-leadership confidence returned through the open audit and visible improvement.
