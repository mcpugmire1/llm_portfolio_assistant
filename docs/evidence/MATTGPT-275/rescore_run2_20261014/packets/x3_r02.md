## Turn 1. Visitor: Has Matt worked on trading platforms?

### A

🐾 Matt has worked on FX/cross-border payments and trade finance integration, but his stories do not document work on a securities trading or trade-execution platform.

At **JP Morgan Chase**, Matt was Technology Delivery Lead for ACCESS Next Generation, a global corporate banking portal for treasury services and cash management. The bank needed payment modules, security controls, and workflows beyond what **Bottomline Technologies’** WebSeries platform provided out of the box.

Matt built the FX/cross-border payment module, integrating it with the bank’s foreign exchange engine so corporate treasurers could initiate cross-currency payments with embedded rate visibility and connectivity to international clearing systems. He also drove the groundwork for trade finance integration, enabling users to view a trade transaction’s lifecycle from initiation to final payment. These were corporate banking and payment capabilities—not documented securities order management or trade execution.

He managed a blended team of **40+** across the US, Mumbai, and Bangalore. ACCESS launched in April **2011**, serving **135,000+** clients across **180+** countries. He also led the payments data migration from regional systems without disruption to live payment operations.

At **RBC**, Matt assessed WebSeries for a corporate banking payment hub; that engagement was not a trading platform implementation. At **Cendian Chemical Logistics**, he built integrations and onboarded a plastics client through webMethods B2B Trading Networks. There, “trading” meant exchanging shipment, order, and invoice documents with logistics partners—not financial-market trading.
