## Turn 2. Visitor: What’s his leadership style?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes—and practices—his leadership.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. He describes teams he inherited as “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” At **Accenture’s** Cloud Innovation Center, even a model designed around autonomy encountered old command-and-control habits: decisions drifted upward, teams waited for direction, and bottlenecks returned. Moving away from those habits required repeated enforcement and revalidation; the path was bumpy.

Matt pushed decisions to the people closest to the work. He brought product managers, designers, and engineers together in balanced teams with shared ownership and decision-making authority. Team leads focused on removing obstacles rather than controlling outcomes. He made intent explicit, used direct feedback and open communication, and coached teams to think clearly—not just execute tasks.

When fear of blame stopped people from experimenting, Matt established a “permit to fail” environment. He organized non-punitive post-mortems, focused discussions on lessons rather than finger-pointing, and supported lean, low-stakes experiments with structured hypotheses and controlled iterations. Teams applied those lessons in subsequent sprints, preventing recurrence of identified failure patterns and shortening project timelines.

He also introduced weekly brainstorming sessions where ideas were considered without judgment. Rather than emphasizing individual accolades, he recognized contributions that advanced shared project goals.

Alongside those practices, Matt grounded engineering culture in automation, continuous delivery, and quality-first practices. At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams achieved **4x delivery acceleration** after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. It did not happen overnight, but the practices stuck.

## Turn 3. Visitor: Give me an example.

### A

🐾 Here’s a concrete example from **Norfolk Southern**.

In early **2020**, Matt’s Cloud Innovation Center team was modernizing the company’s legacy Revenue and Shipment Planning platform. A couple of months in, as the pandemic shifted work to remote, the client reported rising defects—even though the dashboards showed near-complete test coverage. The concerns landed in front of both the client and **Accenture** account leadership, who were already accustomed to waterfall delivery.

Matt took ownership of the failure rather than defending the metrics. He ran a bottom-up audit of the codebase and test suite and showed the gaps openly. An engineer had been building from his own interpretation instead of the backlog story, then writing tests after the code. Coverage looked complete, but defects went undetected. The backlog also lacked defined scenarios and a definition of done.

Instead of simply adding more process, Matt rebuilt the backlog with SMART, testable stories and brought in frontend and backend test-driven development experts from Columbus to sit side by side with the team. They demonstrated the discipline by doing the work, then redid the code and test cases properly.

He used the Samman coaching approach: boot camps, coding katas, practice learning sessions, lunch and learns, and pairing engineers with subject-matter experts through an “I do, we do, you do” progression. He also restructured agile ceremonies and delivered product management and SMART story training.

Defect rates dropped as story quality and test-first discipline took hold. The client stopped finding defects the dashboard could not see, and client confidence was restored through the open audit and visible improvement.
