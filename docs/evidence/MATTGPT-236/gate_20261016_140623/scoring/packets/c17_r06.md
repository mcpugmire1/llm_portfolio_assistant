### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt changed the day-to-day work.

At Fiserv, the 45-person team had no visibility into work status. Requirements lived in a 200-page BRD organized by system modules, and waterfall releases ran on 6-month cycles. Developers were in India, testers in the Philippines, and designers in Ireland. Multiple deadlines had slipped, stakeholders were threatening contract cancellation, and PMO and compliance teams resisted moving to agile.

Matt changed how work was defined and tracked. He decomposed the BRD into persona-based user stories and restructured JIRA from a single To Do/Done board into a multi-tiered Kanban/Scrum architecture. The 47 acceptance criteria became Epics, with stories moving through specific workflow columns.

He established follow-the-sun synchronization: a 7 AM CST scrum-of-scrums for offshore-to-onshore handoffs and 11 PM CST syncs for the return handoff. He introduced bi-weekly sprint demos so stakeholders could sign off on branding, UI, and ADA compliance early, rather than discovering issues at month 6. When stakeholders lacked bandwidth for that feedback cadence, he negotiated dedicated review windows.

Matt maintained existing steering committee reporting while adding sprint-level visibility. To address PMO and compliance resistance, he used JIRA as the system of record, with audit trails, attached ADA scans, and developer comments instead of static Word documents. He also coached offshore teams on empowerment over command-and-control and made screen reader audits part of the Definition of Done.

The team moved to 2-week sprints within 3 months. JIRA provided real-time visibility to 50+ stakeholders across 3 time zones and satisfied PMO and compliance audit requirements. Separately, build and deployment changes added mandatory senior-developer reviews, unit tests, and automated quality gates, supporting the new delivery cadence.
