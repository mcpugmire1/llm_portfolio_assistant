### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt changed the working model.

At Fiserv, the 45-person team was working through 6-month waterfall release cycles. Requirements lived in a 200-page business requirements document organized by system modules, and the team had no visibility into work status. Developers were in India, testers in the Philippines, and designers in Ireland. Multiple deadlines had been missed, stakeholders were threatening contract cancellation, and PMO and compliance teams resisted agile delivery.

Matt replaced the single To Do/Done JIRA board with a multi-tiered Kanban/Scrum structure. He tracked the 47 acceptance criteria as Epics and decomposed the requirements into persona-based user stories rather than system modules. He established 7 AM CST scrum-of-scrums for offshore-to-onshore handoffs and 11 PM CST synchronization for the return handoff.

Instead of waiting until month 6 to discover branding, UI, or accessibility problems, Matt introduced bi-weekly sprint demos for Fiserv stakeholders. He negotiated dedicated review windows when stakeholders lacked bandwidth for that feedback cadence. He kept existing steering committee reporting while adding sprint-level visibility.

For compliance, Matt used JIRA as the system of record, with audit trails, attached accessibility scans, and developer comments rather than static Word documents. He also coached offshore teams on empowerment over command-and-control and made screen reader audits part of the Definition of Done.

Alongside that change, he replaced SVN with Git, required senior-developer code reviews and unit tests, and introduced automated builds and quality gates. Builds fell from days to hours.

The team moved to 2-week sprints within 3 months. JIRA gave 50+ stakeholders across 3 time zones real-time visibility, and PMO and compliance accepted it for audit requirements. The overall program met all 47 acceptance criteria with zero critical defects at launch.
