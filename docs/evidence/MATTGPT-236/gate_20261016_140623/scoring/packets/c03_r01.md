Across the Cloud Innovation Center, teams accelerated delivery 4x after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. The balanced-team work also achieved zero-defect production code through iterative development and real-time feedback loops.

## Turn 2. Visitor: Give me an example where that didn’t work.

### A

🐾 Yes. At Norfolk Southern, the engineering practices Matt introduced did not hold up initially.

On the Cloud Innovation Center’s first major engagement, his team was modernizing the Revenue and Shipment Planning platform. Matt brought in a balanced team and emphasized test-driven development. A couple of months in, as the pandemic forced remote work, the client reported rising defects despite dashboards showing near-complete test coverage. The credibility hit landed in front of both the client and Accenture account leadership.

Pairing, test-first development, and continuous integration had been in place from the start, but the follow-up discipline was missing. An engineer was building from his own interpretation rather than the backlog story and writing tests after the code. The backlog also contained vague stories without defined scenarios or a definition of done. Coverage looked complete while defects went undetected.

Matt took ownership of the failure rather than defending the metrics. He audited the codebase and test suite, exposed the backlog gaps, and brought in frontend and backend TDD experts to work alongside the engineers. The team rebuilt stories and redid code and tests through an “I do, we do, you do” progression.

He then changed the daily workflow rather than relying on training alone. Building test cases became a discrete step before engineering the solution, inside a pair, checked at standup. Pairs rotated daily, and stories entered sprints only when they were testable and small enough for a one-week iteration.

The client stopped finding defects the dashboard could not see. The practices held after the experts left, and the original failure mode did not recur. Matt’s own account names what had been missing: “reinforcement, follow-up, and verification.”
