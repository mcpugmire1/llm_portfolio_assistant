## Turn 3. Visitor: Give me an example.

### A

🐾 Here’s a specific example from Norfolk Southern.

During the Cloud Innovation Center’s first major engagement in 2020, Matt’s team was modernizing the Revenue and Shipment Planning platform. A couple of months in, the client reported rising defects, even though the dashboards showed near-complete test coverage. The concerns landed in front of both the client and Accenture account leadership, just as the pandemic forced the team into remote work.

Matt took ownership of the failure rather than defending the metrics. He ran a bottom-up audit of the codebase and test suite and showed the gaps openly. An engineer had been building from his own interpretation rather than the backlog story, then writing tests after the code. The backlog was also part of the problem: stories lacked defined scenarios and a definition of done.

Instead of simply demanding better results, Matt rebuilt the backlog with SMART, testable stories and brought in frontend and backend TDD experts from Columbus to work alongside the engineers. They redid the code and tests, then reinforced the practices through boot camps, coding katas, and an “I do, we do, you do” coaching progression.

He also changed the daily workflow: pairs rotated daily, test cases came before the solution, and standups checked that sequence. New engineers paired with seasoned practitioners rather than being left to interpret the process alone.

Defect rates dropped, and the client stopped finding defects the dashboard could not see. After the visiting experts left, pairing and test-first remained the team’s default, and the failure pattern did not recur.
