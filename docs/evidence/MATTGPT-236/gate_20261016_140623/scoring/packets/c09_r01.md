## Turn 3. Visitor: Give me an example.

### A

🐾 Here’s an example from Norfolk Southern.

In 2020, Matt’s team was modernizing the Revenue and Shipment Planning platform when the client reported rising defects. The dashboards showed near-complete test coverage. The feedback landed in front of both the client and Accenture account leadership, just as the pandemic forced the team into remote work.

Matt took ownership of the failure rather than defending the metrics. He ran a bottom-up audit of the codebase and test suite and showed the gaps openly. An engineer had been building from his own interpretation, then writing tests after the code. The backlog also contained vague stories without defined scenarios or a definition of done, leaving no clear way to test whether the work met requirements.

Instead of simply telling engineers to follow test-driven development, Matt rebuilt the backlog with SMART, testable stories and brought frontend and backend TDD experts from Columbus to work alongside the team. They redid the code and tests, then coached through boot camps, coding katas, and an “I do, we do, you do” progression.

He also changed the daily workflow so the correction would last after the experts left. Pairs rotated daily, test cases came before implementation, and standups checked that sequence. Stories entered sprints only when testable and small enough for a 1-week iteration. During remote work, pairs traded drivers frequently.

Defect rates dropped, and the client stopped finding defects the dashboard could not see. Pairing and test-first remained the team’s default after the experts left, and the failure mode that caused the crisis did not recur.
