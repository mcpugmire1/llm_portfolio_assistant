## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt scale engineering teams from 4 to 150+ people?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt built the organization.

The stories describe an initial 10-person pilot, rather than 4, growing to 150+ practitioners across engineering, architecture, product, and design at Accenture’s Atlanta and Tampa Cloud Innovation Centers. Growing client demand required more people, but many new hires lacked hands-on cloud-native or Agile experience. Matt also needed hiring, development, and performance management to work without adding layers of managers who did not sit with delivery teams.

He built a hiring engine across 4 channels: internal bench searches, campus recruiting, contract-to-hire, and approved job requisitions. Engineers completed hands-on TDD pairing auditions rather than relying on interview answers alone. Matt held the closing interview and made every final hiring decision. During scaling pushes, the process handled 15 to 20 auditions and added 4 to 6 practitioners per week. Interviewers were capped at 2 slots weekly so recruiting did not compromise delivery or burn people out.

New hires moved through structured enablement and round-robin pairing with seasoned practitioners. The learning model was “I do, we do, you do,” progressing from guided work to independent delivery. Senior engineers coached junior colleagues, while weekly tech talks, peer-led demos, and practice forums kept learning embedded in the working week.

Matt kept the organization deliberately flat, with 11 to 13 practice and client leads reporting directly to him. Experienced engineers served as team anchors rather than another management layer. Monday allocation reviews matched practitioners to engagements and internal learning work. Because managers did not observe daily delivery, evaluations drew on evidence from recent pair partners, product managers, and anchors. Career expectations, promotion calibration, and regular development conversations gave practitioners explicit paths forward.

The organization reached 150+ practitioners, with near-zero attrition in the first 2 years and multiple hires promoted. The hiring, feedback, and promotion processes continued through 4 years of scaling.

## Turn 2. Visitor: Tell me about his payments work

### A

🐾 Matt’s payments work includes global payment processing at JP Morgan Chase and developer integration for American Express’s Virtual Payments Platform.

Regional systems had different data models, entitlements, payment formats, and compliance requirements. The portal was intended to serve 135,000+ clients across 180+ countries, while live payment operations continued throughout the transition.

As Technology Delivery Lead, Matt managed a blended team of 40+ Accenture and bank practitioners across the US, Mumbai, and Bangalore. He defined the onshore-offshore engagement model and spent 70 to 80% of his time embedded with Bottomline in Portsmouth, coordinating with bank stakeholders in Chicago and NY/NJ. He managed where native platform functionality ended and bespoke engineering was required.

He also delivered the centralized gateway connecting payment channels across 12 countries. For migration, he coordinated with compliance teams, introduced compliance dashboards, and owned production validation and defect triage. During critical incidents, he directed cross-team root-cause analysis and daily leadership calls.

ACCESS launched in April 2011. The gateway replaced fragmented integrations, and legacy client configurations, entitlements, and transaction histories were consolidated without disruption to live payment operations.

At American Express, clients struggled to adopt its Virtual Payments Platform because SDKs were missing, documentation was fragmented, and there was no sandbox. Matt brought business and technical teams together through Discovery and Framing workshops. His work included cross-stack SDKs, use-case-driven documentation, virtualized testing environments, and a Client Onboarding Test Drive. API trial usage increased within 3 months, onboarding time decreased, and the team delivered 261+ additional story points beyond its commitment.
