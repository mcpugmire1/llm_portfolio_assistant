## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt scale engineering teams from 4 to 150+ people?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt built and ran that organization.

The CIC stories describe growth from a 10-person pilot to 150+ practitioners, rather than a starting team of 4. At Accenture, Matt built the Atlanta and Tampa Cloud Innovation Centers as client demand grew. Many new hires lacked hands-on cloud-native or Agile experience. The organization also needed ways to hire, develop, and evaluate people without adding traditional management layers.

Matt built a hiring engine across bench search, campus recruiting, contract-to-hire, and approved job requisitions. Instead of relying only on interviews, engineers completed hands-on TDD pairing auditions; product managers and designers worked through live scoping exercises. Interviewers recorded independent votes, and Matt held the closing interview and made every final hiring decision. During scaling pushes, the process added 4 to 6 net-new practitioners per week, with interview participation capped at 2 slots per practitioner per week to protect delivery.

New joiners moved through structured enablement and round-robin pairing with seasoned practitioners. The learning model was “I do, we do, you do,” progressing from guided to independent delivery. Skills matrices, senior-junior mentoring, peer-led demos, and learning inside live client projects supported continued development.

Matt kept the organization deliberately flat: 11 to 13 practice and client leads reported directly to him. Balanced teams combined engineering, product, and design, with experienced engineering anchors inside teams rather than another management layer. Monday allocation reviews matched practitioners to engagements and internal learning work. Because managers did not sit on daily teams, evaluations drew on evidence from recent pair partners, product managers, and anchors, rather than visibility to the boss.

The organization reached 150+ practitioners, with near-zero attrition in the first 2 years and multiple hires promoted. Across the CIC delivery model, 10-person balanced teams delivered equivalent output to traditional 20-person teams, with 4X faster velocity and zero-defect deployments.

## Turn 2. Visitor: Tell me about his payments work

### A

🐾 Matt’s payments work includes building the payment engine behind JP Morgan Chase ACCESS and improving adoption of American Express’s Virtual Payments Platform.

At JP Morgan Chase, regional payment systems had different formats, protocols, client configurations, and compliance records. Bottomline Technologies’ WebSeries platform provided the foundation for ACCESS, but the bank needed purpose-built capabilities for 135,000+ clients across 180+ countries.

As Technology Delivery Lead, Matt managed a blended 40+ person Accenture and bank team across the US, Mumbai, and Bangalore. He defined the onshore-offshore engagement model and spent 70 to 80% of his time embedded with Bottomline in Portsmouth, coordinating with bank teams in Chicago and NY/NJ. He designed and delivered ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and tax payment modules, alongside payment verification, payment-type-specific permissions, sanctions screening, and risk-based approval controls.

He also designed the centralized gateway connecting ACCESS to payment rails across 12 countries, handling message transformation, protocol translation, and routing. For the legacy migration, he coordinated data verification with compliance teams, introduced compliance dashboards, and owned production validation and defect triage. ACCESS launched in April 2011; the migration completed without disruption to live payment operations. Matt also directed critical production incident response, cross-team root-cause analysis, and daily leadership calls.

At American Express, the challenge was adoption: missing SDKs, fragmented documentation, no sandbox, and delayed testing made the Virtual Payments Platform difficult to evaluate and integrate. Matt brought business and technical teams together through Discovery and Framing workshops, then led work on the developer portal, Java/.NET/Python SDKs, use-case-driven documentation, and secure virtualized testing environments. A Client Onboarding Test Drive gathered end-user feedback.

API consumption and trial usage increased within 3 months of relaunch, onboarding time decreased, and the portal team delivered over 261 additional story points beyond its delivery commitment.
