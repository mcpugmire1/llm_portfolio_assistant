## Turn 1. Visitor: Tell me about his Norfolk Southern work.

### A

🐾 Here’s the story behind Matt’s Norfolk Southern work.

From November 2019 to September 2023, Matt worked as a Director on modernizing the railroad’s Revenue and Shipment Planning platform, the Cloud Innovation Center’s first engagement. Core scheduling, billing, and order-to-cash ran on a mainframe dating to the 1970s. Orders could fall out unnoticed until customers called, business rules lived in COBOL and assembly maintained by a shrinking workforce, and clerks manually handled waybill and rating fallout through green-screen consoles.

Matt led domain-driven design and event storming to map the existing business functions and dependencies. Rather than replace the mainframe outright, his team kept it as the system of record while building APIs, microservices, and CockroachDB alongside it. Change data capture synchronized the platforms, and reconciliation checked that nothing was lost. When the client grew restless for tangible progress, Matt brought its revenue management team into the innovation hub to co-create the Shipper Revenue System interface through personas, prototypes, and weekly build-measure-learn iterations.

The work also hit a quality crisis: defects were rising despite near-complete test coverage. Matt took ownership, audited the code and tests, and found tests written after code alongside vague backlog stories. He brought in TDD experts, rebuilt stories with defined scenarios and a definition of done, and reinforced test-first development through coding katas and rotating pairs. The client stopped finding defects the dashboard could not see, and that failure mode did not recur after the experts left.

Alongside delivery, Matt led a 2-day CIC Academy course, delivered twice monthly over 6 months, and paired client contributors into the new build to learn Angular, Node, Spring Boot, and cloud-native practices.

The MVP launched covering 95% of its targeted use cases, capturing real orders with clean data feeding the mainframe. EDI validation changed fallout detection from customer complaints to proactive trading-partner notifications. Delivery scaled to 3 balanced teams, while client contributors gained the skills to support and manage the new application themselves.

### A

🐾 Here’s Matt’s RBC work.

From June 2012 to June 2013, Matt was a Senior Technology Architecture Manager on the bank’s Day2Day Corporate Banking modernization. RBC was evaluating Bottomline WebSeries as its payment hub engine while planning to replace the aging RBC Express portal. Existing systems had performance and scalability limitations, lacked mobile capabilities, and had no API integration with external financial applications. Leadership needed to know whether the proposed platform could meet the business requirements before committing further investment.

Matt conducted an architecture assessment and gap/fit analysis, drawing on prior WebSeries implementation experience. He reviewed code, interviewed stakeholders, analyzed documentation, and created architectural heat maps. Working with the bank’s enterprise architecture team, he delivered target-system models and a cost-to-fix versus benefit comparison. Workshops brought RBC leadership together around the technical findings: WebSeries lacked the modularity required for the bank’s long-term digital operating model. The assessment prevented further investment in that platform path and enabled resources to move to higher-priority initiatives.

Alongside that assessment, Matt established the PMO across RBC, Accenture, and Bottomline Technologies. He defined responsibilities, maintained the program roadmap and dependencies, and led governance meetings. Designated team contacts, daily summaries, and a task-tracking matrix eliminated missed program updates and reduced redundant effort. He also owned engagement margin, staffing senior specialists early and transitioning repeatable work offshore and nearshore as the backlog matured.

When the previous data conversion lead left, Matt took over planning from scratch. He coordinated business partners, IT teams, vendors, and onshore and offshore contributors on transformation rules, mock conversion cycles, and reconciliation checks for payment amounts and beneficiary details.

His deliverables included an end-to-end conversion plan and a mobile modernization blueprint covering API integration, cheque deposit requirements, and cross-device scalability. The architecture assessment’s heat maps and roadmap remained reference material for future modernization planning.
