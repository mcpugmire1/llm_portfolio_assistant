## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt scale engineering teams from 4 to 150+ people?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt built the organization.

The CIC stories describe growth from a 10-person pilot to 150+ practitioners, rather than a starting team of 4. At Accenture’s Atlanta and Tampa Cloud Innovation Centers, the challenge was not just hiring: many new joiners lacked hands-on cloud-native or Agile experience. Matt also needed to run hiring, development, evaluation, and promotions without adding layers of managers who did not work alongside the teams.

He built a hiring engine across 4 channels: internal bench searches, campus recruiting, contract-to-hire, and approved job requisitions. Engineers completed hands-on TDD pairing auditions; product managers and designers worked through live scoping exercises. Interviewers recorded independent votes, and Matt held the closing interview and made every final hiring decision. During scaling pushes, the process produced 4 to 6 net-new practitioners per week. Interview participation was capped at 2 slots per practitioner per week to protect delivery time and avoid burning out interviewers.

New hires moved into tailored enablement and round-robin pairing with experienced practitioners. The learning model was “I do, we do, you do,” progressing from guided work to independent delivery. Skills matrices, mentoring, weekly peer-led demos, and career-development conversations continued after onboarding.

Matt kept the organization deliberately flat, with 11 to 13 practice and client leads reporting directly to him. Balanced teams combined product, design, and engineering, with experienced engineering anchors inside the teams rather than another management layer. Monday allocation reviews matched practitioners to client work and internal learning based on needs, skills, and location.

Because managers did not sit on daily teams, evaluations drew on recent pairing partners, product managers, and anchors. Matt ran monthly feedback sessions and facilitated promotion calibration, so advancement depended on evidence from daily teammates rather than visibility to a boss.

The organization reached 150+ practitioners, with near-zero attrition in the first 2 years and multiple hires promoted.

## Turn 2. Visitor: Tell me about his payments work

### A

🐾 Matt’s payments work includes Execution & Delivery at JP Morgan Chase and developer onboarding for American Express.

At JP Morgan Chase, regional payment systems had different formats, protocols, entitlement structures, and compliance records. The bank was building ACCESS Next Generation on Bottomline Technologies’ WebSeries platform, but its requirements went well beyond the out-of-box offering.

As Technology Delivery Lead, Matt managed a blended team of 40+ across Accenture and bank resources in the US, Mumbai, and Bangalore. He defined the onshore-offshore engagement model and spent 70 to 80% of his time embedded with Bottomline’s engineers in Portsmouth, coordinating with bank stakeholders in Chicago and NY/NJ. He determined where platform configuration ended and custom engineering was required.

Matt designed and delivered ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and federal tax payment modules, along with payment verification and payment-type-specific permissions. He also designed the centralized gateway for message transformation, protocol translation, and routing across 12 countries. He coordinated migration with compliance teams, preserved client configurations and transaction histories, and owned production incident response, including cross-team root-cause analysis and daily leadership calls.

ACCESS launched in April 2011, serving 135,000+ clients across 180+ countries. The legacy-system migration completed without disruption to live payment operations.

At American Express, the Virtual Payments Platform faced a different obstacle: missing SDKs, fragmented documentation, dependency bottlenecks, and no sandbox made integration difficult. Matt brought business and technical stakeholders into North Star and Discovery and Framing workshops, then coordinated stable virtualized testing environments with client teams.

The work included Java, .NET, and Python SDKs, use-case-driven documentation, a secure sandbox, and a Client Onboarding Test Drive. API consumption and trial usage increased within 3 months of relaunch, onboarding time fell, and the team exceeded its delivery commitment by over 261 story points.
