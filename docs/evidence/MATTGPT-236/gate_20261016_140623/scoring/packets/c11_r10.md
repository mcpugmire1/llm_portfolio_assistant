## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt scale engineering teams from 4 to 150+ people?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt built and ran the organization.

The CIC stories describe growth from a 10-person pilot to 150+ practitioners, rather than a starting team of 4. That population included engineering, architecture, product, and design across Accenture’s Atlanta and Tampa Cloud Innovation Centers. Growing client demand required more people, but many new hires lacked hands-on cloud-native or Agile experience. Matt also needed hiring, evaluation, and career development to work without adding layers of management.

He built a hiring engine across bench searches, campus recruiting, contract-to-hire, and governed job requisitions. Engineers completed hands-on TDD pairing auditions rather than relying only on interviews. Interviewers recorded independent votes, and Matt held the closing interview and made every final hiring decision. During scaling pushes, the process produced 4 to 6 net-new practitioners per week. Interview participation was capped at 2 slots per practitioner per week so recruiting did not interrupt delivery.

New hires progressed through structured “I do, we do, you do” learning, then paired with seasoned practitioners. Training covered pair programming, TDD, CI/CD, lean product development, and user-centered design. Senior engineers mentored junior colleagues, while weekly tech talks and peer-led demos continued learning beyond onboarding.

Matt kept the organization deliberately flat: 11 to 13 practice and client leads reported directly to him through a 2-layer structure. Experienced engineers served as team anchors without becoming another management layer. Monday allocation reviews assigned practitioners across client engagements, training, and accelerator development. Because managers did not sit beside practitioners daily, performance reviews drew on evidence from recent pair partners, product managers, and anchors. Matt ran feedback capture, promotion calibration, compensation allocation, and remediation decisions.

The organization reached 150+ practitioners, with near-zero attrition in its first 2 years. Delivery continued alongside that growth: at Capital One, a balanced team of 8 delivered defect-free production code at 4x the output of the client’s internal teams.

## Turn 2. Visitor: Tell me about his payments work

### A

🐾 Matt’s payments work covered payment engines, global connectivity, legacy migration, and developer adoption.

At JP Morgan Chase, regional payment systems had different data models, entitlement structures, and compliance records. The bank was building ACCESS Next Generation on Bottomline Technologies’ WebSeries platform, but its requirements went beyond the out-of-box product. The broader portal served 135,000+ clients across 180+ countries, while the payments gateway needed connectivity across 12 countries.

As Technology Delivery Lead, Matt managed a blended team of 40+ across Accenture and bank resources in the US, Mumbai, and Bangalore. He designed and delivered bespoke payment modules for ACH credits and direct debits, wires, FX/cross-border transfers, and federal tax payments. That included NACHA file validation, payment-type-specific permissions, Message Authentication Code security, and integrations with sanctions screening, transaction monitoring, and risk-based approval controls.

He also designed the centralized gateway for message transformation, protocol translation, and payment routing. Rather than retaining separate regional integrations, it connected ACCESS to clearing networks and internal compliance, settlement, and reporting systems. Matt led the payments-workstream data migration, preserving client configurations, entitlements, transaction histories, and compliance traceability without disruption to live payment operations. ACCESS and its Integrated Global Payments Interface launched in April 2011. He also owned on-call and critical incident response for the payment engine.

At American Express, the challenge was adoption of its Virtual Payments Platform: missing SDKs, fragmented documentation, no sandbox, and delayed testing. Matt co-created a developer portal, developed Java, .NET, and Python SDKs, introduced use-case-driven documentation, and built secure sandbox and virtualized testing environments. His team used Lean Product and XP practices and validated the portal through a Client Onboarding Test Drive.

API consumption and trial usage increased within 3 months of relaunch. Onboarding time fell, and the developer-experience team delivered over 261 additional story points beyond its commitment.
