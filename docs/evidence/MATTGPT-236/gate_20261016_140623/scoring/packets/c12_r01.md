## Turn 1. Visitor: Compare his Fiserv and JP Morgan ACCESS work.

### A

🐾 Here’s how the two engagements compare: Fiserv was a troubled-program recovery; JP Morgan ACCESS was a global payments platform build and consolidation.

At Fiserv, Matt took over development management roughly 7 months into an \$8.5M white-label card portal initiative. The 45-person team had missed consecutive deadlines, production quality was poor, and stakeholders were threatening contract cancellation. 3 external vendors had uncoordinated dependencies, while the underlying payment platform was transitioning from mainframe processing to an SOA-based API architecture.

Matt reset the backlog and timelines, coordinated vendors, and established offshore/onshore handoffs. He moved the team from 6-month release cycles to 2-week sprints within 3 months. Instead of module-based requirements in a 200-page BRD, the team used persona-based user stories and sprint demos. He also replaced SVN with Git, mandated code reviews and unit tests, and introduced continuous integration and quality gates.

The program finished 3% under budget, saving \$255K, with 47/47 acceptance criteria and zero critical defects at launch. A recovered release avoided \$500K in penalties; the portal processed \$45M in transactions in its first month, with 99.97% uptime.

At JP Morgan ACCESS, the challenge was building capabilities beyond Bottomline’s WebSeries offering while consolidating disparate regional systems. As Technology Delivery Lead, Matt managed a 40+ person blended team and worked embedded with Bottomline. He designed and delivered ACH, wire, FX/cross-border and tax-payment modules, MAC security, payment-specific entitlements, and gateway integrations across 12 countries.

Rather than configuring permissions individually, his team built role-based profiles and bulk permission changes, reducing entitlement management from days to minutes. He also led payments data migration without disruption to live payment operations. ACCESS launched in April 2011 as a unified portal serving 135,000+ clients across 180+ countries.

The reported outcomes differ: Fiserv records budget, quality and release-recovery results; ACCESS records payment integration, administrative time reduction and uninterrupted migration.
