## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt scale engineering teams from 4 to 150+ people?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt built and ran that organization.

The documented CIC growth was from a 10-person pilot to 150+ practitioners, rather than 4, across engineering, product, and design. At Accenture’s Atlanta and Tampa Cloud Innovation Centers, growing client demand required recruiting people who often lacked hands-on cloud-native or Agile experience. Matt also needed to manage hiring, development, evaluation, and promotion without adding layers of managers who did not work alongside the teams.

He built a hiring engine across internal bench searches, campus recruiting, contract-to-hire, and approved job requisitions. Candidates completed hands-on auditions: engineers paired on test-driven development, while product managers and designers worked through live client briefs. Interviewers recorded independent votes, and Matt held the closing interview and made every final hiring decision. During scaling pushes, the process produced 4 to 6 net-new practitioners per week, with interview participation capped to protect delivery time.

New hires moved through tailored enablement and round-robin pairing with seasoned practitioners. The learning model was “I do, we do, you do,” progressing from guided to independent delivery. Peer-led tech talks, mentoring, skills matrices, and learning within client projects continued after onboarding. Matt also connected the talent pipeline to Georgia Tech through hackathons, recruiting, and job fairs.

The organization stayed deliberately flat: 11 to 13 practice and client leads reported directly to Matt. Balanced teams combined product, engineering, and design, with team anchors keeping leads informed without another management layer. Monday allocation reviews moved practitioners between engagements and internal training or accelerator development. Performance evaluation drew on evidence from recent pair partners, product managers, and anchors, rather than a manager’s impressions.

Over 4 years, the organization reached 150+ practitioners, with near-zero attrition in its first 2 years. Across CIC delivery, 10-person balanced teams delivered equivalent output to traditional 20-person teams, with 4X faster velocity and zero-defect deployments.

## Turn 2. Visitor: Tell me about his payments work

Matt led a blended team of 40+ Accenture and bank resources across the US, Mumbai, and Bangalore. For the legacy migration, he coordinated data verification with compliance teams, preserved client configurations and transaction histories, and introduced compliance dashboards. He owned production incident response, cross-team root-cause analysis, and daily leadership calls while issues were live. ACCESS launched in April 2011; the migration completed without disruption to live payment operations.

Matt brought business and technical teams together through Discovery and Framing workshops, then led work on a redesigned developer portal, Java/.NET/Python SDKs, use case-driven documentation, and secure virtualized testing environments. A Client Onboarding Test Drive gathered end-user feedback.
