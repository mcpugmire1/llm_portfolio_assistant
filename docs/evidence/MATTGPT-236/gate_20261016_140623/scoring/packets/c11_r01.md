## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt scale engineering teams from 4 to 150+ people?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt built and ran that organization.

The CIC stories describe growth from a 10-person pilot to 150+ practitioners, rather than a starting team of 4. At Accenture’s Atlanta and Tampa Cloud Innovation Centers, growing client demand required recruiting people who often lacked hands-on cloud-native or Agile experience. Matt also needed hiring, evaluation, development, and promotion processes that worked without adding layers of managers.

He built a hiring engine across 4 channels: internal bench searches, campus recruiting, contract-to-hire, and job requisitions. Georgia Tech recruiting, hackathons, and job fairs expanded the talent pipeline. Candidates completed hands-on pairing auditions, not just interviews: engineers practiced TDD at a shared workstation, while product and design candidates worked through messy client briefs. Matt held the closing interview and made every final hiring decision. During scaling pushes, the process delivered 4 to 6 net-new practitioners per week, with interview participation capped at 2 slots per practitioner per week to protect delivery time.

New hires progressed through “I do, we do, you do” learning, then round-robin pairing with seasoned practitioners. Training combined cloud technologies with lean product development, user-centered design, pair programming, TDD, and CI/CD. Peer-led demos, engineering forums, and learning on live client projects continued after onboarding.

The organization stayed deliberately flat, with 11 to 13 practice and client leads reporting directly to Matt. Balanced teams combined product, engineering, and design. Experienced engineering “anchors” kept leads informed without becoming another management layer. Weekly allocation reviews matched practitioners to engagements and internal learning work.

Because managers did not sit beside practitioners daily, evaluations drew on evidence from recent pair partners, product managers, and anchors, rather than visibility to a boss. Matt ran feedback capture, promotion calibration, compensation allocation, and remediation decisions.

The organization reached 150+ practitioners, with near-zero attrition in its first 2 years and multiple hires promoted.

## Turn 2. Visitor: Tell me about his payments work

### A

🐾 Here’s Matt’s payments work, from payment engines to client integration.

At JP Morgan Chase, ACCESS Next Generation needed payment capabilities beyond Bottomline Technologies’ WebSeries platform. Regional systems had different data models, client configurations, and entitlement structures, while each payment type carried distinct formatting, validation, and compliance requirements.

As Technology Delivery Lead, Matt managed a 40+ blended onshore/offshore team across the US, Mumbai, and Bangalore. He designed and delivered ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and federal tax payment modules. That included NACHA file validation, integration with the bank’s foreign exchange engine, Message Authentication Code security, and payment-type-specific permissions, such as allowing wire approval without FX initiation. He also built sanctions screening and risk-based approval controls into payment flows.

Matt led payments data migration from the regional systems, preserving client configurations, transaction histories, and compliance traceability with zero disruption to live payment operations. ACCESS launched in April 2011, consolidating regional systems into a global corporate banking portal serving 135,000+ clients across 180+ countries.

At RBC, fragmented systems and manual processes were delaying cross-border and bulk payments. Matt designed workflows from initiation through settlement and reconciliation, working with the FX team on real-time currency conversion and transaction visibility. He also defined automated alerts, recurring payments, Interac e-Transfer workflows, and revised payment cutoff processes. The result was an implementation-ready blueprint; revised cutoff processes reduced wire transfer delays and improved SLA compliance.

At American Express, his work focused on adoption of the Virtual Payments Platform replacing a legacy mainframe system. Missing SDKs, fragmented documentation, and no sandbox made integration difficult. Matt led developer-portal improvements, cross-stack SDKs, use-case-driven documentation, and secure virtualized testing environments. His team used Lean Product and XP practices and validated the portal through a Client Onboarding Test Drive.

API consumption and trial usage increased within 3 months of relaunch, onboarding time fell, and the team delivered over 261 additional story points beyond its commitment.
