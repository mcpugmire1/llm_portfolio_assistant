### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt changed the day-to-day work.

At Fiserv, the 45-person team was working through 6-month waterfall release cycles, with requirements buried in 200-page business requirements documents organized by system modules. Developers were in India, testers in the Philippines, and designers in Ireland. Work status wasn’t visible, deadlines kept slipping, and stakeholders were threatening contract cancellation. PMO and compliance teams resisted agile, while some stakeholders lacked bandwidth for feedback every 2 weeks.

Matt replaced the single To Do/Done JIRA board with a multi-tiered Kanban/Scrum architecture. He tracked the 47 acceptance criteria as Epics and decomposed the requirements into persona-based user stories rather than system-module tasks. He established follow-the-sun synchronization: a 7 AM CST scrum-of-scrums for offshore-to-onshore handoffs and 11 PM CST syncs for the reverse handoff.

Instead of waiting until month 6 to discover branding, UI, or accessibility problems, Matt introduced bi-weekly sprint demos and negotiated dedicated review windows. He retained steering committee reporting while adding sprint-level visibility. For compliance, he used JIRA as the system of record, with audit trails, attached ADA scans, and developer comments instead of static Word documents. Offshore teams received coaching on empowerment over command-and-control, with JAWS/NVDA screen reader audits built into the Definition of Done.

The engineering workflow changed too: Matt moved source control from SVN to Git/GitHub, required senior-developer code reviews and unit tests, and introduced Hudson CI with SonarQube and JUnit quality gates. For accessibility, he defined development guidelines and led UI refactoring to semantic HTML.

Within 3 months, the team was working in 2-week sprints. JIRA provided real-time visibility to 50+ stakeholders across 3 time zones, and PMO and compliance accepted it as the system of record.
