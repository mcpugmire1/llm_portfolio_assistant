## Turn 1. Visitor: Tell me about his Norfolk Southern work.

### A

🐾 Here’s the story of Matt’s work at Norfolk Southern.

Matt led Revenue and Shipment Planning modernization as the Cloud Innovation Center’s first engagement. The railroad’s core scheduling, billing, and order-to-cash ran on a mainframe dating to the 1970s. Orders could fall out unnoticed until customers called, business rules lived in COBOL and assembly maintained by a shrinking workforce, and clerks manually handled waybill and rating fallout. The work also faced resistance from a client organization shaped around mainframe thinking and account leadership accustomed to waterfall, while the pandemic moved delivery remote.

Change data capture synchronized the platforms, and reconciliation checked that nothing was lost. The engagement also hit a quality crisis: defects rose despite near-complete test coverage. Matt took ownership, audited the code and tests, and found tests written after code against vague backlog stories. He brought in TDD experts, rebuilt stories with defined scenarios and a definition of done, and reinforced test-first work through rotating pairs and coaching. The client stopped finding defects the dashboard could not see.

Alongside delivery, Matt led a 2-day CIC Academy course, delivered twice monthly over 6 months. Pairing spread scarce legacy knowledge and helped client contributors learn Angular, Node, Spring Boot, and cloud-native concepts.

The MVP launched covering 95% of its targeted use cases, capturing real orders with clean data feeding the mainframe. EDI validation moved fallout detection from customer calls to proactive trading-partner notifications. The client team was co-creating in weekly iterations and gained the skills to support and manage the new application.

From June 2012 to June 2013, Matt worked as Senior Technology Architecture Manager on RBC’s Day2Day Corporate Banking modernization. The bank was evaluating Bottomline WebSeries as the payment hub engine while planning to replace RBC Express. Leadership needed to determine whether the proposed platform could meet business requirements before committing further investment.

Matt conducted an architecture assessment across Data, Application, Technology, and Business Architecture. The assessment found that WebSeries lacked the modularity required for RBC’s long-term digital operating model. That finding prevented further investment in a platform path that could not deliver and enabled the bank to redirect technical resources toward higher-priority initiatives.

Alongside that assessment, Matt established the PMO across RBC, Accenture, and Bottomline Technologies. He maintained the program roadmap, budget, staffing, dependencies, and reporting. Designated team contacts, daily summaries, and a task-tracking matrix eliminated missed program updates and reduced redundant effort.

When the previous data conversion lead left, Matt took over the workstream from scratch. He analyzed decades-old payment records, documented source-to-target transformation rules, planned mock conversion cycles, and established reconciliation checks for payment amounts and beneficiary details. He also managed onshore and offshore teams performing data scrubbing and validation.

For mobile readiness, Matt defined API integration points, cheque deposit requirements, and cross-browser and cross-device scalability needs. The deliverables included a modernization blueprint, an end-to-end conversion plan, and governance spanning 3 organizations. The architecture assessment’s heat maps and roadmap remained reference material for future modernization planning.
