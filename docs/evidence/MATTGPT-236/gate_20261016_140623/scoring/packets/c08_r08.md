## Turn 1. Visitor: Tell me about a time he failed.

### A

🐾 Here’s a time Matt failed.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization delivery for a Fortune 500 financial services client. The program was under delivery pressure, with offshore teams executing test cycles across development, QA, UAT, and production. IAM controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from prior phases, along with existing documentation.

Matt was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup, but he relied on that documentation rather than verifying it. He assumed the IAM configuration and environment boundaries were correct. He did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes, implement compensating controls such as data masking and email interception, or verify whether production customer names and email addresses had been scrubbed from downstream environments.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. They identified insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed, then worked collectively to implement corrective controls. The client nevertheless demanded that the tester be removed from the project: a systemic failure resulted in an individual consequence.

Matt’s stated lesson was that “assumptions are risks in disguise.” IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership became day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach, rather than inherited conditions he assumed were already correct.
