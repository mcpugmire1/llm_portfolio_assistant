### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt changed the team’s day-to-day work.

At Fiserv, the 45-person team was working through 6-month waterfall release cycles, with requirements buried in 200-page business requirements documents organized by system modules. Developers in India, testers in the Philippines, and designers in Ireland had no shared visibility into work status. Deadlines had slipped repeatedly, stakeholders were threatening contract cancellation, and PMO and compliance teams resisted agile. Banking stakeholders also lacked bandwidth for feedback every 2 weeks.

Matt decomposed the requirements into persona-based user stories instead of system modules. He replaced a single To Do/Done JIRA board with a multi-tiered Kanban/Scrum architecture, tracking the 47 acceptance criteria as Epics and moving stories through specific workflow columns. He established follow-the-sun handoffs: a 7 AM CST scrum-of-scrums and 11 PM CST synchronization calls.

He introduced bi-weekly demos so stakeholders could sign off on branding, UI, and ADA compliance before the final release. When reviewers could not sustain that cadence, he negotiated dedicated review windows. He maintained steering committee reporting while adding sprint-level visibility, rather than requiring decision makers to abandon their existing reporting immediately.

For PMO and compliance, Matt used JIRA audit trails, attached accessibility scans, and developer comments instead of static Word documents. He coached offshore teams on empowerment over command-and-control and trained developers on screen reader audits using JAWS/NVDA, making those audits part of the Definition of Done.

The team moved to 2-week sprints within 3 months. More than 50 stakeholders across 3 time zones gained real-time work visibility, and PMO and compliance accepted JIRA as the system of record. Sprint demos caught branding and accessibility issues early instead of at month 6.
