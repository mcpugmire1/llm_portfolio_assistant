## Turn 1. Visitor: Tell me about a time he failed.

### A

🐾 Here’s a time Matt failed.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization delivery for a financial services client. The program was under delivery pressure, with offshore teams executing test cycles across development, QA, UAT, and production environments. Identity and access management (IAM) controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from prior phases, along with existing documentation.

Matt was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup. Instead of verifying the configuration, he assumed the IAM controls and environment boundaries were already correct. He focused on delivery pressures, relied on inherited documentation, and did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes. He also did not verify whether production customer data, including names and email addresses, had been masked or scrubbed, or implement compensating controls such as email interception.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. He worked collectively with the team to understand what happened and implement corrective controls, rather than treating the tester’s action as the entire cause. The investigation identified insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed from downstream environments.

The client nevertheless demanded that the tester be removed from the project. Matt described that as “a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence.” Afterward, IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership became day-one responsibilities in his delivery approach.
