## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt scale engineering teams from 4 to 150+ people?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt built and ran the organization.

Matt scaled Accenture’s Atlanta and Tampa Cloud Innovation Centers to 150+ practitioners across engineering, architecture, product, and design. The documented CIC starting point was a 10-person pilot, rather than 4. The challenge was not just hiring: many new joiners lacked hands-on cloud-native or Agile experience, and the organization deliberately avoided layers of managers supervising daily work.

He built a hiring engine across internal bench searches, campus recruiting, contract-to-hire, and job requisitions. Engineers completed hands-on TDD pairing auditions; product managers and designers worked through live scoping exercises. Matt held the closing interview and made the final decision on every offer. During scaling pushes, the process produced 4 to 6 net-new practitioners per week, with interviewers capped at 2 interview slots per week so recruiting did not compromise delivery or burn people out.

New hires progressed through tailored enablement and round-robin pairing with seasoned practitioners. The learning model was “I do, we do, you do,” moving people from guided to independent delivery. Weekly peer-led demos, practice forums, mentoring, and learning within client projects continued after onboarding. Recruitment, hackathons, and job fairs at Georgia Tech also expanded the talent pipeline.

Matt kept the organization flat through 11 to 13 direct-report practice and client leads, with experienced engineering anchors inside balanced teams. Monday allocation reviews matched practitioners to engagements and learning tracks. Because managers did not sit beside practitioners daily, evaluations drew on evidence from recent pairing partners, product managers, and anchors, rather than visibility to a boss. Career expectations, promotion calibration, compensation, and remediation had explicit processes.

The organization reached 150+ practitioners with near-zero attrition in its first 2 years and multiple hires promoted. Across the centers, 10-person balanced teams delivered equivalent output to traditional 20-person teams, with 4X faster velocity and zero-defect deployments.

## Turn 2. Visitor: Tell me about his payments work

Matt served as Technology Delivery Lead, managing a 40+ person blended team across the US, Mumbai, and Bangalore. He designed and delivered bespoke ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and government tax payment modules. He also designed the centralized gateway for message transformation, protocol translation, and routing across 12 countries, replacing fragmented point-to-point integrations. Alongside that engineering, he led payments data migration from legacy regional systems, preserving client configurations, transaction histories, and compliance traceability without disruption to live payment operations. ACCESS launched in April 2011, serving clients across 180+ countries.

At American Express, the problem was adoption: clients faced missing SDKs, fragmented documentation, no sandbox, and testing dependencies when integrating with the new Virtual Payments Platform. Matt led work on a developer portal, cross-stack SDKs, use-case-driven documentation, and a secure sandbox with refreshed client data. Environment virtualization let developers test earlier without waiting on downstream systems; CI/CD kept documentation and APIs synchronized with backend changes.

API consumption and trial usage increased within 3 months of relaunch, and client onboarding time decreased. In the developer-portal work, his team also exceeded its delivery commitment by over 261 story points of customer-focused value.
