## Turn 3. Visitor: Give me an example.

### A

🐾 Here’s a specific example from Norfolk Southern.

During the Cloud Innovation Center’s first major engagement in 2020, Matt’s team was modernizing the railroad’s legacy Revenue and Shipment Planning platform. A few months in, just as the pandemic forced remote work, the client reported rising defects. The team’s dashboards showed near-complete test coverage. The concerns landed in front of both the client and Accenture account leadership, who were already accustomed to waterfall delivery.

Matt took ownership of the failure rather than defending the metrics. He ran a bottom-up audit of the codebase and test suite, then showed the gaps openly. An engineer had been building from his own interpretation instead of the backlog story and writing tests after the code. The backlog was also part of the problem: stories lacked defined scenarios and a definition of done.

Matt rebuilt the backlog with SMART, testable stories and brought in frontend and backend TDD experts from Columbus to work side by side with engineers. The team redid the code and test cases, then practiced through boot camps, coding katas, and an “I do, we do, you do” coaching progression.

He also changed the daily workflow so the practices would survive after the experts left. Pairs rotated daily, test cases came before implementation, and standups checked that sequence. Stories entered sprints only when testable and small enough for a one-week iteration.

The client stopped finding defects the dashboard could not see. Defect rates dropped, pairing and test-first remained the team’s default after the experts left, and the failure mode that caused the crisis did not recur.
