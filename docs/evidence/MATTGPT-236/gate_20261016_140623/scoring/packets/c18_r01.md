## Turn 1. Visitor: Tell me about his Norfolk Southern work.

### A

🐾 Here’s the story of Matt’s work at Norfolk Southern.

Matt led the Revenue and Shipment Planning modernization as a Director at Accenture, on the Cloud Innovation Center’s first engagement. The railroad’s core scheduling, billing, and order-to-cash ran on a mainframe dating to the 1970s. Orders could fall out unnoticed until customers called, business rules lived in COBOL and assembly maintained by a shrinking workforce, and clerks manually worked through waybill and rating problems. The task was to modernize without stopping the railroad, while moving a mainframe-oriented client and waterfall-oriented account leadership toward product delivery.

Matt led domain-driven design and event storming sessions to map business functions and dependencies. The mainframe stayed the system of record while APIs, microservices, and CockroachDB were built alongside it. Change data capture synchronized the platforms, and reconciliation checked that nothing was lost. Architecture work included the Boris Exercise, SNAP-E documentation, asynchronous messaging, and anti-corruption layers for legacy integration.

When the client grew restless for tangible progress, Matt brought revenue management into the innovation hub to co-create the Shipper Revenue System workspace. They worked from personas through high-fidelity prototypes in weekly build-measure-learn iterations. Delivery expanded to 3 balanced teams, with a transition to Monterrey beginning.

The work also hit a quality crisis: defects rose despite near-complete test coverage. Matt took ownership, audited the code and tests, and found tests written after code and backlog stories without clear scenarios. He brought in frontend and backend TDD experts, rebuilt testable stories, and introduced coding katas and hands-on coaching. A 2-day CIC Academy course ran twice monthly over 6 months. Pairing helped spread scarce domain knowledge and taught client contributors Angular, Node, Spring Boot, and cloud-native practices.

The MVP launched covering 95% of targeted use cases, capturing real orders with clean data feeding the mainframe. EDI validation detected fallout proactively and notified trading partners rather than waiting for customer complaints. Defect rates dropped, and client contributors gained the skills to support and manage the new application after Accenture rolled off.

## Turn 2. Visitor: Tell me about his RBC work.

### A

🐾 Here’s Matt’s work at RBC.

From June 2012 to June 2013, Matt worked as a Senior Technology Architecture Manager on corporate banking modernization. RBC was evaluating Bottomline WebSeries as the payment hub engine for its Day2Day Corporate Banking platform while planning to replace the aging RBC Express portal. Existing systems had performance and scalability limitations, lacked mobile capabilities, and offered no API integration with external financial applications. Leadership needed to determine whether the proposed platform could meet business requirements before committing further investment.

Matt conducted architecture assessments across Data, Application, Technology, and Business Architecture. He reviewed code, interviewed stakeholders, analyzed documentation, and created architectural heat maps comparing requirements with WebSeries capabilities. Working with the enterprise architecture team, he developed logical and system models and a transparent cost-to-fix versus benefit comparison. The assessment found that WebSeries lacked the modularity required for RBC’s long-term digital operating model. RBC redirected technical resources toward higher-priority initiatives rather than investing further in that platform path.

Matt also established the PMO across 3 organizations: RBC, Accenture, and Bottomline Technologies. He maintained the program roadmap, budget, schedule, dependencies, and reporting, with designated team contacts, daily summaries, and a task-tracking matrix. He owned engagement margin and managed staffing from early specialist architecture work toward offshore and nearshore delivery as work became repeatable.

Two additional assessments addressed specific customer constraints. Transaction and chargeback history stopped at 180 days, while audit teams needed up to 7 years and waited days or weeks for archived records. Matt delivered requirements and solution designs for extended historical access, scheduled reporting, and scalable exports. For mobile banking, he defined API integration points, cheque deposit requirements, and cross-browser and cross-device scalability needs.

The delivered outputs included the PMO framework, architecture assessment, mobile modernization blueprint, and reporting solution design. The stories describe assessment and planning outcomes, not a completed platform rollout.
