### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt changed the day-to-day work.

At Fiserv, the 45-person team was working through 6-month waterfall release cycles, with requirements buried in a 200-page business requirements document organized by system modules. Developers in India, testers in the Philippines, and designers in Ireland had no shared visibility into work status. Multiple deadlines had been missed, stakeholders were threatening contract cancellation, and PMO and compliance teams resisted moving to agile.

Matt changed how the team defined and tracked work. He decomposed the requirements into persona-based user stories rather than system modules. He replaced a single To Do/Done JIRA board with a multi-tiered Kanban/Scrum architecture, tracking the 47 acceptance criteria as Epics and moving stories through specific workflow columns.

To connect the distributed team, he established follow-the-sun synchronization: a 7 AM CST scrum-of-scrums for offshore-to-onshore handoffs and 11 PM CST syncs for the reverse. He introduced biweekly sprint demos so stakeholders could sign off on branding, UI, and ADA compliance before problems accumulated. When stakeholders lacked time for regular feedback, he negotiated dedicated review windows.

He kept existing steering committee reporting while adding sprint-level visibility. Instead of relying on static Word documents for compliance, he used JIRA audit trails, attached ADA scans, and developer comments. He also coached offshore teams on empowerment over command-and-control and made JAWS/NVDA screen reader audits part of the Definition of Done.

The team reached a 2-week sprint cadence within 3 months. JIRA gave 50+ stakeholders across 3 time zones real-time visibility, and PMO and compliance accepted it as the system of record. Sprint demos caught branding and accessibility issues early rather than at month 6.
