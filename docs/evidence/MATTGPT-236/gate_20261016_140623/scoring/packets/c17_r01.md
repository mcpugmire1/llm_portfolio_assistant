### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt changed the team’s day-to-day work.

At Fiserv, the 45-person team was working through 6-month waterfall release cycles with no visibility into work status. Requirements lived in 200-page business requirements documents organized by system modules. Developers in India, testers in the Philippines, and designers in Ireland were working across time zones while deadlines slipped. PMO and compliance stakeholders resisted agile, and business stakeholders lacked bandwidth for feedback every 2 weeks.

Matt changed how work was defined and tracked. He decomposed the requirements into persona-based user stories rather than system-module tasks. He replaced a single To Do/Done JIRA board with a multi-tiered Kanban/Scrum structure, tracking the 47 acceptance criteria as Epics and moving stories through specific workflow columns.

For daily coordination, he established 7 AM CST offshore-to-onshore handoffs and 11 PM CST onshore-to-offshore syncs. He introduced bi-weekly sprint demos so Fiserv stakeholders could review branding, UI, and ADA compliance before the end of a 6-month cycle. When reviewers lacked time, he negotiated dedicated review windows to keep work from stalling.

He maintained steering committee reporting while adding sprint-level visibility. To address compliance resistance, he used JIRA as the system of record, with audit trails, attached ADA scans, and developer comments instead of static Word documents. Offshore teams received coaching on empowerment over command-and-control, with JAWS/NVDA screen reader audits built into the Definition of Done.

Within 3 months, the team adopted a 2-week sprint cadence. JIRA gave 50+ stakeholders across 3 time zones real-time visibility, and PMO and compliance accepted it for audit requirements. Teams caught branding and accessibility issues during sprint demos rather than discovering them at month 6.
