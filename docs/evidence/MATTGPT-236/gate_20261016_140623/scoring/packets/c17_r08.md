### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt changed the delivery cadence.

At Fiserv, the 45-person team was working through 6-month release cycles with requirements buried in a 200-page BRD organized by system modules. Developers in India, testers in the Philippines, and designers in Ireland had no visibility into work status. Deadlines kept slipping, stakeholders were threatening contract cancellation, and PMO and compliance teams resisted the move to agile.

Matt decomposed the BRD into persona-based user stories instead of system-module requirements. He replaced a single To Do/Done JIRA board with a multi-tiered Kanban/Scrum structure, tracking the 47 acceptance criteria as Epics and moving stories through specific workflow columns. He established follow-the-sun synchronization: 7 AM CST scrum-of-scrums for offshore-to-onshore handoffs and 11 PM CST syncs for the reverse handoff.

He introduced biweekly sprint demos so stakeholders could sign off on branding, UI, and ADA compliance before the end of the release cycle. When reviewers lacked bandwidth, he negotiated dedicated review windows. He kept existing steering committee reporting while adding sprint-level visibility.

For compliance, Matt used JIRA as the system of record, with audit trails, attached ADA scans, and developer comments rather than static Word documents. He also trained offshore teams on screen reader audits using JAWS/NVDA and included those checks in the Definition of Done. Alongside those changes, he introduced mandatory senior-developer code reviews, unit tests, and automated build quality gates.

The team reached a 2-week sprint cadence within 3 months. JIRA provided real-time visibility to 50+ stakeholders across 3 time zones and satisfied PMO and compliance audit requirements. The broader portal recovery delivered all 47 acceptance criteria with zero critical defects at launch, and teams independently maintained accessibility compliance on subsequent releases.
