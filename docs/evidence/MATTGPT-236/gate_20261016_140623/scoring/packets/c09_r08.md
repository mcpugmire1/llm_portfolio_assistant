## Turn 3. Visitor: Give me an example.

### A

🐾 Here’s an example from Norfolk Southern.

During the Cloud Innovation Center’s first major engagement in 2020, Matt’s team was modernizing the railroad’s Revenue and Shipment Planning platform. A few months in, the client reported rising defects, even though the dashboards showed near-complete test coverage. The concerns landed in front of both the client and Accenture account leadership, just as the pandemic forced the team into remote work.

Matt took ownership of the failure rather than defending the metrics. He ran a bottom-up audit of the codebase and test suite and shared the gaps openly. An engineer had been building from his own interpretation instead of the backlog story, then writing tests after the code. The backlog also contained vague stories without defined scenarios or a definition of done.

Matt addressed both the work and the coaching. He rebuilt the backlog with SMART, testable stories and brought frontend and backend TDD experts from Columbus to work alongside the engineers, redoing code and tests properly. Coaching followed an “I do, we do, you do” progression through boot camps, coding katas, practice learning sessions, and pairing.

He then reinforced the practices after the experts left. Pairs rotated daily, new engineers worked with seasoned practitioners, and building test cases became a visible step before engineering the solution, checked at standup rather than inferred from a dashboard.

The client stopped finding defects the dashboard could not see. Defect rates dropped, client confidence was restored through the open audit and visible improvement, and the failure mode did not recur after the outside experts went home.
