## Turn 3. Visitor: Give me an example.

### A

🐾 Here’s an example from Norfolk Southern.

During the Cloud Innovation Center’s first major engagement, the team was modernizing the railroad’s legacy Revenue and Shipment Planning platform. A few months in, the client reported rising defects, even though the dashboards showed near-complete test coverage. The concerns landed in front of both the client and Accenture account leadership, just as the pandemic forced the team into remote work.

Matt took ownership of the failure rather than defending the metrics. He ran a bottom-up audit of the codebase and test suite and showed the gaps openly. An engineer had been building from his own interpretation rather than the backlog story, then writing tests after the code. The backlog was also part of the problem: stories lacked defined scenarios and a definition of done.

Instead of simply telling engineers to follow the process, Matt rebuilt the backlog with SMART, testable stories and brought in frontend and backend test-driven development experts from Columbus to work alongside the team. They redid the code and tests, then reinforced the practices through boot camps, coding katas, and an “I do, we do, you do” progression.

He followed through after the experts left. Engineers rotated pairs daily, new engineers worked with seasoned practitioners, and writing test cases became an explicit step before building the solution, checked at standup. During remote work, pairs traded drivers frequently.

Defect rates dropped, and the client stopped finding defects the dashboard could not see. Pairing and test-first remained the team’s default after the experts went home, and the failure mode that caused the crisis did not recur.
