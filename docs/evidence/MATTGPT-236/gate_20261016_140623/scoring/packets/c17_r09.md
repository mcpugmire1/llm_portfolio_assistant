### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt changed the team’s day-to-day work.

At Fiserv, the 45-person team was working through 6-month waterfall release cycles with no visibility into work status. Requirements lived in 200-page business requirements documents organized by system modules. Developers were in India, testers in the Philippines, and designers in Ireland. Deadlines kept slipping, stakeholders were threatening contract cancellation, and PMO and compliance resisted moving to agile.

Matt replaced the single To Do/Done JIRA board with a multi-tiered Kanban/Scrum architecture. He tracked the 47 acceptance criteria as Epics and decomposed requirements into persona-based user stories instead of system modules. He established 7 AM CST scrum-of-scrums for offshore-to-onshore handoffs and 11 PM CST syncs for the reverse handoff.

Instead of waiting until month 6 to discover branding, UI, or accessibility problems, Matt introduced biweekly sprint demos for Fiserv stakeholders. He negotiated dedicated review windows when stakeholders lacked bandwidth for feedback. He maintained steering committee reporting while adding sprint-level visibility.

For compliance, Matt used JIRA as the system of record, with audit trails, attached accessibility scans, and developer comments rather than static Word documents. He coached offshore teams on empowerment over command-and-control and built screen reader audits into the Definition of Done. Alongside that change, he introduced mandatory senior-developer code reviews, unit tests, and automated build quality gates. Accessibility training and development guidelines enabled teams to maintain compliance on subsequent releases without external accessibility expertise.

The team moved to 2-week sprints within 3 months. JIRA provided real-time visibility to 50+ stakeholders across 3 time zones, and PMO and compliance accepted it for audit requirements. Sprint cadence was not production deployment frequency: production releases improved from less than monthly to weekly-to-monthly cycles. The recovered program met all 47 acceptance criteria with zero critical defects at launch.
