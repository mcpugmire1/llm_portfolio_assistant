## Turn 1. Visitor: Tell me about his Norfolk Southern work.

### A

Matt worked as a Director on Norfolk Southern’s Revenue and Shipment Planning modernization from November 2019 to September 2023, the Cloud Innovation Center’s first engagement. The task was to modernize the revenue engine without stopping the railroad.

The mainframe dated to the 1970s. Orders could fall out unnoticed until customers called, business rules lived in COBOL and assembly maintained by a shrinking workforce, and clerks manually handled waybill and rating fallout. The client organization was accustomed to mainframe thinking, account leadership to waterfall delivery, and Matt’s team was learning new practices as the pandemic forced remote work.

Matt led domain-driven design and event-storming workshops with cross-workstream representation to map business functions, dependencies, and data flows. He used the Boris Exercise and SNAP-E documentation to define APIs, boundaries, and integration risks. The mainframe remained the system of record while APIs, microservices, and CockroachDB rose alongside it, connected through change data capture and reconciliation. When the client grew restless for tangible progress, he brought revenue management into the innovation hub to co-create the Shipper Revenue System workspace through personas, prototypes, and weekly build-measure-learn iterations.

The work also hit a quality crisis: defects were rising despite near-complete test coverage. Matt took ownership in front of the client and account leadership, audited the code and tests, and exposed vague backlog stories and tests written after code. He brought in frontend and backend TDD experts, rebuilt testable stories, and reinforced test-first work through rotating pairs, coding katas, and standup checks. A 2-day CIC Academy course, delivered twice monthly over 6 months, supported the changes. Pairing also spread scarce domain knowledge and helped client contributors learn Angular, Node, Spring Boot, and cloud-native concepts.

The MVP launched covering 95% of its targeted use cases, capturing real orders with clean data feeding the mainframe. EDI validation identified fallout proactively. The client stopped finding defects the dashboard could not see, practices held after the experts left, and client contributors gained the skills to support and manage the new application.

For cross-border payments, he designed workflows from initiation through settlement and reconciliation, incorporating real-time FX, automated alerts, recurring payments, and batch processing.

Another constraint was the platform’s 180-day transaction-history limit. Audit teams needed up to 7 years of records, while treasury teams were stitching together monthly CSVs. Matt delivered requirements and solution designs for extended historical access, scheduled reports, and scalable exports. These were modernization blueprints and implementation designs, not a claim that he shipped a replacement banking platform.
