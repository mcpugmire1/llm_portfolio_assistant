### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt changed the day-to-day work.

At Fiserv, the 45-person team was working through 6-month release cycles, with requirements buried in a 200-page BRD organized by system modules. Developers in India, testers in the Philippines, and designers in Ireland had no shared visibility into work status. Multiple deadlines had slipped, stakeholders were threatening contract cancellation, and PMO and compliance teams resisted moving to agile.

Matt changed how the work was defined and tracked. He decomposed the BRD into persona-based user stories and replaced a single To Do/Done JIRA board with a multi-tiered Kanban/Scrum architecture. The program’s 47 acceptance criteria became Epics, with stories moving through specific workflow columns. He established follow-the-sun synchronization: 7 AM CST scrum-of-scrums for offshore-to-onshore handoffs and 11 PM CST syncs for the return handoff.

Instead of waiting until month 6 to discover branding, UI, or ADA compliance problems, Matt introduced bi-weekly sprint demos. When stakeholders lacked bandwidth, he negotiated dedicated review windows. He maintained steering committee reporting while adding sprint-level visibility, and addressed compliance resistance with JIRA audit trails, attached ADA scans, and developer comments rather than static Word documents.

The engineering changes supported that cadence: mandatory senior-developer code reviews, unit tests before code completion, and Hudson continuous integration with SonarQube and JUnit quality gates. Matt also trained teams on accessibility testing and built screen reader audits into the Definition of Done.

Within 3 months, the team moved to 2-week sprints. JIRA gave 50+ stakeholders across 3 time zones real-time visibility and satisfied PMO and compliance audit requirements. Separately, production deployment frequency improved from less than monthly to weekly-to-monthly cycles, rather than every sprint automatically becoming a production release.
