## Turn 1. Visitor: Tell me about a time he failed.

### A

🐾 Here’s a failure Matt has described directly.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization for a Fortune 500 financial services client. The program had development, QA, UAT, and production environments, with offshore teams running test cycles under delivery pressure. Access controls and environment configurations had been inherited from earlier phases, along with documentation that Matt relied on without confirming its accuracy.

His responsibility was to ensure a safe environment setup. Instead, he assumed the identity and access management (IAM) controls and environment boundaries were correct. He did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes, verify that production customer data had been scrubbed, or implement compensating controls such as data masking and email interception.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. They identified insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed from downstream environments. He worked with the team to implement corrective controls.

The client demanded that the tester be removed from the project. Matt described that as “a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence,” and said these were assumptions he should have verified rather than inherited.

Afterward, Matt made IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership “day-one leadership responsibilities” in his delivery approach.
