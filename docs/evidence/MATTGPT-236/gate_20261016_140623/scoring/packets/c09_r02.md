## Turn 3. Visitor: Give me an example.

### A

🐾 Here’s a specific example from Norfolk Southern.

In 2020, Matt’s team was modernizing the railroad’s legacy Revenue and Shipment Planning platform when the client reported rising defects. The dashboards showed near-complete test coverage. The concerns landed in front of both the client and Accenture account leadership, just as the pandemic forced the team into remote work.

Matt took ownership of the failure rather than defending the metrics. He ran a bottom-up audit of the codebase and test suite and shared the gaps openly. An engineer had been building from his own interpretation rather than the backlog story, then writing tests after the code. The backlog was also part of the problem: stories lacked defined scenarios and a definition of done, leaving engineers without clear requirements to test against.

Instead of simply telling the team to follow the process, Matt rebuilt the backlog with SMART, testable stories and brought in frontend and backend TDD experts from Columbus to work alongside the engineers. They redid the code and test cases, using boot camps, coding katas, and an “I do, we do, you do” coaching progression.

He then reinforced the practices after the experts left. New engineers paired with seasoned practitioners, pairs rotated daily to spread knowledge, and test cases became a visible step before implementation. During remote work, pairs traded drivers frequently to reduce fatigue and break mental blocks.

The client stopped finding defects the dashboard could not see. The original failure mode did not recur, and knowledge stopped bottlenecking on individual engineers. Pairing and test-first remained the team’s default after the outside experts went home.
