## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt scale engineering teams from 4 to 150+ people?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt built and ran that organization.

The CIC stories describe growth from a 10-person pilot to 150+ practitioners, rather than a starting team of 4. At Accenture’s Atlanta and Tampa Cloud Innovation Centers, the challenge was not just hiring: many new joiners lacked hands-on cloud-native or Agile experience. Matt also needed to run hiring, development, evaluation, and promotions without adding layers of management.

He built a hiring engine across 4 channels: internal bench searches, campus recruiting, contract-to-hire, and job requisitions. Engineers completed hands-on TDD pairing auditions; product managers and designers worked through live scoping exercises. Interviewers recorded independent votes, and a failed pairing audition ended the candidacy. Matt held the closing interview and made the final decision on every offer. During scaling pushes, the process produced 4 to 6 net-new practitioners per week, with interview participation capped at 2 slots per practitioner per week to protect delivery.

He recruited for learning and collaborative mindsets, then used structured “I do, we do, you do” enablement and round-robin pairing with experienced practitioners. Training covered lean product management, user-centered design, pair programming, TDD, and CI/CD. Weekly tech talks, peer-led demos, mentoring, and live client work continued the learning after onboarding.

Instead of building a management hierarchy, Matt ran a deliberately flat, 2-layer organization through 11 to 13 practice and client leads. Balanced teams held execution power. Experienced team anchors kept leads informed without becoming another management layer. Monday allocation reviews moved practitioners between client engagements, training, and accelerator development. Because managers did not sit on daily teams, evaluations drew on evidence from recent pair partners, product managers, and anchors rather than visibility to a boss.

The organization reached 150+ practitioners, with near-zero attrition in the first 2 years and multiple hires promoted. Across CIC delivery, 10-person balanced teams delivered equivalent output to traditional 20-person teams, with 4X faster velocity and zero-defect deployments.

## Turn 2. Visitor: Tell me about his payments work

### A

🐾 Matt’s payments work spans global payment processing and virtual payments platform adoption.

At JP Morgan Chase, regional payment systems had different formats, protocols, client configurations, and compliance records. The bank was building ACCESS Next Generation on Bottomline Technologies’ WebSeries platform, but its requirements went well beyond the out-of-box offering. Matt served as Technology Delivery Lead, managing a 40+ blended team across Accenture and bank resources in Mumbai, Bangalore, and the US.

He designed and delivered ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and federal tax payment modules, alongside payment-type-specific entitlements and Message Authentication Code security controls. He also built integrations for transaction monitoring, risk-based approval routing, and sanctions screening. For the global gateway, he defined message transformation, protocol translation, and routing across 12 countries, connecting ACCESS to clearing networks and internal compliance, settlement, and reporting systems.

Matt defined the onshore-offshore engagement model and spent 70 to 80% of his time embedded with Bottomline’s team in Portsmouth, coordinating where native platform functionality ended and bank-specific engineering began. He also owned production incident response, bringing together bank, offshore, and vendor teams for root-cause analysis and daily leadership calls. He led payments data migration with compliance teams, preserving client configurations, entitlements, and transaction histories without disruption to live payment operations. ACCESS launched in April 2011, serving 135,000+ clients across 180+ countries.

At American Express, the problem was adoption: clients lacked SDKs, usable documentation, and a sandbox for the Virtual Payments Platform. Matt worked with business and technical stakeholders through Discovery and Framing workshops, introduced environment virtualization, and helped create cross-stack SDKs, use-case-driven documentation, and a secure sandbox. His team used Lean Product and XP practices and validated the portal through a Client Onboarding Test Drive.

The work reduced onboarding time and integration complexity, increased API consumption and trial usage within 3 months, and delivered over 261 additional story points beyond the team’s commitment.
