## Turn 1. Visitor: Tell me about his Norfolk Southern work.

### A

🐾 Here’s the story behind Matt’s Norfolk Southern work.

Matt led the Cloud Innovation Center’s first engagement: modernizing the railroad’s Revenue and Shipment Planning platform without stopping live operations. Its mainframe dated to the 1970s. Orders could fall out unnoticed until customers called, business rules lived in COBOL and assembly maintained by a shrinking workforce, and clerks manually worked through waybill and rating problems on green-screen consoles. The client organization was accustomed to mainframe thinking, account leadership to waterfall delivery, and Matt’s team was learning a new working model as the pandemic forced remote work.

Matt led domain-driven design and event storming sessions to map business functions and dependencies. Rather than attempt a wholesale replacement, his team kept the mainframe as the system of record while building APIs, microservices, and CockroachDB alongside it. Change data capture synchronized the platforms; reconciliation checked that nothing was lost. API gateways, anti-corruption layers, Docker, Kubernetes, and automated CI/CD supported incremental replacement on live production. The architecture also enabled real-time revenue tracking and shipment adjustments instead of batch reporting.

When the client grew restless for tangible progress, Matt brought its revenue management team into the innovation hub to co-create the Shipper Revenue System workspace. Weekly build-measure-learn iterations took designs from personas to high-fidelity prototypes. Billing clerks and shippers could audit malformed waybill data before it caused downstream pricing errors.

The engagement also hit a quality crisis: defects were rising despite near-complete test coverage. Matt took ownership, audited the code and tests, and found tests written after code and stories without clear scenarios. He rebuilt the backlog and brought in TDD experts for hands-on coaching. A 2-day CIC Academy course, delivered twice a month over 6 months, helped client contributors learn cloud-native practices and share scarce domain knowledge through pairing.

The MVP launched covering 95% of targeted use cases. EDI validation shifted fallout detection from customer complaints to proactive trading-partner notifications. The client stopped finding defects the dashboard could not see, and its team was co-creating in weekly iterations.

## Turn 2. Visitor: Tell me about his RBC work.

### A

🐾 Here’s what Matt worked on at RBC.

From June 2012 to June 2013, Matt served as Senior Technology Architecture Manager on corporate banking modernization. RBC’s existing systems had performance and scalability limitations, lacked mobile capabilities and API integration with external financial applications, and offered limited cross-device access. The bank was evaluating Bottomline WebSeries as the payment hub engine for its Day2Day Corporate Banking platform while planning to replace the aging RBC Express portal. Leadership needed to know whether the proposed platform could meet business requirements before committing further investment.

Matt conducted code reviews, stakeholder interviews, and documentation analysis. He assessed Data, Application, Technology, and Business Architecture, then created architectural heat maps showing gaps between what RBC needed and what WebSeries could deliver. Working with the bank’s enterprise architecture team, he developed target-system models and a cost-to-fix versus benefit comparison. The assessment found that WebSeries lacked the modularity required for the bank’s long-term digital operating model.

Alongside that assessment, Matt established the PMO: the project management plan, staffing responsibilities, budget, schedule, reporting, and risk and dependency management. For contributors across RBC, Accenture, and Bottomline Technologies, he introduced designated points of contact, real-time updates, daily summaries, and a task-tracking matrix. Those changes eliminated missed program updates, reduced redundant effort, and clarified resource assignments.

Matt also assessed mobile readiness, defining API integration points, cheque deposit requirements, and cross-browser and cross-device scalability needs. He delivered a modernization blueprint, rather than a launched mobile product.

The architecture assessment prevented investment in a platform path that could not deliver the requirements and enabled RBC to redirect technical resources toward higher-priority initiatives. Its heat maps and roadmap remained reference material for future modernization planning.
