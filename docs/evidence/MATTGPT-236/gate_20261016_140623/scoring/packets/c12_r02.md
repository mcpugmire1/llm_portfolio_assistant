## Turn 1. Visitor: Compare his Fiserv and JP Morgan ACCESS work.

### A

🐾 Here’s how the two engagements compare.

Both involved Execution & Delivery, but the starting problems differed. At Fiserv, Matt inherited an \$8.5M white-label card portal with missed deadlines, poor quality, and stakeholders threatening contract cancellation. At JP Morgan Chase, ACCESS required custom payment capabilities beyond Bottomline Technologies’ WebSeries platform, while consolidating regional systems and meeting different jurisdictions’ compliance requirements.

At Fiserv, Matt served as Development Manager for a 45-person globally dispersed team and coordinated 3 external vendors. Recovery meant changing both delivery practices and engineering controls: he replaced 200-page requirements documents with persona-based user stories, introduced 2-week sprints and demonstrations, and used JIRA audit trails to address PMO and compliance resistance. He also replaced SVN with Git, mandated senior-developer code reviews and unit tests, and introduced Maven, Artifactory, Hudson, SonarQube, and JUnit. The portal integrated Java EE/Spring with legacy mainframe processing through an SOA integration layer.

For JP Morgan Chase ACCESS, Matt was Technology Delivery Lead for a 40+ blended team, spending 70 to 80% of his time embedded with Bottomline in Portsmouth. His work centered on purpose-built ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and tax-payment modules, MAC payment verification, payment-specific permissions, SSO, and global reporting. He redesigned entitlement administration with role-based profiles and bulk changes, led payments data migration, and translated regional regulatory requirements into data-visibility and access controls. Regional launches followed regulatory readiness rather than a single global deployment.

The outcomes measured different things. Fiserv launched with 47/47 acceptance criteria and zero critical defects, finished 3% under budget (\$255K savings), avoided \$500K in penalties, and secured a \$3M extension. The portal processed \$45M in its first month at 99.97% uptime. ACCESS launched in April 2011, serving 135,000+ clients across 180+ countries. Entitlement administration fell from days to minutes, migration caused no disruption to live payments, and regional launches proceeded without regulatory blockers.
