## Turn 1. Visitor: Tell me about a time he failed.

### A

🐾 Here’s a time Matt failed.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization delivery for a financial services client. The program was under delivery pressure, with offshore teams executing test cycles across development, QA, UAT, and production. IAM controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from prior phases, along with existing documentation.

Matt was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup, but he relied on that documentation rather than verifying the controls. He focused on delivery pressures instead of validating access patterns. He did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes, implement compensating controls such as data masking and email interception, or verify that production customer names and email addresses had been scrubbed from downstream environments.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients. The root cause was insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. He worked with the team to understand what happened and implement corrective controls. The client nevertheless demanded that the tester be removed from the project, a systemic failure with an individual consequence.

Afterward, Matt made IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership day-one responsibilities in his delivery approach. His description of the failure is direct: these were assumptions he should have verified rather than inherited.
