## Turn 3. Visitor: Give me an example.

### A

🐾 Here’s an example from Norfolk Southern.

In early 2020, Matt’s Cloud Innovation Center team was modernizing the client’s legacy Revenue and Shipment Planning platform. A couple of months in, the client reported rising defects, even though the team’s dashboards showed near-complete test coverage. The concerns landed in front of both the client and Accenture account leadership, just as the pandemic forced the team into remote work.

Matt took ownership rather than defending the metrics. He ran a bottom-up audit of the codebase and test suite and showed the gaps openly. An engineer had been building from his own interpretation rather than the backlog story, then writing tests after the code. The backlog was also part of the problem: stories lacked defined scenarios and a definition of done, leaving no clear way to test them.

Matt rebuilt the backlog with SMART, testable stories and brought in frontend and backend TDD experts from Columbus to work alongside the engineers. Instead of issuing a process mandate, he used boot camps, coding katas, practice sessions, and an “I do, we do, you do” coaching progression.

After the immediate correction, he reinforced the daily workflow. Engineers rotated pairs, built test cases before engineering the solution, and checked that sequence at standup. New engineers paired with seasoned practitioners so the practices would not depend on visiting experts or Matt’s direct oversight.

Defect rates dropped, and the client stopped finding defects the dashboard could not see. Pairing and test-first remained the team’s default after the experts left, and the failure mode that caused the crisis did not recur.
