## Turn 1. Visitor: Tell me about a time he failed.

### A

🐾 Here’s a time Matt’s assumptions led to a real incident.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization delivery for a financial services client. The program was under delivery pressure, with offshore teams executing test cycles across development, QA, UAT, and production. IAM controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from earlier phases, along with existing documentation.

Matt was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup. Instead of verifying those controls, he assumed they were correct. He focused on delivery pressures, relied on inherited documentation, and did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes. He also did not verify whether production customer names and email addresses had been masked or scrubbed, or implement compensating controls such as email interception.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. They identified insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed from downstream environments.

The client demanded that the tester be removed from the project. Matt described it as “a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence,” and said these were assumptions he should have verified rather than inherited.

Afterward, he made IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach.
