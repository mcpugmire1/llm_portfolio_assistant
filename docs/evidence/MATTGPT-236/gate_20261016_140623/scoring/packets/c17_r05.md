### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt changed the day-to-day work.

At Fiserv, the 45-person team was working through 200-page business requirements documents organized by system modules, with 6-month release cycles and no visibility into work status. Developers were in India, testers in the Philippines, and designers in Ireland. Multiple deadlines had been missed, stakeholders were threatening contract cancellation, and PMO and compliance teams resisted the move to agile.

Matt replaced the single To Do/Done JIRA board with a multi-tiered Kanban/Scrum architecture. He tracked the 47 acceptance criteria as Epics and decomposed requirements into persona-based user stories instead of system modules. He established follow-the-sun synchronization: 7 AM CST scrum-of-scrums for offshore-to-onshore handoffs and 11 PM CST syncs for the reverse handoff.

Instead of waiting until month 6 to discover branding, UI, or ADA compliance problems, Matt introduced bi-weekly sprint demos. When stakeholders lacked bandwidth for that feedback cadence, he negotiated dedicated review windows. He kept existing steering committee reporting while adding sprint-level visibility.

The compliance change was specific: Matt used JIRA as the system of record, with audit trails, attached ADA scans, and developer comments instead of static Word documents. He coached offshore teams on empowerment over command-and-control and built JAWS/NVDA screen reader audits into the Definition of Done. Alongside that change, he introduced mandatory senior-developer code reviews, unit tests, and Hudson continuous integration with SonarQube and JUnit quality gates.

The team reached a 2-week sprint cadence within 3 months. JIRA provided real-time visibility to 50+ stakeholders across 3 time zones, and PMO and compliance accepted it for audit requirements. Production deployment frequency separately increased from less than monthly to weekly-to-monthly cycles.
