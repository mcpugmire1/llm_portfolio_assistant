# Question q16, run 8

Labels in this packet: A, B.

## Visitor: Tell me about a time Matt failed

### A

🐾 Here’s a time Matt took accountability for a failure.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization delivery for a financial services client. The program had development, QA, UAT, and production environments, with offshore teams executing test cycles under delivery pressure. IAM controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from prior phases, along with documentation.

Matt was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup. Instead of verifying those controls, he assumed they were correct. He relied on inherited documentation, did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes, and did not verify whether production customer names and email addresses had been masked or scrubbed. Compensating controls, such as data masking and email interception, were not in place.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients. The root causes were insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed from downstream environments.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. He worked with the team to understand what happened and implement corrective controls. The client nevertheless demanded that the tester be removed from the project, a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence.

Matt’s stated lesson was that “assumptions are risks in disguise.” IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership became day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach.
