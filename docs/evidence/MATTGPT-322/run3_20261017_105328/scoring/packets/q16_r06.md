# Question q16, run 6

Labels in this packet: A, B.

## Visitor: Tell me about a time Matt failed

### A

🐾 Here’s a time Matt failed.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization delivery for a Fortune 500 financial services client. The program was under delivery pressure, with offshore teams executing test cycles across development, QA, UAT, and production environments. IAM controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from prior phases.

Matt was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup, but he relied on assumptions rather than verifying those controls. He focused on delivery pressures, trusted inherited documentation without confirming its accuracy, and did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes. He also did not verify whether production customer names and email addresses had been masked or scrubbed in downstream environments. Data masking and email interception were not in place as compensating controls.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. They identified insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed, then worked collectively to implement corrective controls. The client nevertheless demanded that the tester be removed from the project. Matt described that as “a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence.”

Afterward, Matt made IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership day-one responsibilities in his delivery approach. His stated lesson was that “assumptions are risks in disguise.”
