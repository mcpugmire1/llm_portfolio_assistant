# Question q16, run 4

Labels in this packet: A, B.

## Visitor: Tell me about a time Matt failed

### A

🐾 Here’s the failure Matt describes in “What I Learned About Assumptions.”

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization delivery for a financial services client. The program was under delivery pressure, with offshore teams running test cycles across development, QA, UAT, and production environments. IAM controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from earlier phases, along with documentation.

Matt was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup. Instead of verifying those controls, he assumed they were correct. He focused on delivery pressures, relied on inherited documentation, and did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes. He also did not verify whether production customer names and email addresses had been masked or scrubbed.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and worked collectively with the team through root cause analysis and remediation. They identified insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed from downstream environments. He led the team in understanding what happened and implementing corrective controls rather than treating the tester’s action as the entire explanation.

The client nevertheless demanded that the tester be removed from the project. Matt describes that as “a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence.”

Afterward, Matt made IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach. His stated lesson was that “assumptions are risks in disguise,” and that leaders own the environment their teams operate in.
