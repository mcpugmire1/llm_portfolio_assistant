## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. He describes engineering teams he inherited as “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” His focus is on removing those barriers so engineers, designers, and product teams can do the best work of their careers.

He makes intent explicit, pushes decisions to the people closest to the work, and uses transparency, direct feedback, and open communication. He coaches teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks. His non-negotiables include psychological safety, integrity and respect, and a focus on outcomes, not optics. He also describes choosing environments where intensity is balanced with respect for people.

At the Accenture Cloud Innovation Center, that included a “permit to fail” learning environment. Teams had avoided risks because they feared blame. Matt organized non-punitive post-mortems and supported lean, low-stakes experiments. Teams applied lessons to subsequent sprints, preventing repeat failure patterns. In product incubation work, he set expectations that early prototypes and user tests would likely fail, then used those results to shape product direction week by week rather than commit engineering effort to untested assumptions.

He also built balanced teams with engineers, product managers, and UX architects sharing accountability. Pair programming, collaborative coding, and retrospectives replaced role silos and reduced handoffs. Teams gained room to voice feedback and change their workflows autonomously.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams accelerated delivery 4x after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. The balanced-team work also reduced project delays, improved retention, and achieved zero-defect production code.

### B

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. He describes many teams he inherited as “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” His focus is on removing those barriers: making intent explicit, building psychological safety so people can speak honestly, and pushing decisions to the people closest to the work.

At Accenture’s Cloud Innovation Center, he established a “permit to fail” learning environment. Teams had avoided risks because they feared blame, slowing experimentation and delivery. Matt organized non-punitive post-mortems and supported lean, low-stakes experiments with structured hypotheses and controlled iterations. Instead of pointing fingers, teams applied lessons from mistakes to subsequent sprints.

In innovation work, he set expectations that early prototypes and user tests would likely fail, intentionally. He used user research, Jobs to Be Done framing, and rapid testing to challenge assumptions before committing to costly builds. Failed experiments became decision inputs rather than setbacks.

He also built balanced teams: Product + Engineering + Design together, with equal participation and shared accountability. Engineers, product managers, and UX architects worked together rather than handing work across role silos. Pair programming, collaborative coding, retrospectives, automation, and continuous delivery were part of daily work.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams achieved 4x delivery acceleration after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. His balanced-team work also achieved zero-defect production code through iterative development and real-time feedback loops.
