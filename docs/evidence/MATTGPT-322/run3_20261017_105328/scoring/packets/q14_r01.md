### B

🐾 Here’s how Matt leads.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. He describes teams he inherited as “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” His focus is on removing those barriers: making intent explicit, creating psychological safety so people can speak honestly, and pushing decisions to the people closest to the work.

At Accenture’s Cloud Innovation Center, he brought engineers, product managers, and UX architects together in balanced teams with equal participation and shared accountability. Instead of work passing between role silos, teams used pair programming, collaborative coding, stand-ups, and retrospectives. Matt connected team goals to client and business outcomes, and gave teams room to voice feedback and change their workflows autonomously.

His “permit to fail” philosophy addressed teams’ fear of blame. He organized non-punitive post-mortems, emphasized lessons rather than finger-pointing, and supported lean, low-stakes experiments. In product incubation, he set expectations upfront that prototypes and user tests would likely fail. Teams used those results to challenge assumptions and shape product direction week by week, before committing to costly builds.

Matt also coaches teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks. He grounds engineering culture in automation, continuous delivery, and quality-first practices, alongside direct feedback, integrity, and respect.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams achieved 4x delivery acceleration after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. His balanced-team work also achieved zero-defect production code, while post-mortem learning prevented recurrence of identified failure patterns.
