## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose.

Most engineering teams he inherited were “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” Engineers, designers, and product teams faced role silos and hierarchical bottlenecks. Some avoided risks because they feared blame. In innovation work, teams often expected polished requirements and predictable outcomes, even when they had assumptions that still needed testing.

Matt’s response was to make intent explicit, push decisions to the people closest to the work, and build psychological safety so people could speak honestly. He used transparency, direct feedback, and open communication. He coached teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks.

At the Accenture Cloud Innovation Center, his “permit to fail” environment included non-punitive post-mortems and lean, low-stakes experiments. Teams shared errors, applied lessons to subsequent sprints, and treated failed experiments as decision inputs rather than setbacks. In product incubation, he set expectations upfront that early prototypes and user tests would likely fail, intentionally, before teams committed to costly builds.

He also assembled balanced teams of engineers, product managers, and UX architects with equal participation and shared accountability. Pair programming, collaborative coding, retrospectives, and continuous delivery replaced siloed handoffs. Teams could voice feedback and implement changes autonomously.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams accelerated delivery 4x after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. His balanced-team work reduced project delays, improved team satisfaction and retention, and achieved zero-defect production code across client engagements.

### B

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. His shorthand is “Builder’s mindset, coach’s heart.” He teaches teams to fish, rather than just fixing problems, and brings Product, Engineering, and Design together in balanced teams.

Many engineering teams he inherited were over-processed, under-trusted, and waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes. At Accenture’s Cloud Innovation Center, fear of blame discouraged experimentation, while hierarchical structures and role silos created bottlenecks. Matt focused on changing those conditions, not adding more process.

He made intent explicit, pushed decisions to the people closest to the work, and used transparency, direct feedback, and open communication. He assembled engineers, product managers, and UX architects with equal participation and shared accountability, connecting team goals to client and business outcomes. Pair programming, collaborative coding, stand-ups, and retrospectives gave teams regular opportunities to share knowledge and address roadblocks.

His “permit to fail” environment included non-punitive post-mortems and lean, low-stakes experiments. In product incubation, he set expectations upfront that early prototypes and user tests would likely fail. Teams used those results to challenge assumptions and shape product direction week by week, before committing to costly builds.

He also grounded engineering culture in automation, continuous delivery, and quality-first practices. At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams achieved 4x delivery acceleration after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. His balanced-team work also produced zero-defect production code, reduced handoffs, and fewer project delays.
