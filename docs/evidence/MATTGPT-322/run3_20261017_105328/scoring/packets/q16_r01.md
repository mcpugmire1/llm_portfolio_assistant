# Question q16, run 1

Labels in this packet: A, B.

## Visitor: Tell me about a time Matt failed

### A

🐾 One failure Matt recounts involved production customer data reaching real clients during testing.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization delivery for a financial services client. The program had development, QA, UAT, and production environments, with offshore teams executing test cycles under delivery pressure. IAM controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from prior phases, along with documentation.

Matt was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup. Instead of verifying the configuration, he assumed those controls and environment boundaries were correct. He focused on delivery pressures, relied on inherited documentation, and did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes. He also did not verify whether production customer names and email addresses had been masked or scrubbed, or implement compensating controls such as email interception.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. They identified insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed from downstream environments, then worked collectively to implement corrective controls.

The client nevertheless demanded that the tester be removed from the project. Matt describes this as “a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence.”

The change he names is specific: IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership are now day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach. His lesson from the incident was that “assumptions are risks in disguise.”
