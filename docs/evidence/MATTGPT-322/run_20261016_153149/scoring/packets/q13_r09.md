## Visitor: Tell me about Matt's payments work at JPMorgan

### A

🐾 Found it!

Matt worked on JP Morgan Chase’s ACCESS Next Generation payments platform from August 2009 to June 2012, serving as Technology Delivery Lead and Senior Technology Architecture Manager. The bank was building its global corporate banking portal on Bottomline Technologies’ WebSeries platform, but its payment, security, and workflow requirements went well beyond the out-of-box offering. Regional systems had different data models, payment formats, entitlement structures, and regulatory requirements, all while live payment processing had to continue.

Matt managed a blended team of 40+ across Accenture and JP Morgan Chase, spanning Mumbai, Bangalore, and the US. He spent 70 to 80% of his time embedded at Bottomline’s Portsmouth offices, managing the build-versus-configure boundary: deciding where WebSeries could handle a requirement and where bespoke engineering was necessary.

He designed and delivered ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and federal tax payment modules. The work included NACHA file validation, foreign-exchange engine integration, payment-specific cut-off and compliance rules, and Message Authentication Code security for verifying payment integrity before release. He also built payment-type-specific permissions, such as allowing wire approval without FX initiation. The broader user administration engine replaced individual permission changes with bulk grouping and role-based profiles, reducing entitlement management from days to minutes.

Alongside the payment engine, Matt delivered the centralized gateway for message transformation, protocol translation, and routing across 12 countries, replacing fragmented point-to-point integrations. He led payments data migration without disruption to live operations, preserving client configurations, entitlements, transaction histories, and compliance traceability. For the regional rollout, he translated jurisdiction-specific requirements into data visibility and access controls, sequencing launches by regulatory readiness rather than a single global deployment. He also owned production incident response and cross-team root-cause analysis.

ACCESS launched in April 2011 as a unified portal serving 135,000+ corporations, financial institutions, governments, and municipalities across 180+ countries. Corporate treasurers gained a single entry point for payments across regions, with real-time global reporting and connected treasury functions.
