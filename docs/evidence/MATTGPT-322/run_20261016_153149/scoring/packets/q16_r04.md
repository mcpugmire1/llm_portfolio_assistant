# Question q16, run 4

Labels in this packet: A, B, C, D.

## Visitor: Tell me about a time Matt failed

### A

🐾 Matt’s example is a failure to verify controls on a global platform modernization delivery.

He was the tech lead for a Fortune 500 financial services client, with offshore teams running test cycles across development, QA, UAT, and production. The program was under delivery pressure. IAM controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from earlier phases, along with documentation.

Matt was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup. Instead of verifying those controls, he assumed they were correct. He relied on inherited documentation, did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes, and did not confirm whether production customer names and email addresses had been masked or scrubbed. He also had not implemented compensating controls such as data masking or email interception.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. They identified insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed from downstream environments. He worked with the team to implement corrective controls.

The client demanded that the tester be removed from the project. Matt described it as “a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence,” and said the assumptions were ones he should have verified rather than inherited.

Afterward, he made IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach. His phrase for the lesson was: “assumptions are risks in disguise.”
