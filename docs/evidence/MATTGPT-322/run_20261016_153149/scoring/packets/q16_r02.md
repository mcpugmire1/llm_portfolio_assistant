# Question q16, run 2

Labels in this packet: A, B, C, D.

## Visitor: Tell me about a time Matt failed

### A

🐾 Here’s the failure Matt points to when asked this question.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization delivery for a financial services client. The program was under delivery pressure, with offshore teams running test cycles across development, QA, UAT, and production environments. IAM controls, environment boundaries, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from prior phases.

Matt was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup, but he relied on the inherited documentation instead of verifying it. He did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes, confirm that production customer data had been masked, or implement compensating controls such as email interception.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients. The root causes were insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed from downstream environments.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. He worked collectively with the team to understand what happened and implement corrective controls, rather than treating the tester’s action as the whole explanation.

The client nevertheless demanded that the tester be removed from the project. Matt describes that as “a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence.”

His stated lesson was that “assumptions are risks in disguise.” IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership became day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach.
