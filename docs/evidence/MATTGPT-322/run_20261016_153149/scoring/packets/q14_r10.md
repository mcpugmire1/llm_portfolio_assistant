## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose.

The starting point in many teams he inherited was being “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” Engineers were constrained by hierarchy and role silos. Some teams avoided risks because they feared blame; others entered innovation work expecting polished requirements and predictable outcomes when they actually had assumptions to test.

Matt’s philosophy is to remove those barriers. He makes intent explicit, pushes decisions to the people closest to the work, and uses transparency, direct feedback, and open communication. He coaches teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks.

At the Accenture Cloud Innovation Center, that meant bringing engineers, product managers, and UX architects into balanced teams with equal participation and shared accountability. He connected team goals to client and business outcomes, introduced pair programming and collaborative coding, and used stand-ups, retrospectives, and knowledge-sharing sessions to surface roadblocks.

His “permit to fail” environment included non-punitive post-mortems and lean, low-stakes experiments. Teams discussed lessons rather than pointing fingers, applied those lessons in subsequent sprints, and tested product assumptions before committing to costly builds. He also embedded clients into teams rather than keeping them at arm’s length until delivery.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams achieved 4x delivery acceleration after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. His work breaking down engineering, design, and product silos also produced zero-defect production code, reduced project delays, and improved team satisfaction and retention.
