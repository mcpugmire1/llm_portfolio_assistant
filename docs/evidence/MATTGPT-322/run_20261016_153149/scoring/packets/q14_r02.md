## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. He describes engineering teams he inherited as “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” Across roles, including leading a 150-person Cloud Innovation Center at Accenture, his focus was to create an environment where engineers, designers, and product teams could do the best work of their careers.

He makes intent explicit, pushes decisions to the people closest to the work, and uses transparency, direct feedback, and open communication. He coaches teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks. His non-negotiables include psychological safety, integrity and respect, cross-functional collaboration early and often, and “outcomes, not optics.” He also describes walking away from opportunities where intensity is not balanced with respect for people, regardless of the title.

His “permit to fail” culture addressed teams’ fear of blame. Matt organized non-punitive post-mortems, emphasized lessons rather than pointing fingers, and supported lean, low-stakes experiments with structured hypotheses and controlled iterations. In product incubation, he set expectations that early prototypes and user tests would likely fail intentionally, so teams could validate assumptions before committing to costly builds.

He also assembled balanced teams of engineers, product managers, and UX architects with equal participation and shared accountability. Teams used pair programming, collaborative coding, retrospectives, and knowledge-sharing sessions rather than relying on role silos and handoffs.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams achieved 4x delivery acceleration after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. The balanced-team work also reduced project delays and achieved zero-defect production code across client engagements.
