## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. He describes teams he inherited as “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” His focus was on removing those barriers, creating an environment where engineers, designers, and product teams could speak honestly, challenge assumptions, and make decisions closest to the work.

At Accenture’s Cloud Innovation Center, he made intent explicit, used transparency and direct feedback, and coached teams to think clearly rather than just execute tasks. He paired that with engineering practices: automation, continuous delivery, and quality-first development. His non-negotiables included integrity and respect, continuous learning, and “outcomes, not optics.”

His “permit to fail” environment addressed teams’ fear of blame. Matt organized non-punitive post-mortems and supported low-stakes experiments with structured hypotheses and controlled iterations. In product incubation, he set expectations that early prototypes and user tests would likely fail intentionally: the goal was to test assumptions before committing to costly builds. Teams applied lessons to subsequent sprints, prevented recurring failure patterns, and reduced wasted engineering effort.

He also brought engineers, product managers, and UX architects into balanced teams with shared accountability. Pair programming, collaborative coding, retrospectives, and open feedback replaced role silos and reduced handoffs.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams achieved 4x delivery acceleration after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. His balanced-team work also produced zero-defect production code, reduced project delays, and improved team satisfaction and retention.
