## Visitor: Tell me about Matt's payments work at JPMorgan

### A

🐾 Here’s what Matt worked on at JP Morgan Chase.

From August 2009 to June 2012, Matt was Technology Delivery Lead for payments work on ACCESS Next Generation, the bank’s unified corporate banking portal. Existing treasury operations ran through separate regional systems, while Bottomline Technologies’ WebSeries platform needed custom engineering to meet the bank’s payment, security, and compliance requirements. Corporate treasurers faced fragmented regional payment systems, and client administrators spent days manually configuring user permissions.

Matt managed a blended team of 40+ Accenture and bank resources across Mumbai, Bangalore, and the US. He spent 70 to 80% of his time embedded at Bottomline’s Portsmouth offices, determining where native platform capabilities ended and bespoke engineering was required. He designed and delivered ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and federal tax payment modules, including payment verification through Message Authentication Codes, payment-type-specific permissions, sanctions screening, and contextual guidance explaining payment fields and requirements.

He also designed the centralized payments gateway: message transformation, protocol translation, and routing across 12 countries, connecting ACCESS to clearing networks and internal compliance, settlement, and reporting systems. For administrators, he introduced role-based access controls, bulk permission changes, and a centralized dashboard. Entitlement management dropped from days to minutes, replacing tedious per-user configuration.

Alongside that engineering, Matt led payments data migration without disrupting live payment operations. He mapped regional regulatory requirements into data visibility and access controls, sequencing launches by regulatory readiness rather than a single global deployment. He also owned production incident response and coordinated root-cause analysis across bank, offshore, and vendor teams.

ACCESS launched in April 2011, consolidating regional systems into a portal serving 135,000+ clients across 180+ countries. Corporate treasurers gained a single entry point for payments across regions, without separate local logins, alongside unified cash reporting and access to treasury functions.
