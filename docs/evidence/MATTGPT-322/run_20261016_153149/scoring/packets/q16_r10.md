## Visitor: Tell me about a time Matt failed

### A

🐾 Here’s a time Matt failed.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization delivery for a financial services client. The program was under delivery pressure, with offshore teams executing test cycles across development, QA, UAT, and production environments. IAM controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from prior phases.

Matt was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup, but he relied on assumptions rather than verifying those controls. He trusted inherited documentation without confirming its accuracy, did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes, and did not verify whether production customer names and email addresses had been masked or scrubbed. He also had not implemented compensating controls such as data masking or email interception.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. They identified insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed from downstream environments. He worked collectively with the team to understand what happened and implement corrective controls.

The client demanded that the tester be removed from the project. Matt described it as “a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence.” His stated lesson was that “assumptions are risks in disguise.” IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership became day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach.
