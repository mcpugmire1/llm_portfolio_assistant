## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. He makes intent explicit, pushes decisions to the people closest to the work, and coaches teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks.

Many engineering teams he inherited were “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams also faced role silos and fear of blame. Engineers avoided risks, while hierarchical structures and handoffs slowed delivery.

Matt worked to replace those conditions with psychological safety and shared accountability. His “permit to fail” environment included non-punitive post-mortems, transparent sharing of errors, and lean, low-stakes experiments. Teams applied lessons from mistakes to subsequent sprints rather than pointing fingers. In product incubation, he set expectations that early prototypes and user tests would likely fail intentionally: the goal was to test assumptions before committing to costly builds.

He assembled balanced teams with engineers, product managers, and UX architects, giving each equal participation and ownership. He embedded clients into the work rather than keeping them at arm’s length. Daily practices included pair programming, test-driven development, continuous integration, retrospectives, and direct feedback. Teams could voice concerns and implement workflow changes autonomously.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, the leadership approach produced 4x delivery acceleration once teams embraced lean product and modern engineering practices. The balanced-team work reduced project delays and achieved zero-defect production code across client engagements. As Matt describes it, the change “didn’t happen overnight, but it stuck.”
