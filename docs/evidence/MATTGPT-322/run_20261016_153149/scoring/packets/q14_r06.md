## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. He describes the goal as creating an environment where engineers, designers, and product teams can do the best work of their careers. Many teams he inherited were “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” Engineers hesitated to take risks because they feared blame, while hierarchical structures and role silos slowed decisions and delivery.

Matt’s response was to make intent explicit, push decisions to the people closest to the work, and coach teams to think clearly rather than just execute tasks. At Accenture’s Cloud Innovation Center, he built balanced teams of engineers, product managers, and UX architects with equal participation and shared accountability. Pair programming, collaborative coding, retrospectives, and open feedback replaced isolated work and unnecessary handoffs. Clients worked inside those teams rather than handing over requirements and waiting for a finished product.

His “permit to fail” environment included non-punitive post-mortems and lean, low-stakes experiments. Teams discussed what they learned instead of pointing fingers. In early-stage product work, he set expectations that prototypes and user tests would sometimes fail intentionally: the purpose was to test assumptions before committing to costly builds. Teams used those findings to shape product direction week by week.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, his leadership-philosophy story records 4x delivery acceleration once teams embraced lean product and modern engineering practices. The balanced-teams work also recorded zero-defect production code, fewer project delays, and improved team satisfaction and retention. Teams began experimenting more freely and applying post-mortem lessons to prevent repeat errors.
