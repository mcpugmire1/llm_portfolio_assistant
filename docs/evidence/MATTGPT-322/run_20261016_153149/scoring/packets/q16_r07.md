# Question q16, run 7

Labels in this packet: A, B, C, D.

## Visitor: Tell me about a time Matt failed

### A

🐾 Here’s a time Matt got it wrong.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization delivery for a Fortune 500 financial services client. The program was under delivery pressure, with offshore teams executing test cycles across development, QA, UAT, and production environments. IAM controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from prior phases.

Matt was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup. Instead of verifying those controls, he relied on inherited documentation and assumed the environment boundaries were correct. He focused on delivery pressures without auditing who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes. He also did not verify whether production customer names and email addresses had been masked or scrubbed, or implement compensating controls such as email interception.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. He worked collectively with the team to understand the insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and unscrubbed production data, then implement corrective controls.

The client nevertheless demanded that the tester be removed from the project. Matt described it as “a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence.”

His stated lesson was that “assumptions are risks in disguise.” IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership became day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach.
