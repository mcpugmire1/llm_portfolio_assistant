## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. Across his roles at Accenture, he inherited engineering teams that were “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” Engineers, designers, and product teams faced hierarchical bottlenecks and role silos. In some teams, fear of blame kept people from experimenting, slowed delivery, and left mistakes unspoken.

Matt focused on removing those barriers rather than adding more process. He made intent explicit, pushed decisions to the people closest to the work, and used transparency, direct feedback, and open communication. His aim was to coach teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks.

His “permit to fail” environment included non-punitive post-mortems, transparent sharing of errors, and lean, low-stakes experiments. In innovation work, he set expectations that early prototypes and user tests would likely fail. Teams used those results to challenge assumptions and shape product direction week by week, before committing to costly builds.

He also assembled balanced teams of engineers, product managers, and UX architects with equal participation and shared accountability. Pair programming, collaborative coding, retrospectives, automation, and continuous delivery gave people regular feedback on their work. Teams could voice concerns and change their workflows without waiting for permission.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams achieved 4x delivery acceleration after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. The balanced-team work reduced handoffs and project delays, improved team satisfaction and retention, and achieved zero-defect production code across client engagements.
