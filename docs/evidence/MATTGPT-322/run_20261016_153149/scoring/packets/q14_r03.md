## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

### B

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. He describes teams he inherited as “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” His focus is on removing those barriers: making intent explicit, creating psychological safety so people speak honestly, and pushing decisions to the people closest to the work.

At Accenture’s Cloud Innovation Center, that included a “permit to fail” learning environment. Teams had avoided risks because they feared blame, slowing delivery and experimentation. Matt organized non-punitive post-mortems, emphasized lessons rather than pointing fingers, and supported low-stakes experiments with structured hypotheses and controlled iterations. In product incubation, he set expectations that early prototypes and user tests would likely fail. Teams used user research, rapid testing, and those failed experiments to shape product direction week by week, before committing to costly builds.

He also built balanced teams: Product + Engineering + Design together. Engineers, product managers, and UX architects shared accountability rather than working through hierarchical handoffs. He embedded pair programming, collaborative coding, retrospectives, and knowledge-sharing sessions, and gave teams room to voice feedback and change their workflows autonomously. His stated aim was to coach teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks.

Across his leadership of a 150-person Cloud Innovation Center, teams achieved 4x delivery acceleration after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. His balanced-team work also achieved zero-defect production code through iterative development and real-time feedback loops.
