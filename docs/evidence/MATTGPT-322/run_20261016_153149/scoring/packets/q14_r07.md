## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose.

Many engineering teams he inherited were “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” His leadership philosophy starts with removing those barriers: make intent explicit, build psychological safety so people can speak honestly, and push decisions to the people closest to the work. He coaches teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks.

At the Accenture Cloud Innovation Center, that included a “permit to fail” environment. Teams had avoided risks because they feared blame. Instead of pointing fingers when something went wrong, Matt organized non-punitive post-mortems and supported lean, low-stakes experiments with structured hypotheses and controlled iterations. Teams applied lessons to subsequent sprints to prevent recurring failure patterns.

In product incubation, he set expectations that early prototypes and user tests would likely fail, intentionally. He used user research, Jobs to Be Done framing, and rapid testing to validate assumptions before committing to costly builds. Failed experiments became decision inputs rather than setbacks.

He also brought engineers, product managers, and UX architects together in balanced teams with equal participation and shared accountability. Pair programming, collaborative coding, retrospectives, automation, and continuous delivery were part of daily work, rather than relying on hierarchy and handoffs.

His non-negotiables include integrity, respect, continuous learning, and “outcomes, not optics.” At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams achieved 4x delivery acceleration after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. His balanced-team work also achieved zero-defect production code across client engagements.
