## Visitor: Tell me about a time Matt failed

### A

🐾 Here’s a time Matt failed.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization for a Fortune 500 financial services client. The program was under delivery pressure, with offshore teams executing test cycles across development, QA, UAT, and production. IAM controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from prior phases, along with documentation.

Matt was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup. Instead of verifying the IAM configuration and downstream access controls, he assumed the inherited setup was correct. He focused on delivery pressures, relied on the documentation without confirming its accuracy, and did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes. He also did not implement compensating controls such as data masking or email interception, or verify that production customer names and email addresses had been scrubbed from downstream environments.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. They identified insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed, then worked collectively to implement corrective controls.

The client demanded that the tester be removed from the project. Matt described it as “a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence.” His stated lesson was that “assumptions are risks in disguise.” IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership became day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach.
