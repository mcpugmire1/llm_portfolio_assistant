## Visitor: Tell me about a time Matt failed

### A

🐾 Here’s a time Matt failed.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization delivery for a financial services client. The program was under delivery pressure, with offshore teams executing test cycles across development, QA, UAT, and production. IAM controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from prior phases, along with existing documentation.

Matt was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup, but he relied on assumptions rather than verifying those controls. He focused on delivery pressures, accepted the inherited documentation without confirming its accuracy, and did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes. He also did not implement compensating controls, such as data masking or email interception, or verify that production customer names and email addresses had been scrubbed from downstream environments.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. They worked collectively to understand what happened and implement corrective controls. The client nevertheless demanded that the tester be removed from the project, a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence.

Matt’s stated lesson was that “assumptions are risks in disguise.” IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership became day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach, rather than inherited conditions he assumed were correct.
