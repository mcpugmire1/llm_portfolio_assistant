## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose.

Most engineering teams he inherited were “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” His focus was to remove those barriers: create psychological safety so people could speak honestly, make intent explicit, and push decisions to the people closest to the work.

At Accenture’s Cloud Innovation Center, that included a “permit to fail” learning environment. Teams had avoided risks because they feared blame, slowing delivery and experimentation. Matt organized non-punitive post-mortems, emphasized lessons rather than finger-pointing, and supported lean, low-stakes experiments with structured hypotheses and controlled iterations. Teams applied post-mortem learnings to subsequent sprints to prevent recurring failures. In product incubation, he set expectations that early prototypes and user tests would likely fail, then used those results to shape product direction week by week.

He also built balanced teams: Product + Engineering + Design together. Engineers, product managers, and UX architects shared accountability rather than working through hierarchical handoffs. Matt embedded pair programming, collaborative coding, retrospectives, and knowledge-sharing sessions. Teams could voice feedback and implement changes autonomously. His stated practice was to “coach teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks.”

Across a 150-person Cloud Innovation Center, his leadership philosophy story reports 4x delivery acceleration once teams embraced lean product and modern engineering practices. The balanced-team work achieved zero-defect production code through iterative development and real-time feedback loops.
