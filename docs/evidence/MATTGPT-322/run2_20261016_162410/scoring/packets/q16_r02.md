## Visitor: Tell me about a time Matt failed

### A

🐾 Matt’s story “What I Learned About Assumptions” addresses this directly.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization delivery for a financial services client. The program had development, QA, UAT, and production environments, with offshore teams executing test cycles under delivery pressure. IAM controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from prior phases, along with documentation.

Matt was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup. He relied on that documentation rather than verifying the configuration. He did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes, confirm that production customer data had been scrubbed, or implement compensating controls such as data masking and email interception.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. They identified insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed from downstream environments. He worked with the team to understand what happened and implement corrective controls.

The client demanded that the tester be removed from the project. Matt described that as “a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence.”

Afterward, Matt made IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach. His stated lesson was that “assumptions are risks in disguise.”
