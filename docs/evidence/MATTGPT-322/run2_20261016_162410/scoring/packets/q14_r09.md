## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. Across his roles, he inherited engineering teams that were “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” His focus was to create an environment where engineers, designers, and product teams could do the best work of their careers, with trust, clear intent, and decisions pushed to the people closest to the work.

At the Accenture Cloud Innovation Center, he established a “permit to fail” learning environment. Teams had avoided risks because they feared blame, slowing experimentation and delivery. Matt introduced non-punitive post-mortems, transparent sharing of mistakes, and lean, low-stakes experiments with structured hypotheses. Teams applied lessons to subsequent sprints, preventing recurrence of identified failure patterns.

In innovation work, he set expectations upfront that prototypes and user tests would sometimes fail intentionally. Rather than waiting for polished requirements, teams used user research, rapid testing, and Build-Measure-Learn loops to validate assumptions before committing to costly builds. Clients worked inside balanced teams rather than handing over requirements and waiting for a finished product.

Matt brought engineering, product, and design together with equal participation and shared accountability. He embedded pair programming, TDD, continuous integration, retrospectives, and direct feedback. He coached teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams achieved 4x delivery acceleration after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. The balanced-team work also produced zero-defect production code through iterative development and real-time feedback loops.
