## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. In the engineering teams he inherited, people were often “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” His focus was to remove those barriers: make intent explicit, create psychological safety so people could speak honestly, and push decisions to the people closest to the work.

At the Accenture Cloud Innovation Center, that included a “permit to fail” learning environment. When fear of blame discouraged experimentation, Matt organized non-punitive post-mortems and supported lean, low-stakes experiments with structured hypotheses and controlled iterations. Teams applied lessons to subsequent sprints rather than pointing fingers. In product incubation, he set expectations that early prototypes and user tests would likely fail intentionally: the goal was to reduce risk quickly and cheaply, not be right on the first try.

He also built balanced teams with engineers, product managers, and UX architects sharing accountability, rather than working through hierarchical handoffs. Daily practices included pair programming, collaborative coding, retrospectives, direct feedback, and opportunities for teams to change their own workflows. He coached teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks.

His stated non-negotiables include integrity, respect, continuous learning, and “outcomes, not optics.” He walks away from opportunities where intensity is not balanced with respect for people.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams achieved 4x delivery acceleration after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. The balanced-team work also achieved zero-defect production code through iterative development and real-time feedback loops.
