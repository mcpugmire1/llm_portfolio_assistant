## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. He describes teams he inherited as “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” His focus is on removing those barriers: making intent explicit, creating psychological safety so people speak honestly, and pushing decisions to the people closest to the work.

At the Accenture Cloud Innovation Center, that included a “permit to fail” learning environment. Teams had avoided risks because they feared blame. Matt organized non-punitive post-mortems, emphasized lessons rather than finger-pointing, and supported low-stakes experiments with structured hypotheses and controlled iterations. Teams applied post-mortem learnings in subsequent sprints to prevent recurring failure patterns.

For innovation work, he set expectations that early prototypes and user tests would likely fail, intentionally. Instead of treating failed experiments as rework or incompetence, teams used them as decision inputs. User research, jobs-to-be-done framing, and rapid testing helped them validate assumptions before committing to costly builds.

Matt also assembled balanced teams with engineers, product managers, and UX architects sharing accountability. Rather than relying on hierarchical handoffs, he embedded pair programming, collaborative coding, retrospectives, and direct feedback. He coached teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks.

His non-negotiables include integrity and respect, clear purpose, continuous learning, and “outcomes, not optics.” At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams achieved 4x delivery acceleration after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. His balanced-team work also achieved zero-defect production code through iterative development and real-time feedback loops.
