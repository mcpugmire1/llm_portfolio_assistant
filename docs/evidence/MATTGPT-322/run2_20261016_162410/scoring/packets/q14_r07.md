## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. His phrase is “Builder’s mindset, coach’s heart.” He teaches teams to fish, rather than just fixing problems, and brings Product, Engineering, and Design together in balanced teams.

Across teams he inherited, engineers were often over-processed, under-trusted, and waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes. At Accenture’s Cloud Innovation Center, fear of blame also discouraged experimentation. Hierarchical structures and role silos created bottlenecks and slowed delivery.

Matt worked to remove those barriers. He made intent explicit, pushed decisions to the people closest to the work, and used transparency, direct feedback, and open communication. He coached teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks. Balanced teams brought engineers, product managers, and UX architects together with equal participation and shared accountability. Pair programming, collaborative coding, retrospectives, automation, and continuous delivery became part of daily work.

His “permit to fail” environment had specific practices behind it: non-punitive post-mortems, transparent sharing of errors, and lean, low-stakes experiments. In innovation work, he set expectations that early prototypes and user tests would likely fail intentionally. Teams used user research, Jobs to Be Done framing, and rapid testing to validate assumptions before committing to costly builds. Failed experiments became decision inputs rather than setbacks.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams accelerated delivery 4x after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. In the balanced-team work, teams reduced handoffs and project delays and achieved zero-defect production code through iterative development and real-time feedback loops.
