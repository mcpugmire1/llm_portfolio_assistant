## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. He builds psychological safety so people can take risks and speak honestly, pushes decisions to the people closest to the work, and coaches teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks.

Across his roles at Accenture, including leading a 150-person Cloud Innovation Center, Matt inherited teams that were over-processed, under-trusted, and waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes. Hierarchical structures and role silos created bottlenecks. Fear of blame kept people from experimenting, while early-stage product teams sometimes expected polished requirements and predictable outcomes where there were only assumptions to test.

Matt made intent explicit and used transparency, direct feedback, and open communication. He established a “permit to fail” learning environment through non-punitive post-mortems and lean, low-stakes experiments. Teams examined mistakes for lessons rather than pointing fingers. In product incubation, he set expectations that prototypes and user tests would sometimes fail, then used those results to shape product direction week by week.

He also assembled balanced teams with engineers, product managers, and UX architects sharing accountability. Pair programming, collaborative coding, retrospectives, automation, and continuous delivery were part of daily work, rather than separate process requirements.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams achieved 4x delivery acceleration after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. His work breaking down engineering, design, and product silos also produced zero-defect production code, reduced handoffs, and fewer project delays.
