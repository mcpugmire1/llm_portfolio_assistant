## Visitor: Tell me about Matt's payments work at JPMorgan

### A

🐾 Here’s the story behind Matt’s payments work at JP Morgan Chase.

From August 2009 to June 2012, Matt served as Technology Delivery Lead for ACCESS Next Generation, the bank’s unified corporate banking portal. Bottomline Technologies’ WebSeries platform provided the foundation, but its out-of-box capabilities did not cover the bank’s payment requirements. Regional systems had different data models, entitlement structures, and compliance records. Payment connectivity spanned 12 countries, each with distinct formats, protocols, and regulatory requirements.

Matt managed a blended team of 40+ Accenture and bank resources across Mumbai, Bangalore, and the US. He defined the onshore-offshore engagement model and spent 70 to 80% of his time embedded at Bottomline’s Portsmouth offices. He worked directly with the vendor to determine where WebSeries configuration ended and custom engineering was required, while coordinating with bank stakeholders in Chicago and NY/NJ.

He designed and delivered ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and federal tax payment modules, plus the centralized gateway for message transformation, protocol translation, and routing. His work included payment verification through Message Authentication Codes, sanctions screening, risk-based transaction restrictions, and payment-type-specific permissions. Working with enterprise architects, security specialists, and Bottomline, he replaced individual permission configuration with role-based profiles and bulk changes, reducing entitlement administration from days to minutes.

Matt also coordinated legacy-data migration and worked with regional compliance officers across APAC, EMEA, and the Americas to translate local requirements into data visibility and access controls. Regional launches were sequenced by regulatory readiness rather than a single global deployment. During production incidents, he directed cross-team root-cause analysis and ran status updates and daily leadership calls.

ACCESS launched in April 2011, serving 135,000+ corporate, financial institution, government, and municipal clients across 180+ countries. The gateway supported payments across 12 countries. Legacy migration completed without disruption to live payments, and regional launches proceeded without regulatory blockers.
