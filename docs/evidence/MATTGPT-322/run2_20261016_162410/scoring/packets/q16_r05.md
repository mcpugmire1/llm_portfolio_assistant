## Visitor: Tell me about a time Matt failed

### A

🐾 Here’s a time Matt failed and took accountability.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization delivery for a financial services client. The program was under delivery pressure, with offshore teams running test cycles across development, QA, UAT, and production environments. His responsibility was to ensure a safe environment setup, but he relied on inherited documentation rather than verifying the IAM configuration, environment boundaries, and downstream access controls.

He focused on delivery pressures instead of validating access patterns. He did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes, implement compensating controls such as data masking and email interception, or verify that production customer names and email addresses had been scrubbed from downstream environments.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. He worked collectively with the team to understand what happened and implement corrective controls. The investigation identified insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed.

The client nevertheless demanded that the tester be removed from the project. Matt described it as “a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence.”

His stated lesson was that “assumptions are risks in disguise.” IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership became day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach.
