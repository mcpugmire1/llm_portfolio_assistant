## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. He builds a “permit to fail” learning environment and balanced teams: Product + Engineering + Design together.

Across his roles at Accenture, Matt inherited teams that were over-processed, under-trusted, and waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes. Hierarchical structures and role silos created bottlenecks. Some teams avoided experimentation because they feared blame; others entered innovation work expecting polished requirements and predictable outcomes when they had assumptions to test.

Matt made intent explicit and pushed decisions to the people closest to the work. He used transparency, direct feedback, and open communication, coaching teams to think clearly rather than just execute tasks. He brought engineers, product managers, and UX architects together with equal participation and shared accountability, connecting team goals to client and business outcomes.

His “permit to fail” practices included non-punitive post-mortems, transparent sharing of mistakes, and lean, low-stakes experiments. In early product work, he set expectations that prototypes and user tests would likely fail, then helped teams use those findings to shape product direction week by week. Pair programming, collaborative coding, retrospectives, automation, and continuous delivery supported that daily work.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams accelerated delivery 4x after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. In his work breaking down engineering, design, and product silos, teams reduced handoffs and project delays and achieved zero-defect production code through iterative development and real-time feedback loops.
