## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. Across his work at Accenture, he encountered engineering teams that were “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” His goal was to create an environment where engineers, designers, and product teams could do the best work of their careers, built on trust, clarity, and shared purpose rather than just process or tools.

He makes intent explicit, pushes decisions to the people closest to the work, and uses transparency, direct feedback, and open communication. He coaches teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks. At the Cloud Innovation Center, he assembled balanced teams of engineers, product managers, and UX architects with equal participation and shared accountability. Pair programming, collaborative coding, stand-ups, and retrospectives gave those teams regular opportunities to share knowledge, identify roadblocks, and change how they worked.

His “permit to fail” philosophy addresses fear of blame directly. Matt organized non-punitive post-mortems, emphasized lessons learned rather than pointing fingers, and supported lean, low-stakes experiments. In product incubation, he set expectations that early prototypes and tests would sometimes fail. Teams used those results to shape product direction week by week rather than treating them as setbacks.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams achieved 4x delivery acceleration after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. His balanced-team work also reduced handoffs and project delays and achieved zero-defect production code.
