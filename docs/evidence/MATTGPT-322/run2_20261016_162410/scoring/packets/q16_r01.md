# Question q16, run 1

Labels in this packet: A, B, C, D.

## Visitor: Tell me about a time Matt failed

### A

🐾 Here’s a time Matt failed on a platform delivery.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization for a financial services client. The program was under delivery pressure, with offshore teams running test cycles across development, QA, UAT, and production. IAM controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from earlier phases.

Matt was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup, but he relied on inherited documentation rather than verifying it. He focused on delivery pressures instead of validating access patterns. He did not audit who could trigger downstream email jobs, implement compensating controls such as data masking and email interception, or confirm that production customer data had been scrubbed from downstream environments.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. They identified insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed, then worked collectively to implement corrective controls.

The client demanded that the tester be removed from the project. Matt described this as “a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence.”

Afterward, Matt made IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach. His stated lesson was that “assumptions are risks in disguise.”
