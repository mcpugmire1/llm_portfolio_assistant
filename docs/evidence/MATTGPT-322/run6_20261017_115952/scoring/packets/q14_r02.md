## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. He describes the teams he inherited as “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” His focus is on removing those barriers: making intent explicit, creating psychological safety so people can speak honestly, and pushing decisions to the people closest to the work.

At Accenture’s Cloud Innovation Center, that included establishing a “permit to fail” learning environment. Teams had avoided risks because they feared blame. Matt organized non-punitive post-mortems, emphasized lessons rather than pointing fingers, and supported lean, low-stakes experiments. Teams began experimenting more freely, applied post-mortem learnings to subsequent sprints, and prevented recurrence of identified failure patterns.

He also builds balanced teams: Product + Engineering + Design together. Rather than keeping those roles in separate silos, Matt assembled engineers, product managers, and UX architects with equal participation and shared accountability. He embedded pair programming, collaborative coding, retrospectives, and knowledge-sharing sessions. Teams could voice feedback and implement changes autonomously instead of waiting for permission.

His coaching focus is to help teams think clearly, not just execute tasks. He pairs that with automation, continuous delivery, and quality-first practices. At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams accelerated delivery 4x after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. In the balanced-team work, teams reduced handoffs and project delays, improved satisfaction and retention, and achieved zero-defect production code.
