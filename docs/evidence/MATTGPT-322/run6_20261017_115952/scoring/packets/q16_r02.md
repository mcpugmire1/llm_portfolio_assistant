# Question q16, run 2

Labels in this packet: A, B.

## Visitor: Tell me about a time Matt failed

### A

🐾 Here’s a time Matt’s assumptions led to a production-data incident.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization delivery for a Fortune 500 financial services client. The program was under delivery pressure, with offshore teams executing test cycles across development, QA, UAT, and production. IAM controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from prior phases, along with existing documentation.

Matt was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup. He relied on that documentation rather than confirming its accuracy. He did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes, verify whether production customer data had been masked, or implement compensating controls such as email interception.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. They identified insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed from downstream environments. He worked collectively with the team to understand what happened and implement corrective controls.

The client demanded that the tester be removed from the project. Matt described it as “a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence,” and acknowledged that these were assumptions he should have verified rather than inherited.

His stated lesson was that “assumptions are risks in disguise.” IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership became day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach.
