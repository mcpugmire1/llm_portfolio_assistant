Labels in this packet: A, B.

## Visitor: Tell me about a time Matt failed

### A

🐾 Matt’s story “What I Learned About Assumptions” is about a failure on a global platform modernization delivery.

Matt was the tech lead for a Fortune 500 financial services client. The program was under delivery pressure, with offshore teams executing test cycles across development, QA, UAT, and production. IAM controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from prior phases. Matt was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup, but he relied on the existing documentation rather than verifying that the controls were correct.

He focused on delivery pressures instead of validating access patterns. He did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes, implement compensating controls such as data masking and email interception, or verify whether production customer names and email addresses had been scrubbed from downstream environments.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. He worked collectively with the team to understand what happened and implement corrective controls. The root causes were insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed.

The client nevertheless demanded that the tester be removed from the project. Matt described it as “a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence.” His stated lesson was that “leaders own the environment their teams operate in.” IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership became day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach.

### B

🐾 Here’s a time Matt’s assumptions led to a real production-data incident.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization delivery for a financial services client. The program was under delivery pressure, with offshore teams running test cycles across development, QA, UAT, and production. IAM controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from prior phases, along with existing documentation.

Matt was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup. Instead of verifying the configuration, he assumed the IAM controls and environment boundaries were correct. He relied on inherited documentation without confirming its accuracy, did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes, and did not verify whether production customer names and email addresses had been masked or scrubbed. Compensating controls, including data masking and email interception, were not in place.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. He worked with the team to understand what happened and implement corrective controls. The root causes were insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed from downstream environments.

The client demanded that the tester be removed from the project. Matt described that as “a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence.”

Afterward, Matt made IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership day-one responsibilities in his delivery approach. His stated lesson was that “assumptions are risks in disguise,” and that leaders own the environment their teams operate in.
