## Visitor: Tell me about Matt's payments work at JPMorgan

### A

From August 2009 to June 2012, Matt served as Technology Delivery Lead for JP Morgan Chase’s ACCESS Next Generation payments work. The bank was building its global corporate banking portal on Bottomline Technologies’ WebSeries platform, but its payment, security, and workflow requirements went well beyond the out-of-box offering. Regional systems also had different data models, payment formats, and compliance requirements. Entitlement changes that should have taken minutes required days of manual configuration.

Matt managed a blended team of 40+ across Accenture and JP Morgan Chase, spanning Mumbai, Bangalore, and the US. He spent 70 to 80% of his time embedded at Bottomline’s Portsmouth offices, working through where the platform’s native capabilities ended and bespoke engineering was required.

He designed and delivered ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and tax payment modules, including NACHA file validation, embedded foreign-exchange rate visibility, and Message Authentication Code security for payment verification before release. He also designed the centralized gateway for message transformation, protocol translation, and payment routing across 12 countries, replacing fragmented point-to-point integrations.

For corporate administrators, Matt introduced bulk permission changes across users, accounts, and products from a single screen, a capability absent from the legacy systems. Payment-type-specific controls allowed, for example, wire approval without FX initiation. Entitlement management dropped from days to minutes.

Matt also led the payments data migration and translated regional regulatory requirements into data visibility and access controls. Rather than separate regional platform instances, the team maintained a single global architecture with jurisdiction-specific configuration and sequenced launches by regulatory readiness.

ACCESS launched in April 2011, serving 135,000+ clients across 180+ countries. The gateway supported payments across 12 countries; the legacy migration completed without disruption to live payment operations, and regional launches proceeded without regulatory blockers.

Contextual help explained wire fields, tax codes, and FX requirements directly within payment workflows.

Corporate treasurers gained a single entry point for payments across regions rather than separate local logins. For corporate client administrators, his user administration engine replaced tedious per-user configuration with bulk permission changes across users, accounts, and products. Entitlement management fell from days to minutes.

Matt led payments data migration without disruption to live payment operations and coordinated jurisdiction-specific data visibility and access controls across APAC, EMEA, and the Americas. Regional launches were sequenced by regulatory readiness, with compliance verification dashboards supporting sign-off. He also owned production incident response and cross-team root-cause analysis.
