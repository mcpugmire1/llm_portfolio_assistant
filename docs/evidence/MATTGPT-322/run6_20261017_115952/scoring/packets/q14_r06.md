## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. He builds a “permit to fail” learning environment and balanced teams: Product + Engineering + Design together. His aim is to create an environment where engineers, designers, and product teams can do the best work of their careers.

Most engineering teams he inherited were stuck: over-processed, under-trusted, and waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes. At Accenture’s Cloud Innovation Center, fear of blame kept teams from experimenting, while hierarchical structures and role silos created bottlenecks and slower delivery.

Matt made intent explicit and pushed decisions to the people closest to the work. He used transparency, direct feedback, and open communication, and coached teams to think clearly rather than just execute tasks. Instead of pointing fingers when something failed, he organized non-punitive post-mortems and carried lessons into subsequent sprints. Teams tested assumptions through lean, low-stakes experiments before committing to costly builds.

He assembled balanced teams of engineers, product managers, and UX architects with equal participation and shared accountability. Pair programming, collaborative coding, retrospectives, automation, and continuous delivery were part of daily work. Teams could voice feedback and implement changes autonomously.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams achieved 4x delivery acceleration once they embraced lean product and modern engineering practices. In the balanced-team work, teams reduced handoffs and project delays and achieved zero-defect production code through iterative development and real-time feedback loops.
