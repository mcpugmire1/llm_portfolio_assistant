## Visitor: Tell me about Matt's payments work at JPMorgan

### A

🐾 Here’s the story behind Matt’s payments work at JP Morgan Chase.

From August 2009 to June 2012, Matt served as Technology Delivery Lead for ACCESS Next Generation, the bank’s unified corporate banking portal. Its WebSeries foundation could not meet the bank’s requirements out of the box. Regional payment systems had different formats, client configurations, entitlement structures, and compliance records. Corporate administrators were spending days configuring permissions, while the program faced aggressive delivery timelines.

Matt managed a blended team of 40+ Accenture and JP Morgan Chase resources across Mumbai, Bangalore, and the US. He spent 70 to 80% of his time embedded at Bottomline Technologies’ Portsmouth offices, determining where WebSeries’ native capabilities ended and bespoke engineering was required.

He designed and delivered ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and federal tax payment modules, including NACHA validation, payment-integrity verification through Message Authentication Codes, and contextual help explaining payment-specific requirements. He also built payment-type-specific permissions: a user could be authorized for wire approval without being permitted to initiate FX payments.

Alongside those modules, Matt designed the centralized payments gateway connecting internal settlement and compliance systems with payment rails across 12 countries. It handled message transformation, protocol translation, and routing instead of retaining fragmented point-to-point integrations.

For administrators, he introduced bulk permission changes across users, accounts, and products from a single screen, reducing entitlement management from days to minutes. For regional compliance teams, he translated jurisdiction-specific requirements into data visibility and access controls, sequencing launches by regulatory readiness rather than a single global deployment.

ACCESS launched in April 2011, serving 135,000+ clients across 180+ countries. The payments migration completed without disruption to live payment operations, and the regional compliance framework enabled launches across APAC, EMEA, and the Americas without regulatory blockers.

He led payments data migration from legacy regional systems, preserving client configurations, entitlements, transaction histories, and compliance traceability. Corporate treasurers gained a single entry point for payments across regions, replacing separate local logins and fragmented integrations. The migration completed without disruption to live payment operations, and the redesigned entitlement engine reduced administration from days to minutes through bulk permission changes and role-based profiles.
