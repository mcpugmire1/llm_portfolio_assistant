## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. He creates a “permit to fail” learning environment and brings Product + Engineering + Design together in balanced teams.

His own phrase is “Builder’s mindset, coach’s heart.” He teaches teams to fish, rather than just fixing problems. His core values are Empathy, Authenticity, Curiosity, Integrity, and Leadership.

Matt describes his work this way: he builds what’s next, modernizes what’s not, and grows teams along the way.

### B

🐾 Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose.

Many engineering teams he inherited were “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” Teams avoided risks because they feared blame, while hierarchical structures and silos between engineering, design, and product slowed delivery. His focus was to create an environment where people could speak honestly, challenge assumptions, and make decisions close to the work.

Matt makes intent explicit, uses transparency and direct feedback, and coaches teams to think clearly rather than just execute tasks. At the Accenture Cloud Innovation Center, he assembled balanced teams of engineers, product managers, and UX architects with equal participation and shared accountability. He connected team goals to client and business outcomes, introduced pair programming and collaborative coding, and used stand-ups, retrospectives, and knowledge-sharing sessions to identify roadblocks. Teams could voice feedback and implement changes autonomously.

His “permit to fail” philosophy meant non-punitive post-mortems and lean, low-stakes experiments, not pointing fingers. In innovation engagements, he set expectations upfront that prototypes and user tests would likely fail. Teams used those results to challenge assumptions and shape product direction week by week, before committing to costly builds. He also chooses work environments around psychological safety, clear purpose, integrity, and respect, with a focus on outcomes rather than optics.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams accelerated delivery 4x after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. The balanced-team work also reduced handoffs and project delays and achieved zero-defect production code through iterative development and real-time feedback loops.
