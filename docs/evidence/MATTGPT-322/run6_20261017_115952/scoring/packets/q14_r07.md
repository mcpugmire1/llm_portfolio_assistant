## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. He describes himself as having a “builder’s mindset, coach’s heart” and teaches teams to fish rather than just fixing problems.

Many engineering teams he inherited were “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” His response was to make intent explicit, push decisions to the people closest to the work, and coach teams to think clearly rather than simply execute tasks. He uses transparency, direct feedback, and open communication so people can raise problems and challenge assumptions.

At Accenture’s Cloud Innovation Center, he established a “permit to fail” learning environment. Teams had avoided risks because they feared blame. Matt organized non-punitive post-mortems, emphasized lessons rather than finger-pointing, and supported low-stakes experiments with structured hypotheses and controlled iterations. Teams applied those lessons in subsequent sprints to prevent recurring failure patterns.

He also brought engineers, product managers, and UX architects together in balanced teams with equal participation and shared accountability. Instead of handing work between separate functions, they used pair programming, collaborative coding, retrospectives, and real-time feedback. Matt connected team goals to client and business outcomes and gave teams room to change their own workflows.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams accelerated delivery 4x after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. His balanced-team work also achieved zero-defect production code through iterative development and real-time feedback loops.
