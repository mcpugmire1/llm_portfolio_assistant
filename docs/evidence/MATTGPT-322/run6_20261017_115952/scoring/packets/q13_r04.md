## Visitor: Tell me about Matt's payments work at JPMorgan

### A

🐾 Here’s the story behind Matt’s payments work at JP Morgan Chase.

From August 2009 to June 2012, Matt served as Technology Delivery Lead for ACCESS Next Generation, the bank’s global corporate banking portal. Its treasury operations relied on separate regional systems, while Bottomline Technologies’ WebSeries platform needed custom engineering to meet the bank’s payment, security, and compliance requirements. Corporate treasurers faced separate local logins and regional payment systems. Client administrators spent days manually configuring permissions.

Matt managed a blended team of 40+ Accenture and bank resources across Mumbai, Bangalore, and the US. He spent 70 to 80% of his time embedded at Bottomline’s Portsmouth offices, managing where the platform’s native capabilities ended and bank-specific development was required.

He designed and delivered ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and government tax payment modules, including NACHA file validation, foreign exchange integration, and Message Authentication Code security so users could verify payment integrity before release. Contextual help explained wire formatting, tax payment codes, and FX parameters directly within payment workflows.

He also delivered the centralized payments gateway, handling message transformation, protocol translation, and routing across 12 countries. For administrators, he replaced individual permission configuration with role-based profiles, bulk changes across users and accounts, and a centralized dashboard. Entitlement management dropped from days to minutes.

Alongside that engineering, Matt led payments data migration without disruption to live payment operations. He coordinated jurisdiction-specific data visibility and access controls across APAC, EMEA, and the Americas, and owned production incident response, root-cause analysis, and live status communications.

ACCESS launched in April 2011, consolidating regional systems into a unified portal serving 135,000+ corporations, financial institutions, governments, and municipalities across 180+ countries. Corporate treasurers gained a single entry point for payments across regions, real-time global reporting, and single sign-on across treasury functions.
