# Question q16, run 5

Labels in this packet: A, B.

## Visitor: Tell me about a time Matt failed

### A

🐾 Here’s a time Matt’s assumptions led to a production-data incident.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization delivery for a Fortune 500 financial services client. The program had development, QA, UAT, and production environments, with offshore teams executing test cycles under delivery pressure. IAM controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from prior phases, along with existing documentation.

Matt was responsible for ensuring a safe environment setup, but he relied on that documentation instead of verifying it. He focused on delivery pressures without confirming access patterns, auditing who could trigger downstream email processes, or verifying that production customer names and email addresses had been scrubbed. He also had not implemented compensating controls such as data masking or email interception.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. They identified insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed from downstream environments. He worked with the team to implement corrective controls.

The client demanded that the tester be removed from the project. Matt described it as “a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence.”

Afterward, IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership became day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach. His stated lesson was that “assumptions are risks in disguise.”
