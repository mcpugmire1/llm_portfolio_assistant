# Question q16, run 4

Labels in this packet: A, B.

## Visitor: Tell me about a time Matt failed

### A

🐾 Here’s a time Matt failed.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization delivery for a Fortune 500 financial services client, with offshore teams testing across development, QA, UAT, and production environments. The program was under delivery pressure. IAM controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from prior phases, along with documentation Matt relied on without confirming its accuracy.

His responsibility was to ensure a safe environment setup. Instead, he assumed the IAM configuration and environment boundaries were correct. He did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes, implement compensating controls such as data masking or email interception, or verify whether production customer names and email addresses had been scrubbed from downstream environments.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. The team traced the incident to insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed. They worked collectively to understand what happened and implement corrective controls.

The client nevertheless demanded that the tester be removed from the project. Matt described it as “a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence.” His stated lesson was that “assumptions are risks in disguise.” IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership became day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach.
