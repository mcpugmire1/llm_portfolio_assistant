## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

### B

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. Across the engineering teams he inherited, the recurring problem was teams that were “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” At Accenture, his work ranged from modern engineering initiatives to leading a 150-person Cloud Innovation Center. His focus was creating an environment where engineers, designers, and product teams could do the best work of their careers.

He makes intent explicit, pushes decisions to the people closest to the work, and uses transparency, direct feedback, and open communication. He coaches teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks. His non-negotiables include psychological safety, integrity and respect, outcomes rather than optics, and continuous learning.

That includes a “permit to fail” learning environment. When teams avoided risks because they feared blame, Matt organized non-punitive post-mortems and supported lean, low-stakes experiments with structured hypotheses and controlled iterations. In product incubation, he set expectations that early prototypes and user tests would likely fail intentionally: teams were testing assumptions before committing to costly builds. Post-mortem learnings went into subsequent sprints to prevent repeat failures.

He also built balanced teams with engineers, product managers, and UX architects sharing accountability, rather than working through hierarchical handoffs. Daily practices included pair programming, collaborative coding, retrospectives, automation, and continuous delivery.

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams achieved 4x delivery acceleration once they embraced lean product and modern engineering practices. In the balanced-team work, teams reduced project delays and achieved zero-defect production code through iterative development and real-time feedback loops.
