## Visitor: Tell me about Matt's payments work at JPMorgan

### A

🐾 Here’s the story behind Matt’s payments work at JP Morgan Chase.

From August 2009 to June 2012, Matt served as Technology Delivery Lead for ACCESS Next Generation, the bank’s global corporate banking portal. Treasury operations ran across separate regional systems with different payment formats, client configurations, permissions, and compliance records. Bottomline Technologies’ WebSeries platform provided the foundation, but the bank needed custom engineering for its 135,000+ clients across 180+ countries. Corporate administrators spent days configuring permissions, while treasurers navigated separate regional payment systems.

Matt managed a blended team of 40+ Accenture and bank resources across the US, Mumbai, and Bangalore. He spent 70 to 80% of his time embedded at Bottomline’s Portsmouth offices, working through where native platform capabilities ended and bank-specific development was required.

He designed and delivered payment modules for ACH credits and direct debits, wires, FX/cross-border payments, and federal tax payments. The work included NACHA file validation, embedded FX rate visibility, Message Authentication Code security, and payment-type-specific permissions. He also built contextual help explaining wire fields, tax codes, and cross-border requirements directly within payment workflows.

Alongside the payment engine, Matt designed the centralized gateway for message transformation, protocol translation, and routing across 12 countries. He led payments data migration, preserving client configurations, entitlements, transaction histories, and compliance traceability without disrupting live payment operations. He also owned production incident response and coordinated root-cause analysis across the bank, offshore teams, and Bottomline.

For corporate administrators, his user administration engine replaced individual permission changes with bulk updates across users, accounts, and products. Entitlement management dropped from days to minutes, with a centralized dashboard showing who could access which payment functions.

ACCESS launched in April 2011. Corporate treasurers gained a single entry point for wire, ACH, and cross-border transfers across regional accounts, eliminating separate local logins, alongside real-time global reporting and single sign-on across treasury functions.
