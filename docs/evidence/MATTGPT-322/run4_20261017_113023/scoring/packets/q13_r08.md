## Visitor: Tell me about Matt's payments work at JPMorgan

### A

🐾 Here’s the story behind Matt’s payments work at JP Morgan Chase.

From August 2009 to June 2012, Matt served as Technology Delivery Lead for payments on ACCESS Next Generation, the bank’s global corporate banking portal. Its regional systems had different payment formats, client configurations, entitlement structures, and transaction histories. Bottomline Technologies’ WebSeries platform provided the foundation, but the bank’s requirements went well beyond its out-of-box capabilities. The broader portal served 135,000+ clients across 180+ countries, with aggressive delivery timelines and live payment operations to preserve.

Matt managed a blended team of 40+ Accenture and bank resources across Mumbai, Bangalore, and the US. He spent 70 to 80% of his time embedded at Bottomline’s Portsmouth offices, determining where platform configuration ended and bespoke engineering was required.

He designed and delivered ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and federal tax payment modules. That included NACHA file validation, integration with the bank’s foreign exchange engine, and Message Authentication Code security so corporate users could verify payment integrity before release. He also built payment-type-specific permissions, such as allowing wire approval without FX initiation.

Alongside those modules, Matt designed the centralized payments gateway: message transformation, protocol translation, and routing across payment networks in 12 countries. He led payments data migration from legacy regional systems, preserving client configurations, entitlements, transaction histories, and compliance traceability. He also owned production incident response and coordinated root-cause analysis across the bank, offshore teams, and Bottomline.

ACCESS and the Integrated Global Payments Interface launched in April 2011. Corporate treasurers gained a single entry point for payments across regions, replacing separate local logins and fragmented integrations. The migration completed without disruption to live payment operations, and the redesigned entitlement engine reduced administration from days to minutes through bulk permission changes and role-based profiles.
