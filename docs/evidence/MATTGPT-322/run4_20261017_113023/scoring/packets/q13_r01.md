## Visitor: Tell me about Matt's payments work at JPMorgan

Regional systems also had different data models, payment formats, and compliance requirements. Entitlement changes that should have taken minutes required days of manual configuration.

He designed and delivered ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and tax payment modules, including NACHA file validation, embedded foreign-exchange rate visibility, and Message Authentication Code security for payment verification before release. He also designed the centralized gateway for message transformation, protocol translation, and payment routing across 12 countries, replacing fragmented point-to-point integrations.

For corporate administrators, Matt introduced bulk permission changes across users, accounts, and products from a single screen, a capability absent from the legacy systems. Payment-type-specific controls allowed, for example, wire approval without FX initiation. Entitlement management dropped from days to minutes.

Matt also led the payments data migration and translated regional regulatory requirements into data visibility and access controls. Rather than separate regional platform instances, the team maintained a single global architecture with jurisdiction-specific configuration and sequenced launches by regulatory readiness.

ACCESS launched in April 2011, serving 135,000+ clients across 180+ countries. The gateway supported payments across 12 countries; the legacy migration completed without disruption to live payment operations, and regional launches proceeded without regulatory blockers.

### B

🐾 Here’s the story behind Matt’s payments work at JP Morgan Chase.

From August 2009 to June 2012, Matt served as Technology Delivery Lead for ACCESS Next Generation, the bank’s unified corporate banking portal. The challenge went beyond installing Bottomline Technologies’ WebSeries platform. Existing regional systems had different data models, payment formats, permissions, and compliance requirements. The bank needed purpose-built capabilities for 135,000+ clients across 180+ countries, while keeping live payment operations running.

Matt managed a blended team of 40+ Accenture and bank resources across the US, Mumbai, and Bangalore. He spent 70-80% of his time embedded at Bottomline’s Portsmouth offices, working through where native platform capabilities ended and custom engineering was required. He designed and delivered ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, and federal tax payment modules, including NACHA file validation, payment-type-specific permissions, and Message Authentication Code security so corporate users could verify payment integrity before release.

He also designed the centralized payments gateway: message transformation, protocol translation, and routing across 12 countries. Rather than retaining fragmented point-to-point integrations, the gateway connected ACCESS to clearing networks and internal compliance, settlement, and reporting systems.

For administrators, Matt replaced individual permission configuration with role-based profiles and bulk changes across users, accounts, and products. Entitlement management dropped from days to minutes. For regional compliance teams, he translated jurisdiction-specific requirements into data visibility and access controls, sequencing launches by regulatory readiness rather than a single global deployment.

ACCESS launched in April 2011. The payments migration completed without disruption to live payment operations, preserving client configurations, entitlements, transaction histories, and compliance traceability. Matt also owned production incident response, coordinating root-cause analysis and leadership updates when payment issues surfaced.
