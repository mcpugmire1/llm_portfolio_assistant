# Question q16, run 4

Labels in this packet: A, B.

## Visitor: Tell me about a time Matt failed

### A

🐾 Here’s a time Matt took accountability for a delivery failure.

Matt was the tech lead on a global platform modernization for a financial services client. The program was under delivery pressure, with offshore teams running test cycles across development, QA, UAT, and production environments. IAM controls, environment segregation, and downstream access configurations had been inherited from prior phases, along with documentation.

Matt’s responsibility was to ensure a safe environment setup. Instead, he relied on that documentation without confirming its accuracy. He assumed the IAM configuration and environment boundaries were correct, did not audit who could trigger downstream jobs or email processes, and did not verify whether production customer data had been masked or scrubbed. Compensating controls, such as data masking and email interception, were not in place.

An offshore tester accidentally triggered an email job in UAT. It sent communications containing unmasked production customer data to thousands of real clients.

Matt took accountability and led the team through root cause analysis and remediation. They worked collectively to understand what happened and implement corrective controls. The root causes were insufficient IAM checks, unclear environment boundaries, and production data that had not been scrubbed from downstream environments.

The client demanded that the tester be removed from the project. Matt described that as “a systemic failure that resulted in an individual consequence.” His stated lesson was that “assumptions are risks in disguise.” IAM validation, environment verification, PII scrubbing, and risk ownership became day-one leadership responsibilities in his delivery approach.
