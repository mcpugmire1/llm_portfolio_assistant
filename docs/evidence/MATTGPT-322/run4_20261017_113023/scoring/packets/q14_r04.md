## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. He describes teams he inherited as “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” His focus is on removing those barriers: making intent explicit, creating psychological safety so people can speak honestly, and pushing decisions to the people closest to the work.

At Accenture’s Cloud Innovation Center, he put a “permit to fail” learning environment into practice. Teams had avoided risks because they feared blame. Matt organized non-punitive post-mortems, emphasized lessons rather than finger-pointing, and supported lean, low-stakes experiments with structured hypotheses and controlled iterations. Teams applied post-mortem learning to subsequent sprints, preventing recurrence of identified failure patterns.

For innovation work, he set expectations that early prototypes and user tests would likely fail, and that this was intentional. Rather than expecting polished requirements and predictable outcomes, teams used user research, rapid testing, and evidence from failed experiments to shape product direction week by week.

Matt also builds balanced teams: Product + Engineering + Design together. He brought engineers, product managers, and UX architects into teams with equal participation and shared accountability. Pair programming, collaborative coding, retrospectives, automation, and continuous delivery were part of daily work. His stated aim was to “coach teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks.”

At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams achieved 4x delivery acceleration after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. His balanced-team work also achieved zero-defect production code through iterative development and real-time feedback loops.
