## Visitor: What's Matt's leadership philosophy?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt describes his leadership philosophy.

Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose. He creates a “permit to fail” learning environment and builds balanced teams: Product + Engineering + Design together.

His own description is “Builder’s mindset, coach’s heart.” He teaches teams to fish, doesn’t just fix problems. His core values are Empathy, Authenticity, Curiosity, Integrity, and Leadership.

He puts it this way: “I build what’s next, modernize what’s not, and grow teams along the way.”

### B

🐾 Matt leads with empathy, clarity, and purpose.

He describes teams he inherited as “over-processed, under-trusted, waiting for permission instead of owning outcomes.” His focus is on removing those barriers: making intent explicit, creating psychological safety so people speak honestly, and pushing decisions to the people closest to the work. He coaches teams to think clearly, not just execute tasks.

At the Accenture Cloud Innovation Center, that included a “permit to fail” learning environment. Teams had avoided risks because they feared blame. Matt organized non-punitive post-mortems, emphasized lessons rather than pointing fingers, and supported lean, low-stakes experiments. In innovation engagements, he set expectations upfront that prototypes and user tests would likely fail. Teams used those results to challenge assumptions and shape product direction week by week, before committing to costly builds.

He also brought engineers, product managers, and UX architects into balanced teams with equal participation and shared accountability. Rather than relying on handoffs between separate functions, he embedded pair programming, collaborative coding, stand-ups, retrospectives, and knowledge-sharing sessions. Teams connected their goals to client and business outcomes and could voice feedback and change their workflows autonomously.

His working principles include integrity and respect, continuous learning, and “outcomes, not optics.” Engineering practices remain part of that philosophy: automation, continuous delivery, and quality-first practices. At the Cloud Innovation Center, teams achieved 4x delivery acceleration after embracing lean product and modern engineering practices. The balanced-team work also achieved zero-defect production code through iterative development and real-time feedback loops.
