## Turn 2. Visitor: Give me an example where that didn’t work.

### A

🐾 Here's a great example.

At the **Accenture Cloud Innovation Center**, Matt faced a situation where his leadership style didn't initially succeed. The team had a history of avoiding risks due to fear of blame, which led to stagnation, reduced innovation, and slower delivery cycles. This reluctance to experiment caused missed opportunities in adopting modern engineering practices and improving team agility.

To address this, Matt promoted a "permit to fail" culture. He organized non-punitive post-mortems to analyze mistakes and identify lessons learned, encouraging transparent sharing of errors. He emphasized the value of lessons learned from mistakes in team meetings and communications, and supported lean, low-stakes experiments to test hypotheses. This fostered an environment where teams felt comfortable exploring new approaches through structured hypotheses and controlled iterations.

The result was a significant shift in team dynamics. With a safety net for failures, team members began experimenting with new technologies and approaches, generating more innovative ideas. Post-mortem learnings were applied to subsequent sprints, preventing the recurrence of identified failure patterns and improving overall reliability. Lean experiments and a fail-fast mindset enabled quicker pivots and shorter project timelines, ultimately leading to a more agile and innovative team environment.

He also embedded reusable patterns into modernization toolchains and appointed a cross-functional review board to maintain and evolve the library.

Teams subsequently adopted pre-approved patterns, accelerated project initiation, and improved architectural alignment. They spent less effort repeating solved work and more time on unique challenges.
