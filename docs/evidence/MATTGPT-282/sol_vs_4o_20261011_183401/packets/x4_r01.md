## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt calculate ROI?

### A

🐾 Looking across Matt's portfolio, I see clear patterns.

In his role at the **Accenture** Cloud Innovation Center, Matt focused on demonstrating clear ROI by aligning modern engineering practices with client needs. He established metrics that showcased the transformation impact, such as achieving **4X faster velocity** and **zero-defect deployments** through test-driven development and automated pipelines. These metrics not only demonstrated the efficiency and quality of the work but also justified the investment in the Cloud Innovation Centers, contributing to over **\$100M** in repeatable business by fiscal year 2023.

At **JP Morgan**, Matt's turnaround of a CRM program that had a negative estimate at completion resulted in a reversal of the relationship, with the client requesting him by name for follow-on work. Similarly, at **Fiserv**, he recovered an **\$8.5M** program to deliver **3%** under budget, avoiding **\$500K** in penalties and securing a **\$3M** extension. These outcomes were achieved by managing engagement margins, pushing scope creep into change requests, and optimizing resource deployment, which were key components of his ROI calculation.

By focusing on delivery excellence and client satisfaction, Matt was able to build a strong reputation that led to significant repeat business, emphasizing the value of his approach in calculating and demonstrating ROI.

### B

🐾 Here's how Matt put the numbers behind the investment case.

Matt used cost-to-fix versus benefit comparisons, delivery measures, and financial forecasts. His stories do not document a single standardized ROI formula or a calculated ROI percentage; they report the costs, benefits, and commercial results he tracked.

At **RBC**, leadership needed to decide whether Bottomline WebSeries could support its corporate banking platform before committing further investment. Matt conducted code reviews, stakeholder interviews, and an architecture assessment, then delivered target-state models with a “transparent cost-to-fix vs. benefit comparison.” Architectural heat maps exposed gaps between the bank’s requirements and the platform’s capabilities. The assessment found that WebSeries lacked the required modularity, preventing investment in that platform path and allowing resources to move to higher-priority initiatives. No dollar amount for the avoided investment is recorded.

At **Accenture’s** Cloud Innovation Centers, the investment question included whether pair programming’s costs were offset by velocity gains and quality improvements. Matt used delivery comparisons: **4X** faster velocity, **10-person** balanced teams delivering equivalent output to traditional **20-person** teams, and **zero-defect deployments** through test-driven development and automated pipelines. He also owned the centers’ P&L, budget, and cost management. The centers contributed to **\$100M+** in repeatable business through delivery excellence and client relationships—not a reported ROI percentage.

For engagement economics, Matt tracked forecasts, actuals, variance, contingency, and staffing costs. At **Fiserv**, recovery of an **\$8.5M** program produced **3% under budget**, or **\$255K** in preserved margin, plus **\$500K** in avoided penalties and a **\$3M** contract extension. Those were separate financial outcomes, not a combined ROI calculation.
