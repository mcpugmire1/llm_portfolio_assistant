## Turn 1. Visitor: Compare his Fiserv and JP Morgan ACCESS work.

### A

🐾 Here’s how the two engagements compare.

Both involved Execution & Delivery, but the starting problems differed: **Fiserv** was a delivery recovery; **JP Morgan Chase** ACCESS was a global payments build and consolidation.

At **Fiserv**, the **\$8.5M** white-label card portal had missed consecutive deadlines, and stakeholders were threatening contract cancellation. The **45-person** team and **3 external vendors** had uncoordinated dependencies while payment processing was moving from a mainframe toward SOA-based APIs. Matt traveled to Omaha Monday–Thursday, ran daily **7 AM** and **11 PM** onshore/offshore transition calls, reset the backlog and timelines, and coordinated payment-processing, fraud-detection, and hosting vendors. He also covered the program manager’s responsibilities during paternity leave.

He replaced SVN with Git, required senior-developer code reviews and unit tests, and introduced Maven, Artifactory, Hudson, and SonarQube. Lead time fell to **one day to one week**. With no accessibility expertise on the team, he took on the ADA/AODA SME role, led semantic-markup refactoring, and trained developers to maintain compliance across client brands. The program delivered **3% under budget**, met **47/47 acceptance criteria**, and launched with **zero critical defects**. A recovered release avoided **\$500K in penalties**.

At **JP Morgan Chase**, regional systems and Bottomline’s out-of-box WebSeries capabilities could not cover the required payment workflows. Matt managed a **40+** blended team across the US, Mumbai, and Bangalore, defined the onshore/offshore engagement model, and spent **70–80%** of his time embedded with Bottomline in Portsmouth. He coordinated directly with bank stakeholders and the vendor on what to configure versus custom-build.

His work covered ACH, wire, FX/cross-border, tax payments, MAC security, payment-specific entitlements, SSO, and reporting. He also delivered gateway connectivity across **12 countries**, coordinated legacy-data verification with compliance teams, and directed production incident response and daily leadership calls. Migration completed without disruption to live payments; compliance dashboards became operational tooling. ACCESS launched in **April 2011**, consolidating regional systems into a portal serving **135,**000+ client**s** across **180+ countries**.
