## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt calculate ROI?

### A

🐾 Here's a great example.

At **Accenture**, Matt's approach to calculating ROI was deeply embedded in the operational and financial management of the Cloud Innovation Centers (CIC) he built in **Atlanta** and **Tampa**. He owned full P&L accountability, which included managing both the budget and the revenue side. The ROI was demonstrated through measurable client results and consistent transformation outcomes. For instance, the CIC achieved a **4X faster velocity** compared to traditional approaches, with **10-person balanced teams** delivering the equivalent output of traditional **20-person teams**. This was coupled with zero-defect deployments through test-driven development and automated pipelines.

Matt's focus was on delivering exceptional outcomes that generated organic referrals, contributing to over **\$100M** in repeatable business. The consistent delivery of these results justified premium pricing based on proven ROI, with clients specifically requesting CIC methodology and practitioners for new initiatives. This approach not only demonstrated clear ROI but also built credibility for the practice, fueling the sales pipeline through referenceable case studies.

### B

🐾 Here’s how Matt put numbers behind the investment case.

Matt used cost-to-fix versus benefit comparisons, delivery economics, and measured results. The accounts describe those inputs and outcomes, but don’t specify a single ROI formula, payback period, or percentage return.

At **RBC**, leadership needed to decide whether Bottomline WebSeries could support the Day**2Day** Corporate Banking platform before committing further investment. Matt assessed Data, Application, Technology, and Business Architecture, conducted code reviews and stakeholder interviews, and created architectural heat maps. He worked with the enterprise architecture team on target-system models with a transparent cost-to-fix versus benefit comparison, then held workshops with leadership to connect business goals with the technical findings. The assessment found that WebSeries lacked the required modularity; **RBC** redirected resources rather than continuing down that platform path. No dollar value is given for that avoided investment.

At **Accenture’s** Cloud Innovation Centers, Matt addressed concerns about pair programming costs with measured velocity gains and quality improvements: **4X** faster velocity, **10-person** balanced teams delivering equivalent output to traditional **20-person** teams, and zero-defect deployments through TDD and automated pipelines. He also owned the centers’ budgets, cost base, chargeability, and contractor spending. In operating-model engagements, restructuring into lean, self-regulating balanced teams reduced costs **15–35%**.

For engagement economics, Matt managed forecasts, actuals, variance, contingency, staffing costs, and scope changes. At **Fiserv**, recovery of an **\$8.5M** program finished **3%** under budget—**\$255K** in preserved margin—with **\$500K** in contractual penalties avoided and a **\$3M** extension secured. Those were distinct financial outcomes, not a stated ROI percentage.
