# Extra x4 (ROI), run 10

## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt calculate ROI?

### A

🐾 Here's a great example.

In his work at the **Accenture** Cloud Innovation Center (CIC), Matt demonstrated a clear approach to calculating ROI by focusing on measurable client results. The CIC was tasked with bringing Silicon Valley innovation and product culture to Fortune **500 clients**, which required a shift from traditional consulting models to modern product engineering practices. Matt built the Atlanta and Tampa Cloud Innovation Centers from the ground up, establishing them as premier capabilities for digital transformation.

To prove the business case and justify investment, Matt ensured that the centers delivered consistent transformation outcomes across Fortune **500 clients**. These outcomes included achieving **4X faster velocity** compared to traditional approaches, with 10-person balanced teams delivering the equivalent output of traditional 20-person teams, and zero-defect deployments through test-driven development and automated pipelines. The centers contributed to **\$100M+** in repeatable business by FY23, establishing a predictable standard of success that justified premium pricing based on proven ROI.

By focusing on delivering measurable results such as faster velocity and zero defects, Matt was able to demonstrate a clear return on investment for the clients and for **Accenture**. This approach not only built credibility for the practice but also fueled the sales pipeline through referenceable case studies, proving that modern product engineering could become a sustainable practice at an enterprise consulting scale.

### B

🐾 Here’s what Matt measured.

Matt used cost-to-fix versus benefit comparisons, delivery performance, and engagement economics. His stories don’t specify a single ROI formula, a payback period, or a calculated ROI percentage. Execution & Delivery and Strategic & Advisory provide the concrete examples.

At **RBC**, leadership needed to decide whether Bottomline WebSeries could support the Day**2Day** Corporate Banking platform before committing further investment. Matt reviewed code, interviewed stakeholders, assessed architecture, and created heat maps of the gaps. He worked with the enterprise architecture team on target-system models with a “transparent cost-to-fix vs. benefit comparison,” then held workshops with leadership to connect business goals with the technical findings. The assessment found that WebSeries lacked the required modularity. **RBC** redirected resources rather than investing further in that platform path; the story does not give a dollar value for the avoided investment.

At **Accenture’s** Cloud Innovation Centers, Matt addressed concerns about pair programming costs by comparing velocity and quality improvements against the investment. Reported results included **4X faster velocity**, **10-person** balanced teams delivering equivalent output to traditional **20-person** teams, and zero-defect deployments. He presented those results through executive briefings and client workshops. Operating-model transformation engagements separately reported **15–35%** cost reductions.

For engagement economics, Matt managed forecasts, actuals, contingency, staffing costs, and scope changes. At **Fiserv**, the **\$8.5M** program recovery finished **3%** under budget, preserving **\$255K** in margin and avoiding **\$500K** in contractual penalties. A **\$3M** extension was a separate commercial outcome—not a stated ROI calculation.
