### C

🐾 Yes—the first Cloud Innovation Center engagement with **Norfolk Southern** went wrong despite the balanced-team model and emphasis on test-driven development.

In early **2020**, Matt’s team began modernizing the client’s legacy Revenue and Shipment Planning platform. A couple of months in, as the pandemic forced work remote, the client reported rising defects. The dashboards showed near-complete test coverage. The feedback caught the team off guard, and the credibility hit landed in front of both the client and **Accenture** account leadership, who were already accustomed to waterfall delivery.

Matt’s investigation found that an engineer had been solutioning from his own interpretation rather than the backlog story, then writing tests after the code. Coverage looked complete, but defects went undetected. The backlog was also part of the problem: vague stories lacked defined scenarios and a definition of done.

Matt took ownership of the failure rather than defending the metrics. He ran a bottom-up audit of the codebase and test suite and showed the gaps openly. He rebuilt the backlog with SMART, testable stories and brought in frontend and backend TDD experts from Columbus to work side by side with the team.

Instead of just restating the process, he used Samman-style coaching: boot camps, coding katas, practice learning sessions, and pairing engineers with subject-matter experts through an “I do, we do, you do” progression. The team redid the code and test cases and restructured agile ceremonies.

Defect rates dropped, and the client stopped finding defects the dashboard could not see. The story documents an initial failure followed by a course correction—not an engagement that remained unsuccessful.
