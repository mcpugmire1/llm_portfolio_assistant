## Turn 1. Visitor: How did Matt scale engineering teams from 4 to 150+ people?

### A

🐾 Here’s how Matt built the teams and the organization around them.

Matt’s scope grew from small delivery teams—including a **3–5-person** interface team at **AT&T**—to **150+ practitioners** at **Accenture’s** Atlanta and Tampa Cloud Innovation Centers. The CIC itself grew from a **10-person** pilot, and its population included engineering, architecture, product, and design—not engineers alone.

Growing client demand required recruiting people who often lacked hands-on cloud-native or Agile experience. Meanwhile, the deliberately flat organization had no managers sitting on daily teams. Matt needed hiring, development, evaluation, and promotion processes without adding middle-management layers.

He built a hiring engine across **4** channels: internal bench search, campus recruiting, contract-to-hire, and governed job requisitions. Engineers completed hands-on TDD pairing auditions; product managers and designers worked through live scoping exercises. Interviewers recorded independent votes, and a failed pairing audition ended the candidacy. Matt held the closing interview and made every final hiring decision. During scaling pushes, **15–20 auditions per week** produced **4–6 net-new practitioners per week**, with interview participation capped at **2 slots per practitioner per week** to protect delivery.

New joiners learned through tailored enablement, round-robin pairing with seasoned practitioners, and “I do, we do, you do.” Senior–junior coaching, peer-led tech talks, and live client projects carried learning into daily work. Balanced teams combined product, engineering, and design, using pair programming, TDD, and CI/CD.

The organization ran through **11–13 direct-report practice and client leads**. Team anchors kept leads informed without another management layer; Monday allocation reviews assigned practitioners across client work and internal R&D. Because managers did not observe daily work, Matt built evaluations around evidence from recent pair partners, product managers, and anchors, then ran feedback capture, promotion calibration, and compensation allocation.

Over about **4 years**, the CIC reached **150+ practitioners**, with near-zero attrition in the first **2 years**. Its **10-person** balanced teams delivered equivalent output to traditional **20-person** teams, with **4X faster velocity** and zero-defect deployments.
