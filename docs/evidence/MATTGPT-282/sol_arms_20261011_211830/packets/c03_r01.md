## Turn 2. Visitor: Give me an example where that didn’t work.

### A

🐾 At **Norfolk Southern**, the balanced-team setup and emphasis on test-driven development did not prevent a quality crisis.

Early in **2020**, Matt launched the Cloud Innovation Center’s first major engagement to modernize the client’s Revenue and Shipment Planning platform. A couple of months in, as the pandemic shifted work to remote, the client reported rising defects—even though dashboards showed near-complete test coverage. The credibility hit landed in front of both the client and **Accenture** account leadership.

An engineer had been building from his own interpretation rather than the backlog story, then writing tests after the code. Coverage looked complete, but defects went undetected. The backlog also contained vague stories without defined scenarios or a definition of done. Matt’s initial emphasis on TDD had not established the actual test-first practice.

Matt took ownership in front of the client and account leadership. Instead of defending the dashboard, he ran a bottom-up audit of the codebase and test suite and showed the gaps openly—including the backlog problems. He rebuilt the backlog with SMART, testable stories and brought frontend and backend TDD experts from Columbus to work side by side with the team, redoing code and tests. Boot camps, coding katas, practice learning sessions, and “I do, we do, you do” pairing followed.

Defect rates dropped, and the client stopped finding defects the dashboard could not see. The story does not quantify that reduction. Client confidence was restored through the open audit and visible improvement—not through the initial process setup alone.

Cross-functional teams and shared ownership were in place, but this story records fragmented work rather than consistent reuse across teams.

Matt responded by adding a centralized resource and review structure. He built a repository in Git and Confluence with reusable patterns for transitioning monoliths to microservices, managing dependencies, and implementing CI/CD pipelines. He organized weekly workshops where teams presented solutions and contributed to the library, embedded patterns directly into pipelines and modernization toolchains, and appointed a cross-functional review board to maintain and evolve the library.

The reported results were faster project initiation, greater architectural consistency, and less redundant effort. The story does not quantify the time saved or cost reduction, and it does not document a failed project or an unresolved leadership failure.

So the specific problem was consistency during scaling: teams were solving already-solved problems separately. Matt changed the operating model by introducing shared patterns, regular knowledge-sharing, and a review board rather than leaving each team to work independently.
