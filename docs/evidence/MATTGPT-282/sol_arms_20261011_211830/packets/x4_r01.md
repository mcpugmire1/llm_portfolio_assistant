### A

🐾 Here's what Matt measured.

Matt used delivery results and operating economics to make the investment case. His stories do not specify a formal ROI formula, a complete cost-and-benefit worksheet, or a calculated ROI percentage. They do describe the measures he used to justify investment in **Accenture’s** Cloud Innovation Centers and track engagement finances.

For the Atlanta and Tampa centers, clients questioned the cost of pair programming. Matt addressed that concern by comparing velocity, team size, and quality against traditional delivery: **4X** faster velocity, **10-person** balanced teams delivering equivalent output to traditional **20-person** teams, and zero-defect deployments through test-driven development and automated pipelines. He used those velocity gains and quality improvements to explain how the investment was offset—not just the number of engineers assigned.

He required internal bench deployment before approving external contractors. Across individual engagements, he tracked staffing costs, forecast variance, actuals, contingency release, and scope changes. He planned for loaded-rate increases of roughly **20%** or more when consultants and managers were promoted mid-engagement.

At **Fiserv**, the financial results were recorded separately: an **\$8.5M** program finished **3%** under budget, preserving **\$255K** in margin; the recovered release avoided **\$500K** in contractual penalties; and delivery secured a **\$3M** extension. Those are distinct financial outcomes, not a single ROI percentage.

For the centers, the reported business result was **\$100M+** in repeatable business by **FY23**, across **30+** product engagements and **15+** Fortune **500** clients. That figure measures repeatable business—not net profit or a calculated investment return.
